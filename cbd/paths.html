Mixed paths

Seven ways to hold the house, not three. Every path is a present value in the transfer year, on the cash basis used by the owner-brief options page, and discounted at the 5.0% rate used there.

Reading the table: paths A and B hold the house, so a market fall does not change them. Paths C to G put money into equities, so the stress columns move them. Path F sells after 5 years of living in the house. Path G sells after 5 years of letting it out. The 5-year sale date is an assumption.

How each row is built

  • A and B are the cbe owner-brief values, unchanged: cbe/options.html. Their property inputs are owner records with no official source (flag F01).
  • C, D, E sell at transfer. The proceeds are the cbe figure after the 6.0% selling cost. D and E invest them in equal monthly tranches over 24 or 36 months, parked in 3-month bills at the after-tax rate until bought (Phase-in test on phased.html).
  • F lives in the house for 5 years, collecting the cbe occupancy cash flows. It then sells. The gain above the $250,000 exclusion is taxed at 37.1% (20% federal, 3.8% net investment income tax, 13.3% California). The remaining proceeds are phased in over 36 months.
  • G lets the house for 5 years, collecting the cbe rent cash flows. On sale the depreciation taken is recaptured at 42.1% (25% federal, 3.8%, 13.3%). The rest of the gain is taxed at 37.1%. The remaining proceeds are phased in over 24 months.
  • Crash scenarios: a 57% fall over 17 months from entry (2008) and an 89% fall over 34 months (1929), then growth at the net return. Both are stylised paths built from the official peak-to-trough figures; they are not the actual daily record.

Known gaps, all flagged: California tax on Treasury interest is not modelled. California conformity with the federal home-sale exclusion is not modelled, which is conservative for path F. The depreciation period of 27.5 years is an assumption carried from the cbe desk.
