Property A: sell, let or live in it, and how to phase in the money

Summary for the owner, the attorney and the tax adviser. Figures in present value at the 2045 midpoint of the 2040 to 2050 transfer window, rounded to the nearest $10.

Headline

Under the base assumptions the sale path that scores highest is C: $9,256,470 in present value at the transfer year (2045 midpoint), against $2,834,850 for keeping the house and living in it. That ranking is the base case only. It changes if a deep fall in equities comes soon after the sale, which is the risk the phase-in is meant to cover.

Cash basis, as in the owner-brief options page. Living in the house is shown at its cash value only, so it looks worse than it is. The imputed rent is shown on the options page.

Reinvestment risk, beside the headline

The sale proceeds are assumed to sit in 3-month Treasury bills at 4.32% (Treasury, 13 November 2026), which is 2.56% after federal tax at 37% and the 3.8% surcharge. Equity is assumed at 6.40% net. The gap between those two rates is the cost of waiting.

What would change the answer

  1. A market fall in the first 2 to 3 years after the sale. A 2008-style fall at entry makes the 36-month phase-in (E) the best sale path, by $3,370,790 over the lump sum. A 1929-style fall makes letting the house (B) the best of all seven paths, because B has no equity exposure.
  2. The price of the phase-in in a rising market. In the base case the 36-month phase-in gives up $682,130 of present value against the lump sum (C). That is the premium paid for protection.
  3. Portfolio return. Living in the house beats selling only if the net portfolio return is below 5.20% a year; letting it beats selling only below 5.15% (cbe break-evens, econ basis).
  4. Rent for Property A. Letting beats selling only at a gross yield of at least 5.99% (cbe, let versus sell; living in it, 4.68%). No rent figure for Property A has been supplied or verified.
  5. Price growth. Living in the house beats selling only if house prices grow at least 5.58% a year; letting it, at least 5.73% (cbe break-evens).
  6. Filing status for the home-sale exclusion. A joint return doubles the exclusion from $250,000 to $500,000, which changes path F (live 5 years, then sell).
  7. Whether the transfer is into the trust as the statute describes. The exclusion depends on the trust and the principal-residence tests. This desk has not read the trust instrument.
