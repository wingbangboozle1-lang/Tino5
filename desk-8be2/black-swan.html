Real economies are never consistent

Growth and inflation vary year to year. A 50-year projection that assumes one smooth rate can mislead. This section replays uneven history and tests the two crash entries most likely to hurt a seller who reinvests: 1929 and 2008.

Phased-investing path (the protection)

After inheriting and selling Property A, park the after-tax proceeds in 3-month to 2-year U.S. Treasuries for 24–36 months, then phase into stocks (e.g., equal monthly tranches over the following 12–24 months).

Trade-off. If markets rise during the 24–36 month parking window, you miss some gains — that is the cost of the cushion. The phased-investing path is a risk-reduction tool, not a return guarantee. Crashes of 1929/2008 magnitude are not in the most recent 49-year record; this desk flags that gap rather than pretending it is covered.

Keeping the house through a crash

If you keep and rent Property A instead, a crash does not mark your portfolio to market — but rent may fall, vacancies may rise, and a reassessed property tax bill stays due. Neither choice removes risk; they move it. Test both on the what-if page.
