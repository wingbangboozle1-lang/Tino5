What would change the answer?

This desk answers one question: when the inherited home passes through the trust (planned 2040–2050), should the heir move in, rent it out, or sell and reinvest — and what could flip that answer?

The short answer today: selling and reinvesting is the baseline best plan, but only if three things hold. If any one breaks, the ranking can change:

  • Home prices keep rising faster than costs. If the inherited home's long-run price growth falls below roughly 4–5% a year after costs, keeping it to rent looks better than selling.
  • Stocks do not crash right when proceeds are reinvested. Selling into a 1929- or 2008-style entry and buying stocks at the top is the main way the sell option loses. The black-swan section shows how parking proceeds in Treasuries first softens it.
  • You can move in within a year. The Proposition 19 parent-child exclusion (keeps the low assessment) requires the heir to occupy the home as a primary residence within one year of transfer; otherwise the property reassesses to market value.
