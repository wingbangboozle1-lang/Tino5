CHOICES / AFTER-TAX ANALYSIS

Decision framework

There is no defensible single “best” choice until date-of-death value, actual taxable basis, occupancy eligibility, accurate carrying costs, leases, and the then-current tax rules are known. Use this like-for-like comparison to maximize after-tax net value and minimize avoidable cost.

Conclusion from verifiable evidence: no option can be ranked on profit today. A sale-versus-hold recommendation based on gross property value or gross rent alone would ignore substantial unverified inputs. Keep all options open; before transfer, preserve records and identify the successor trustee. At transfer, obtain an appraisal and assessor guidance before moving, leasing, or selling.

DECISION GATES

Resolve in this order

Gate 1 · Is the transferor’s property included in a taxable estate?

Inventory all estate assets, ownership interests, prior taxable gifts, debts/deductions, and applicable spouse portability. A 2026 threshold cannot determine a 2040–2050 tax bill. IRS Form 706 instructions ↗

Gate 2 · Does either home qualify for Prop 19 treatment?

Confirm relationship, transferor’s primary-residence status, transferee’s move-in date, claim filing date, transfer-date market value, factored base-year value, and whether the county considers the property eligible. Test each property independently. BOE requirements ↗

Gate 3 · What is the real full cost to occupy or operate?

Collect county tax bills, utility statements, insurance quotes for the intended occupancy and number of units, inspection reports, maintenance history, leases, and actual paid rental ledger. Unknown amounts are listed on Flagged for Review ↗.

Gate 4 · Compare the after-tax alternatives with common dates and assumptions

Use at least low/base/high scenarios for appreciation, rent, insurance, repairs, and investment returns. Enter each assumption, source and sensitivity result in a private working paper—not in this public site.

Practical initial stance: preserve the option to occupy or rent until both the Prop 19 deadline and sale tax consequences are understood; do not postpone legally required notices. If Prop 19 qualification is important, check with the county before a move-out or rental change. This is a process recommendation, not a conclusion that any property should be held.
