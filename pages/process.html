How trust inheritance works

General California workflow when a trust holds real property. The trust instrument, title, family relationships, property type, and current law control. Use this timeline as a checklist for the successor trustee and qualified advisers—not a substitute for reviewing the trust or obtaining legal advice.

Time-sensitive: some deadlines run from death, not the later date the trust distributes a property. Where the law says “within” a time period, calendar it immediately and confirm the applicable rules/forms with the county and counsel.
AT DEATH / FIRST 30 DAYS
  1. Secure the trust and verify title.

    Locate the executed trust and amendments, deeds, title/vesting, insurance, mortgage, leases, tax bills, utility accounts, and asset schedule. Confirm which person is successor trustee and whether the property was actually titled into the trust. If property was omitted, court procedures may be needed. Santa Clara Superior Court — trust duties ↗

  2. Accept trusteeship and obtain certified death documentation.

    A successor trustee may sign a written acceptance and certification of trust to demonstrate authority to financial institutions and other third parties. Follow the trust’s successor-selection provisions. Court trust guidance ↗

  3. Protect the properties and document condition.

    Notify existing insurer and lender as required by policy/contract; confirm coverage for occupancy, tenant units, vacancy, and trustee administration. Record photos, inspect safety issues, and maintain utilities. Request written policy-specific instructions from the insurer. California DOI insurance guidance ↗

WITHIN STATUTORY WINDOWS
  1. Notify beneficiaries and heirs where the trust becomes irrevocable.

    The California Superior Court information states a 60-day notice period after the trustee takes office or settlor’s death, whichever is later. Notice has required trust, trustee, and administration details; counsel should review the notice and mailing list. Santa Clara Superior Court ↗

  2. Notify every county assessor of each California real-property death transfer.

    File the Change in Ownership Statement—Death of Real Property Owner (BOE-502-D) within 150 days after death for trust-held real property; if probate applies, filing is due with the inventory and appraisal. Use the assessor in each county where property is located. California BOE notice ↗

  3. Get date-of-death appraisals and asset inventory.

    Record fair market value for real estate and other assets; document debts, ownership interests, basis, improvements, depreciation, adjusted taxable gifts, and any marital/community-property interests. Ask a qualified appraiser/CPA whether an alternate valuation election is appropriate. IRS basis guidance ↗ · Court inventory guidance ↗

  4. Evaluate federal Form 706 and portability.

    Determine if the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts exceeds the federal filing threshold in effect for the year of death. If spouse portability is desired, a Form 706 election may be appropriate even if no estate tax is owed. The general due date is 9 months from death; Form 4768 can request an automatic 6-month filing extension. IRS Form 706 instructions ↗

  5. Apply promptly for the Prop 19 family-home exclusion if potentially eligible.

    For the current law framework, a qualifying transferee must make the home their principal residence within 1 year; file the homeowners’ or disabled-veterans’ exemption within 1 year to receive the exclusion as of transfer. The county’s Prop 19 claim should also be filed within applicable timing; the exact allowance and process at a future transfer may differ. California BOE Prop 19 ↗ · SF Assessor family-transfer guide ↗

ADMINISTRATION / DISTRIBUTION
  1. Account for trust income and file required returns.

    Trust/estate income after death is different from the inherited principal. Rental income, interest, and dividends can be taxable; the trustee may need a new EIN, fiduciary income-tax return, and beneficiary Schedule K-1s. Track expenses and preserve original invoices. IRS Publication 559 ↗ · IRS Publication 527 ↗

  2. Obtain county assessment and tax-bill confirmation.

    Death is generally the property-tax change-in-ownership date, including trust property; exclusion is not automatic. Ask each assessor for the change-in-ownership determination, assessed value, supplemental bill timing, exemptions, and Prop 19 result. Santa Clara County Assessor ↗ · San Francisco Assessor ↗

  3. Decide use only after checking title, trust powers, leases, tax basis, assessment, and insurance.

    Compare owner-occupancy, rental operation, and sale with an accountant, real-estate attorney, property manager, and insurers. If renting or selling, retain receipts and distinguish repairs from improvements and personal from rental use. IRS sale-of-home rules ↗ · IRS rental rules ↗

  4. Distribute or continue trust management as the instrument directs.

    Pay valid debts/expenses, keep trustee accounting, reserve for taxes/liabilities, and distribute only as authorized by trust terms. A trust can continue after death and may require ongoing investment and reporting. Santa Clara Superior Court — successor trustee duties ↗
