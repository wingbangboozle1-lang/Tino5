Executive summary

Two properties, one trust, and a decision that is really two decisions: what to do with the Cupertino house (Property A) when it passes, and how to structure the transfer so that the tax outcome is not left to the county assessor. Everything below is derived from public official sources; the working numbers are model output and every assumption is named.

Recommendation

Sell Property A within the year of death, unless you actually want to live in Cupertino.

On the base case a sale produces — of net wealth by 2065, against — for holding and renting and — for moving in. The sale works because it converts a 4%-a-year asset into a diversified portfolio assumed at 6%, and because the step-up in basis means the sale is essentially tax-free on the day it happens.

Two conditions attach to that recommendation. First, the trust must include Property A in the parents' estate — otherwise the step-up disappears and the sale becomes a large capital-gains event (flag F-14). Second, the gap is not large: if Cupertino appreciates faster than — a year, holding overtakes selling. Treat the recommendation as a conditional, and revisit it at the transfer date with a real appraisal.

What to do, in order

Now → 2039

  • Confirm Property A carries the homeowners' exemption — that is the proof it is the parents' principal residence and therefore Prop 19 eligible.
  • Read the trust and confirm three things in writing: the house is titled to the trust, the trust is revocable, and the assets are includible in the estate (step-up).
  • Get a replacement-cost estimate and a home inspection so insurance and maintenance stop being guesses (F-07, F-08).
  • Find the solar interconnection date and the remaining NEM term.
  • Decide as a family who would live there; the decision is on a 12-month clock after death.

The first 12 months after death

  • Serve the trustee's notification, get the EIN, order the date-of-death appraisal.
  • Record the trustee's deed — that starts the assessor's review.
  • File BOE-266 by month 12 and BOE-19-P within 3 years. File both even if the plan is to sell.
  • Keep the tax installments current: 10 December and 10 April, 10% penalty if missed.
  • If selling, sell in the year of death. If holding as a rental, accept the full reassessment and re-underwrite the rent.

What could change this answer

The model is deterministic. The three inputs that matter are growth in Property A's value (A1), the return on the reinvested proceeds (A15), and whether you want to live in Cupertino at all — which is a preference, not a calculation. Everything else, including insurance and utilities, moves the totals by thousands, not millions.
