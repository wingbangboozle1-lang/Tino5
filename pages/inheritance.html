TRUST INHERITANCE · PRACTICAL CHECKLIST

What happens after a death—and which clocks matter?

A high-level checklist, not a substitute for the trust, California probate counsel, or tax advice. Exact steps depend on trust wording, title, debts, beneficiaries and the person authorized to act.

!
Start the Prop 19 and tax clocks immediately.

For a qualifying family-home exclusion, the transferee must establish the home as their principal residence and apply for a homeowners’ or disabled-veterans’ exemption within one year. County guidance says death is generally the transfer date for inherited property. Separately, a federal Form 706 filing / payment deadline is generally nine months after death if required; a filing extension does not automatically extend payment.

STEP BY STEP

Estate administration and property handoff

01

Identify the acting fiduciary and governing documents

Locate the executed trust and amendments, pour-over will, deeds, account/beneficiary records, and any prior gift/estate tax returns. Confirm who is successor trustee and whether court administration is required. Do not assume a trust avoids all court filings.

California Courts: probate overview ↗
Immediately
02

Secure property, records, insurance, tenants and bills

Keep required coverage active; notify insurers of ownership/occupancy changes; secure vacant areas; review leases, deposits, utilities, mortgage/lien and tax bills; document condition and income. Contact qualified property counsel before changing occupancy or lease arrangements.

California DOI: residential insurance coverage ↗
First days / weeks
03

Get the date-of-death valuation and full inventory

Obtain qualified appraisals for real estate and statements for financial assets, debts and business interests. Preserve the final appraisal and tax-basis records. Federal inherited-property basis is generally date-of-death fair market value, subject to exceptions and estate reporting consistency rules. A later sale can produce capital gain; California taxes capital gains as ordinary income.

IRS Publication 551: inherited property basis ↗
As soon as practicable
04

Check federal estate-tax filing, gift history and portability

For 2026 deaths, the Form 706 filing threshold is $15,000,000 of gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts and the specific exemption; the IRS threshold for the actual death year controls. A return may also be filed to elect portability for a surviving spouse even when not otherwise required. Generally due nine months after death; request extension on time if needed.

IRS estate-tax FAQs: threshold and portability ↗
Within 9 months if required
05

Notify assessor / recorder; file claims and report ownership change

Ask the assessor what forms, notices and evidence are required for the trust transfer; file applicable change-in-ownership reports and Prop 19 claim documents. San Francisco instructs owners to file a Death of Real Property Owner form within 150 days of death; confirm the corresponding current Santa Clara County filing requirements directly. For a qualifying intergenerational family-home claim, the homeowner’s / disabled-veterans’ exemption is due within one year; the separate BOE-19-P claim is generally due within three years and before a transfer to a third party. A late claim may only produce prospective relief. A county determination is essential. Do not assume trust ownership, a low old assessment, or an ancestor’s exemption carries over automatically.

SF Assessor: change-in-ownership / death form ↗ · SF Prop 19 claim ↗ · Santa Clara Assessor: parent-child transfer ↗

1-year exemption / move-in; separate 3-year exclusion claim
06

Determine reassessment and the post-transfer tax bill

Proposition 19 can exclude reassessment only for a qualifying transfer of a family home that was the transferor’s principal residence and becomes the eligible transferee’s principal residence within one year, subject to the current value limit and timely filing. For multiple units, treatment may differ by unit/use. Ask the county for a written determination; establish a reserve until the supplemental bill is known.

California BOE Prop 19 fact sheet ↗

One-year move-in / filing requirement
07

Settle liabilities, distribute or retitle, then choose a property strategy

Pay valid administration expenses and liabilities, keep trust accounting and tax records, complete required deeds/recording, and confirm lender/title/insurance requirements. Before selling or renting, have a tax professional calculate basis, depreciation, taxable gain, rental income, and any residence-sale exclusion. Compare the three paths using verified net cash flow.
