Category 04 · Trust inheritance checklist

What the inheritance process could look like

A living trust may avoid a court-supervised probate transfer for assets properly held in the trust, but it does not skip tax, assessor, creditor, title, tenancy, or fiduciary work. The actual trust instrument controls.

Not legal advice or a substitute for the trust. These are planning steps, not a complete statutory checklist. Trustee duties, notifications and transfer documents depend on the trust, asset title, family facts and current law. Consult a California trust/estate attorney and CPA at the time.

Sequence

At the event, proceed in this order

Deadlines shown are current-law references only.

  1. Confirm authority and trust terms

    Locate the executed trust and amendments, death certificate, deeds, beneficiary designations and successor-trustee acceptance. Confirm whether each property is actually titled to the trust and which terms govern distribution. California Courts explain that a living trust names a successor trustee and assets placed in it can pass outside probate. California Courts: living trusts

  2. Secure and inventory the estate

    Secure vacant property, notify insurers and lenders, preserve records, identify assets and debts, and arrange lawful management of existing tenancies. Do not cancel coverage, change locks, or remove tenants without checking policy and law. Keep a private, dated asset/liability inventory. California Courts: probate and trust terms

  3. Get independent date-of-death values

    Order defensible date-of-death appraisals for real estate and an inventory of other assets; retain title, cost, improvement, depreciation, gift and trust records. These values support tax basis, property-tax rules and estate valuation. IRS guidance generally sets inherited basis at fair market value on the date of death, subject to exceptions. IRS Publication 551

  4. Contact each county assessor promptly

    For California property-tax purposes, inheritance through a trust can be a change in ownership on the date of death. Ask the county to identify reporting forms, confirm the new assessed value/supplemental bill, and determine whether an exclusion is available. Never wait for formal trust distribution to start the inquiry. California BOE Prop 19 Fact Sheet

  5. Make any eligible family-home election on time

    For a qualifying parent-child family-home exclusion, the heir generally must occupy the property as their principal residence within one year and file a homeowners’ or disabled-veterans’ exemption within one year for relief from the transfer date. The BOE-19-P exclusion claim is generally due within three years or before transfer to a third party, whichever is earlier. Verify the current rules and forms with the correct county assessor. BOE requirements and forms

  6. Have the CPA test estate and income-tax filings

    Calculate the federal gross estate under applicable rules, account for adjusted taxable gifts, and review whether Form 706, trust/estate income-tax returns and beneficiary statements are required. For a 2026 death, the federal basic exclusion and Form 706 filing threshold are $15,000,000; Form 706 is generally due nine months after death. The year-of-death instructions control. IRS estate-tax FAQs · IRS Form 706 instructions

  7. Reconcile recurring costs and rental obligations

    Collect tax statements, insurance declarations/quotes, utility bills and solar settlement statements, repair/inspection records, leases, rent ledgers, permits and any local registration/fee notices. Keep Property B tenancy facts private and obtain local landlord-tenant advice before changing use or occupancy. SF Rent Board law guide · California DOI insurance comparison tool

  8. Compare occupancy, rental and sale choices

    Use the same holding period and include after-tax net rent, full ownership costs, sale costs and gains tax, tax/eligibility effects, risk, liquidity, and the value of housing. Keep a reserve for taxes and necessary repairs. Only then decide whether to retain, rent, or sell. Do not assume investment returns. SEC performance-claims guidance

  9. Distribute and close the administration properly

    Follow the trust’s distribution instructions only after legal, tax, debt, title, and property matters are addressed. Record required deeds, retain statements and appraisals, issue appropriate tax documents and preserve a final accounting for beneficiaries. Ask counsel which steps apply to this trust. California Courts: estate-planning documents
