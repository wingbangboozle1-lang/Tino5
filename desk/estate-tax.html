Inheritance tax

Three different taxes that share a name, and only one of them actually applies here.

"Inheritance tax" names three different things, and conflating them is the most expensive mistake available in this analysis.

1. A tax on receiving an inheritance

California has none. The state's estate tax was repealed effective 1 January 2005, and no inheritance tax has replaced it. A beneficiary receives inherited property free of any California transfer tax.

State Controller, Estate tax

2. A tax on the estate

Federal, and the threshold is high. The filing threshold is $15,000,000exact for a death in 2026 and $15,480,000exact for 2027. Above the exclusion the rate is 40%. A surviving spouse can inherit the first spouse's unused exclusion, but only if a timely return is filed to elect it.

IRS, Estate tax · Rev. Proc. 2026-35 · Form 706 instructions

3. Reassessment at the county

This is the one that actually bites. It is not a tax on inheriting; it is the loss of the deceased's low assessed value. A child who moves into a parent's home can keep the old base-year value plus the value cap of $1,044,586exact, and pays tax on any excess of market value over that sum. A child who does not move in pays tax on full market value.

RTC s.63.2 · BOE LTA 2026/026

The estate is far below the exclusion in every year tested, and the margin widens, because the exclusion is indexed while the houses are assumed to grow only slightly faster than inflation. On these assumptions no federal estate tax is due at all. That is the correct answer for most families, and it is worth stating plainly so that the real risk, which is the reassessment, is not obscured by a tax that does not apply.

Where this projection breaks. Two ways. First, if the "considerable amount of money" also inherited is a substantial portfolio rather than two houses, the gross estate is much larger than modelled here, because this projection carries real property only. Second, if the exclusion is legislated downward, the whole conclusion changes. It doubled in 2026 and is indexed, but it has moved before and can move again.

The income-tax side of inheriting

Inherited property is not income. It takes a basis equal to fair market value at the date of death, so a sale at that value realises no gain. Only appreciation after the death is taxed, and only when realised. That is the single most valuable feature of the whole transaction, and it means the worst thing to do with an inherited house is to sell it decades later without having documented the basis and the improvements.

What the two houses mean for the estate

The estate is not just the inherited house. It is everything the heir owns, including the already-owned house. That is why the projected gross estate above starts at the combined value of both. It is also why a decision about Property A is really a decision about the whole balance sheet.
