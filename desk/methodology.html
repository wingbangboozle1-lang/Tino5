Three accounting decisions that matter

  1. The sell path carries none of the house's costs. Selling means the house is gone, so its property tax, insurance, utilities, and maintenance are all zero. Charging them while also crediting the invested proceeds double-counts.
  2. The move-in path credits the unit vacated at the other house. Moving into the inherited house frees the unit the heir currently occupies, so it becomes rentable. It is priced at the per-unit rent the owner reports, which is an owner figure rather than a market rent.
  3. Property tax is split in two. The ad valorem part tracks the assessed value at the county's published composite rate. Fixed charges, which are bonds and parcel taxes, do not track value and are grown at inflation instead. Treating a blended effective rate as if it were all ad valorem would compound the fixed charges every year.

What the model does not do

Not modelledWhy it matters
Bracket-by-bracket taxOne combined marginal rate is applied every year. A real computation moves through brackets and could differ materially in the transfer year.
State and federal interaction on the saleThe state treatment of capital gains is a flat add-on to the federal rate.
Refinancing or leverageNeither house carries a mortgage in the model. A mortgage would change the return on equity substantially.
ProbateThe model assumes the trust works. The process page prices the alternative.
The other house's own appreciation as a decision variableIt is held in every path, so it cancels out of the comparison rather than being valued.
Sequence of returns in the sell pathThe reinvestment return is a constant. A real portfolio has a path, and the path matters.
Mid-year conventions in depreciationDepreciation is taken for whole recovery years only. A mid-month convention would shift the first and last year by a fraction of a year, which is immaterial against the other assumptions.

Reproducing the numbers

python3 scripts/model_v5.py         # writes desk/data/model-v5.json
python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts -p 'test_model_v5.py'
python3 scripts/build_desk_v5.py    # writes desk/*.html
python3 scripts/verify_desk_v5.py   # structural, sourcing, privacy checks

The model is deterministic. Running it twice produces byte-identical output, and the test suite pins every arithmetic relationship that a published figure depends on.

Rounding policy

Money is rounded to the nearest $10 at the point of output, after all arithmetic. Statutory amounts, rates, and thresholds are not rounded, because they are fixed by law and rounding them would misstate them. Rounded figures are labelled as rounded wherever they appear.

Privacy policy

Nothing on this site identifies a person, an address, a parcel, or a ZIP code. The two properties are Property A and Property B. A county or a city is named only where a rate or a procedure is local, because that is the level at which the source is published. No figure here is precise enough to be matched to a specific bill, and the build's checker fails if one becomes so.

This desk is a research aid. It is not legal, tax, investment, or appraisal advice. Every figure is either cited to an official source, or labelled as model output, or left blank and listed on Flagged for review. Amounts are rounded to the nearest $10 so that no figure can be matched back to a specific bill.
