Projections 2026-2076

Value, assessed value, and every carrying cost, year by year, with the arithmetic shown.

Two different things are projected here, and they should not be confused. The first is the cost of holding the house, year by year. The second is the value of the house and of the alternative paths at the end of the horizon.

Rounding. Every amount on this page is rounded to the nearest $10. Rates, caps, and thresholds are quoted exactly. A fifty-year projection is a scenario, not a prediction: the range on Black swans is the honest measure of how little a single path means.

The pattern is stark. Selling wins in eight of the nine combinations. Holding wins only when home values compound at 5% and the reinvestment rate is at the low end at the same time. That is the honest summary of the central case: the sell path is robust across a wide range of assumptions, and only a sustained high-growth, low-rate world reverses it.

The 3.5% growth assumption sits between the two realised series: below the 4.28% the national index has delivered over thirty-five years, and above the 2.53% the price level has delivered over twenty-five. That is deliberate. A house is a real asset and should beat inflation, but land in a mature market should not be expected to repeat a run that included a historic credit boom.
