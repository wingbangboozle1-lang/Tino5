Overview

A decision desk for a future inheritance, built to be audited line by line.

One question drives everything here. When a house held in a family trust passes to the heir at some point between 2040 and 2050, should the heir move into it, rent it out, or sell it and reinvest? The heir already owns a second house outright. That second house is analysed separately throughout, because it is not part of the inheritance.

Two houses, two very different situations. The inherited house is assessed at a small fraction of its market value. That makes its tax bill look trivial and makes how it is transferred enormously valuable, because the low assessment can be kept or lost depending on what the heir does in the first year. The already-owned house has a tax charge that is roughly double what the city's published rate implies, and that discrepancy is the clearest administrative item in the whole desk.

How to read the numbers

Every amount is rounded to the nearest $10. Statutory amounts, rates, and thresholds are quoted exactly and marked as such, because rounding a legal threshold would misstate it. Where a figure could not be confirmed against an official source it is left blank and flagged rather than estimated. Nothing on this site identifies a person, a property, or a location more precisely than a county or a city.

This desk is a research aid. It is not legal, tax, investment, or appraisal advice. Every figure is either cited to an official source, or labelled as model output, or left blank and listed on Flagged for review. Amounts are rounded to the nearest $10 so that no figure can be matched back to a specific bill.
