Law watch

The rules the plan depends on, and what a change to each would cost.

The plan spans thirty to fifty years. Every rule it relies on can be changed in that time, by legislation, by initiative, or by a court. This page lists the rules the plan depends on, their current state, and what a change would cost.

Ranked by how much damage a change would do

  1. A repeal or narrowing of the family-home exclusion. This is the whole basis of the hold path's tax advantage. Without it, inheriting the house means inheriting a market-value assessment, and holding loses its structural edge. The exclusion is recent, it was passed by initiative, and it can be changed by initiative.
  2. A cap on stepped-up basis. Proposals to limit the basis step-up at death have been made repeatedly. If it were curtailed, a long hold would end with a very large taxable gain.
  3. A reduction in the federal exclusion. Not a live risk for this estate at the moment, given the size of the margin, but it is the item most likely to be raised in national debate, and it would apply to the whole balance sheet, not only the inherited house.
  4. Rent-control expansion. The state cap sunsets in 2030, but local ordinances are stricter and more durable. If the house were rented, the rent would be constrained for as long as the tenancy lasted.
  5. Insurance-market change. Not a law change in the ordinary sense, but the regulatory environment determines whether cover is available at all, and at what price.
