Black-swan regimes and phased entry

How the ranking changes under a 1929-style or 2008-style crash at transfer, and why phased entry is path-dependent.

Why this page matters. A black-swan crash right after transfer changes the ranking. The model tests two historical-style paths: a 1929-style fall (stocks down 89%, about 34 months to trough, about 22 years to regain the peak) and a 2008-style fall (stocks down 57%, about 17 months to trough).

What is not established: the 1929 housing path is a proxy (2008 housing, flagged [F07]). The phased-entry results depend on the crash timing and cannot be used as a forecast.

Phased entry: what the model shows

In the 1929-style path the phased entry outcome is far above the sell-at-once outcome. This follows from entering near the trough of a path the model already knows. It is shown to illustrate sensitivity, not as a plan. Do not treat it as evidence that waiting pays.

Pending review: the 1929 phased results exceed the base-regime sell-at-once result, and the 2008 phased result is also high. Both are under review ([F07]).
