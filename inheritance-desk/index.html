Executive summary

Inheritance decision for Property A, with rental and owner-occupancy options for Property B. Source-linked; privacy-first.

What would change the answer (read first)

  • The rent you avoid. If the heir would otherwise pay rent, living in the house beats renting it out only while the avoided rent is worth more than about 3.54% of value a year (base case, 2040 transfer; the model assumes 4.00%). If the heir would not pay rent elsewhere (cash-only, A0), renting it out beats living there once the gross rent yield exceeds about 0.46%.
  • Stock returns. The sell-and-invest result is driven by the assumed stock return (6% base, 3%–4% in weaker regimes). A lower return favours keeping the house.
  • The economic regime. In the stagflation regime (5% inflation, 4% stocks), living in the house ranks first: $2,311,910 against $1,563,640 for selling and investing at once. In the base regime selling ranks first. See Scenarios.
  • A crash at transfer. A 1929-style or 2008-style fall right after transfer changes the ranking. Phased entry only helps if it happens to enter after a fall, which nobody can know in advance (see black-swan page).
  • The Prop 19 cap wording. Official pages describe the cap two ways (see F03). The result changes with the wording used.
  • Property tax rate. Your tax-rate-area composite rate is not yet confirmed (F01).
