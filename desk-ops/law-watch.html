Law watch: enacted, proposed, and not confirmed

The owner asked for the three to be kept apart. Only the first is used as an input; published guidance is used where the statute leaves an operational gap; proposals are listed so they are not mistaken for rules, and are never in the model.

The rule this page follows. A proposal is displayed with its status and is never an input to the model. If a proposal were enacted, the input would change and a rebuild would show whether the recommendation moved — which is exactly what the build is for.

Enacted law

RuleWhat it saysEffect on the decisionSource
Federal basic exclusion enacted$15,000,000 for a 2026 death and $15,480,000 for 2027, indexed from 2026 and made permanent by Public Law 119-21.Sets whether the transfer tax is zero. Carried forward after 2027 without assuming indexation.official
Form 706 timing enactedDue nine months after death; an automatic six-month extension is available on Form 4768, including for a portability-only filing.Fixes the first administrative deadline in the timeline.official
Intergenerational exclusion enactedA parent-child transfer of a principal residence is excluded from change in ownership if the heir occupies within one year and files the exemption; the exclusion is removed when the heir stops occupying.The largest property-tax lever in the decision, and reversible.official
The intergenerational value cap enactedFactored base year value plus $1,044,586 for transfers from 16 February 2025 to 15 February 2027; the $1,000,000 is adjusted every second 16 February.Applied as carried forward after 2027 rather than extrapolated.official
The 1% tax above one million enacted1% of California taxable income above $1,000,000, in addition to all other taxes, with credits and the joint-return rule switched off.Applied to the sale year; it is not a rate on the sale price.official
Documentary transfer tax enactedA county may levy 55 cents per $500; a city inside it may add up to half again.Priced into every sale as separate county and city lines.official
Section 121 home-sale exclusion enactedUp to $250,000 of gain excluded, $500,000 on a joint return, after two of the last five years of ownership and use.The second relief, available only to the live-then-sell path.official
Net investment income tax enacted3.8% on net investment income above $200,000 single or $250,000 joint, not indexed.Applied to the taxable part of a sale gain.official
Prop 13 assessment limit enacted1% of assessed value, rising by the lesser of 2% or inflation, reset at a change in ownership.Quoted from the county's own compilation, which restates it.official

Published official guidance

RuleWhat it saysEffect on the decisionSource
Claim timing and the late-filing window guidanceThe claim must be filed within three years of the transfer or before a transfer to a third party, whichever is earlier, or within six months of a supplemental or escape assessment notice.Turns the claim deadline into an operational date; also makes selling before filing fatal.official
Continual-use requirement guidanceThe exclusion is maintained only while the property is continually used as the family home, and is removed if the heir becomes ineligible for the exemption.Quantified as the lost-exclusion exposure on the taxes page.official
Secured tax due dates and penalties guidanceFirst instalment due 1 November, delinquent 10 December; second due 1 February, delinquent 10 April; 10% penalty then 1.5% a month from 1 July.The only certain deadline before the transfer.official

Scope. A decision aid for one household question: when the inherited house arrives, should the heir live in it, let it out, or sell it and invest? Every figure is a model output from a round modelling base — not either property's value — rounded to $10. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published and marked. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice.

Privacy. No names, streets, parcel numbers, postcodes, cities or neighbourhoods appear on this site, and no figure from the household's own records is published at any precision. The conclusions are published as break-evens, rankings and rates, which move only slightly across a tenfold range of property values — the small movement is caused by a fixed statutory cap and is tested and stated on the method page.
