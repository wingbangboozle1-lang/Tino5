Three views of the same model: the value and assessment of the inherited house, the carrying cost of holding it, and what each path is worth at the horizon. Everything is nominal dollars on a round modelling base, rounded to $10.

The inherited house: value, assessment and tax

The assessment is the load-bearing line. Under the intergenerational exclusion the assessed value is the factored base year value plus whatever the market value exceeds the cap by, and it then grows by the lesser of 2% or inflation each year. If the heir does not occupy, the assessment resets to full market value at the transfer and the yearly tax roughly doubles.

The projection is not the argument. These numbers move by millions when one input moves by one percentage point — the break-even is 10.49% against a baseline assumption of 4.0%, a gap of 648.5% of a percentage point. Any projection presented without that gap invites a decision it cannot support.

Scope. A decision aid for one household question: when the inherited house arrives, should the heir live in it, let it out, or sell it and invest? Every figure is a model output from a round modelling base — not either property's value — rounded to $10. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published and marked. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice.

Privacy. No names, streets, parcel numbers, postcodes, cities or neighbourhoods appear on this site, and no figure from the household's own records is published at any precision. The conclusions are published as break-evens, rankings and rates, which move only slightly across a tenfold range of property values — the small movement is caused by a fixed statutory cap and is tested and stated on the method page.
