Questions, information and answers

20 questions stand between the household and a decision that can be defended. Each carries what is known now, what is missing, who can supply it, and — the column that matters — which decision it blocks. The owner asked for suggestions on how to discover missing information; that is the fourth column.

How to run the discovery, in order

  1. Buy the two measurements first. An appraisal and three rental comparables for the inherited house are the only purchases on this list that change the model's inputs rather than its caveats. Everything else can wait for them.
  2. Then ask the county. One call to the assessor establishes the tax rate area, the factored base year value on the current roll, and the exact claim forms the county wants. Ask for the answer in writing.
  3. Then the advisers, with the briefing sheet. The one-page sheet exists so that the attorney and the CPA answer the same questions rather than two different ones.
  4. Then the trust. Whether the trust or the heir is the transferor, and what the trust says about occupancy, decides whether any of the above is even available.
  5. Only then the market assumption. House-price growth and equity returns are the two inputs nobody can measure in advance. They come last because they are the ones the household must choose, and the choice should be written down with a date.
The honest summary. With what is known today, the decision cannot be made defensibly. It can be framed — the break-evens, the exposures and the deadlines are all established — but the four measurements that would turn the frame into a decision do not exist yet. That is not a failure of the analysis; it is the finding.

Scope. A decision aid for one household question: when the inherited house arrives, should the heir live in it, let it out, or sell it and invest? Every figure is a model output from a round modelling base — not either property's value — rounded to $10. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published and marked. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice.

Privacy. No names, streets, parcel numbers, postcodes, cities or neighbourhoods appear on this site, and no figure from the household's own records is published at any precision. The conclusions are published as break-evens, rankings and rates, which move only slightly across a tenfold range of property values — the small movement is caused by a fixed statutory cap and is tested and stated on the method page.
