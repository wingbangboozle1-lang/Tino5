Uneven economy

Reading the spread

The sale path has the widest spread of any path — $35,721,720 — because it puts the entire estate into the market on one day. The living path has the narrowest among the hold options, because the house is a slower-moving asset than equities. A household that cannot tolerate the first number should not choose the path that produces it merely because its mean is highest.

Why no fifty-year path is drawn through these states. A fifty-year projection of a crashing economy is not a forecast, it is theatre: the errors compound faster than the signal. The states here are run at the same horizon as every other comparison, so their results can be put beside the baseline rather than on a separate page where they look like prophecy.

Scope. A decision aid for one household question: when the inherited house arrives, should the heir live in it, let it out, or sell it and invest? Every figure is a model output from a round modelling base — not either property's value — rounded to $10. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published and marked. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice.

Privacy. No names, streets, parcel numbers, postcodes, cities or neighbourhoods appear on this site, and no figure from the household's own records is published at any precision. The conclusions are published as break-evens, rankings and rates, which move only slightly across a tenfold range of property values — the small movement is caused by a fixed statutory cap and is tested and stated on the method page.
