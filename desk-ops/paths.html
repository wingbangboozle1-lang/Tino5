All eight paths, described before they are scored

The owner asked for mixed paths, not only the three pure choices. Three pure choices, five mixed ones. Each is stated as a rule the heir could actually execute, because a path that cannot be written down as a rule cannot be chosen.

The three pure choices

Live in it

The heir occupies the house. It cannot be let. Their own housing is the return.

Cash at 2045 / 20 years — low $7,059,870, middle $4,565,360, high $1,977,410. With housing: $14,083,050.

Let it out

Kept as a rental. No principal-residence exclusion is available on a later sale.

Cash at 2045 / 20 years — low $13,391,230, middle $11,420,340, high $9,241,240. With housing: $11,420,340.

Sell at the transfer

Sold in the year of inheritance. The stepped-up basis means little or no gain.

Cash at 2045 / 20 years — low $19,883,470, middle $19,139,330, high $18,287,920. With housing: $18,047,600.

Five mixed paths

Live in it, then sell

Occupied for the first years (securing the exclusion and the 121 gain exclusion), then sold. This is the only holding path that gets both reliefs.

Cash at 2045 / 20 years — low $12,148,280, middle $10,393,560, high $8,536,320. With housing: $15,355,780.

Let it, then sell

Let from the transfer, sold later. Rent now, exclusion lost.

Cash at 2045 / 20 years — low $15,505,910, middle $14,072,680, high $12,472,160. With housing: $13,634,660.

Sell, park in Treasuries, phase in

Sold at the transfer; proceeds parked in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries for 24-36 months, then moved into equities in equal monthly tranches.

Cash at 2045 / 20 years — low $19,372,900, middle $18,637,300, high $17,794,470. With housing: $17,545,560.

Live, then sell, parked, phased in

Occupied first, sold, then the 24-36 month Treasury park and phased entry.

Cash at 2045 / 20 years — low $12,148,280, middle $10,393,560, high $8,536,320. With housing: $15,355,780.

Let, then sell, parked, phased in

Let first, sold, then the Treasury park and phased entry.

Cash at 2045 / 20 years — low $15,499,270, middle $14,069,810, high $12,472,160. With housing: $13,631,800.

assumption Each state is a declared scenario, not a forecast; the two crash states are anchored on the published drawdowns. The listing is mechanical: for each state, the path with the highest cash result.

The trap in that table. The winner changes with the state, so choosing the path that wins the baseline is choosing a forecast, whether or not it is labelled one. The reason to prefer a mixed path is not that it wins anywhere; it is that it loses least across states. That is a different question, asked on Uneven economy.

Scope. A decision aid for one household question: when the inherited house arrives, should the heir live in it, let it out, or sell it and invest? Every figure is a model output from a round modelling base — not either property's value — rounded to $10. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published and marked. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice.

Privacy. No names, streets, parcel numbers, postcodes, cities or neighbourhoods appear on this site, and no figure from the household's own records is published at any precision. The conclusions are published as break-evens, rankings and rates, which move only slightly across a tenfold range of property values — the small movement is caused by a fixed statutory cap and is tested and stated on the method page.
