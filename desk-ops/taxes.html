Taxes and assessment, separated

The owner asked for three things to be kept apart: the estate tax, the income-tax basis, and the property-tax assessment. They are decided by three different authorities on three different dates, and confusing them is the most expensive error available here.

A married couple can shelter up to twice that, but only if the first estate elects portability on a timely filed return — and a return must be filed to elect it even where no tax is due. IRS estate-tax FAQ The form is due nine months after death, with an automatic six-month extension on Form 4768.

California. The state imposes no estate or inheritance tax. That is widely reported but was not confirmed from an official state page in this session, so it is carried as an open item (F10) rather than asserted. It does not change any number here: the model taxes the estate against the federal exclusion only, so if a state tax existed the result would be worse than shown, never better.

2. Property tax: the assessment is where the money is

This is the largest single line in the decision and the one the earlier desks got wrong. Under the intergenerational exclusion the assessed value is the transferor's factored base year value plus the amount by which market value exceeds that base plus an indexed cap. The cap is $1,044,586 for transfers from 16 February 2025 through 15 February 2027. BOE LTA 2025/009

The finding that changes the decision. Occupying the house is worth $14,620 a year at the top of the local rate range — 20% of the tax bill. It is not a rounding error and it is not permanent: RTC 63.2(a)(1)(B)–(C) removes the exclusion the moment the heir stops occupying and re-enrols the base year value at the following lien date. Any path that assumes the reduction lasts after the heir moves out is wrong.
The cap is carried forward, not invented. The statute adjusts the $1,000,000 amount every second 16 February for the federal house-price index. The last published amount is $1,044,586 for transfers through 15 February 2027. No later amount is published at the date of this page, so every projection after 2027 uses this figure and marks it carried forward. Inventing a 2045 cap would have been the easier choice and the wrong one.

3. Income tax: the basis, the sale, and the 1% above one million

The owner asked specifically for the Form 540 instructions on the 1% tax above one million and on capital-gain treatment. What can be said from the statute alone is this: the tax is 1% of California taxable income above $1,000,000, imposed in addition to every other tax, with credits, filing-status re-computation and the joint-return rule expressly switched off. RTC 17043 It is not a rate on a sale price, and it is not a surtax on the gain: it applies to the margin above one million of taxable income in the year of sale.

A sale in the year of inheritance produces no federal gain on these assumptions, because the basis steps up to the date-of-death value and the selling cost pushes the result below it. That is why the owner's instruction to model a sale in the same tax year as the inheritance before deciding anything else is the right instruction: it establishes the floor every holding path has to beat, and the floor is nearly all of the price.

  • Step-up in basis. Property acquired from a decedent takes a basis equal to its value at the date of death. IRS Topic 409 directs the reader to Publication 551 for inherited property; that publication was not opened in this session, so the rule is carried as an assumption the CPA should confirm (F: Pub 551).
  • Section 121. Up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 on a joint return) is excluded on the sale of a main home after two of the last five years of ownership and use. IRS Topic 701 This is the second relief that only the live-then-sell path can use.
  • Net investment income tax. 3.8% on net investment income above modified AGI of $200,000 single or $250,000 joint, not indexed. IRS Gain excluded under section 121 is not net investment income.
  • Long-term rates. 0%, 15% and 20% by taxable income; unrecaptured section 1250 gain at a maximum 25%. IRS Topic 409
  • Transfer tax. The county may levy 55 cents per $500 of value (11.00%). RTC 11911 A city inside that county may add up to half that amount. The city rate actually applied to the building is an assumed range because the city's published schedule could not be opened (F05).

For a sale of this size the difference is material and one careful hour with the 2025 Form 540 instructions closes it (F09).

Scope. A decision aid for one household question: when the inherited house arrives, should the heir live in it, let it out, or sell it and invest? Every figure is a model output from a round modelling base — not either property's value — rounded to $10. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published and marked. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice.

Privacy. No names, streets, parcel numbers, postcodes, cities or neighbourhoods appear on this site, and no figure from the household's own records is published at any precision. The conclusions are published as break-evens, rankings and rates, which move only slightly across a tenfold range of property values — the small movement is caused by a fixed statutory cap and is tested and stated on the method page.
