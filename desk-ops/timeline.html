The deadlines, as a timeline

Nine dates bind this transfer. Two of them are lost by doing nothing, one is lost by moving out, and one is lost by selling before filing. Each is quoted with the rule that creates it.

The order of operations after a death

  1. Days 1–30. Secure the property, notify the insurer, and order an independent appraisal as of the date of death. The appraisal fixes both the estate value and the new basis; it cannot be obtained later with the same authority.
  2. Days 30–90. The trustee confirms title and the trust's terms, and the change in ownership is reported to the assessor. The assessor will learn of the transfer whether or not the heir volunteers it.
  3. Before the first anniversary. The heir moves in and files for the homeowners' exemption. This is the single date that decides whether the exclusion is available at all.
  4. Before the third anniversary, and before any sale. The intergenerational claim is filed. Selling first closes the window.
  5. Nine months after death. Form 706 is filed if the estate is above the threshold, or to elect portability. The extension is six months and must be requested.
  6. Every year. The assessed value and the two policy questions are re-read. Each is carried forward on the model and marked.
What is not on this timeline because nobody knows it yet. The date of death, and therefore every date above, is unknown. unverified The timeline is stated relative to the death for that reason. It should be turned into a dated calendar the month the death occurs.

Scope. A decision aid for one household question: when the inherited house arrives, should the heir live in it, let it out, or sell it and invest? Every figure is a model output from a round modelling base — not either property's value — rounded to $10. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published and marked. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice.

Privacy. No names, streets, parcel numbers, postcodes, cities or neighbourhoods appear on this site, and no figure from the household's own records is published at any precision. The conclusions are published as break-evens, rankings and rates, which move only slightly across a tenfold range of property values — the small movement is caused by a fixed statutory cap and is tested and stated on the method page.
