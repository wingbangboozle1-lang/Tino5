Attorney & CPA briefing sheet

One page. Print it. It asks each adviser only for what that adviser can answer, and lists the dates that bind. No figure from the household's own records appears on it.

To the attorney

  1. Does the trust instrument make the heir the transferee at death, or does the trust continue to hold the property? Which entity is the transferor for the purposes of the intergenerational exclusion? (RTC 63.2 treats a transfer by death as a transfer, "whether within or outside a trust" — LTA 2026/026.)
  2. What does the trust say about occupancy, sale and distribution, and does it constrain the choice between living, letting and selling?
  3. Is an independent appraisal at the date of death required by the instrument, and who is empowered to commission it?
  4. If the estate may exceed the federal exclusion, what is the liquidity plan, and does the instrument authorise a borrowing or a partial distribution to fund the tax?
  5. Should the estate elect portability, and what does that require before the nine-month date?
  6. Is a like-kind exchange contemplated for any property, and if so what must happen before a contract is signed?

To the CPA

  1. Confirm the basis of the inherited property against Publication 551 and the Form 706 as filed.
  2. Does section 121 apply to a later sale by the heir, on what facts, and for how much?
  3. What is the California treatment of the gain, and how does the 1% tax above $1,000,000 of taxable income (RTC 17043) apply in the year of sale?
  4. Is the net investment income tax payable on the gain, and is any part of it excluded under section 121?
  5. What depreciation is available if the house is let, over what period, and what is the recapture on a later sale?
  6. Has the estate made prior taxable gifts that reduce the available exclusion?
  7. What is the estimated tax position in the year of sale, and are estimated payments required?

The four figures to bring to the meeting

  1. The date-of-death appraisal.
  2. The county's current roll entry and the tax rate area for the parcel.
  3. The trust's distribution and occupancy terms.
  4. The household's written answer to one question: what would make you choose each of living, letting and selling?
What this sheet deliberately omits. No names, no addresses, no parcel number, no account balance and no projected figure from the household's records. The advisers will need those in person; the sheet exists to be carried, not to be lost.

Print styling is included in the page: use your browser's print command and the navigation, footer and background are dropped.

Scope. A decision aid for one household question: when the inherited house arrives, should the heir live in it, let it out, or sell it and invest? Every figure is a model output from a round modelling base — not either property's value — rounded to $10. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published and marked. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice.

Privacy. No names, streets, parcel numbers, postcodes, cities or neighbourhoods appear on this site, and no figure from the household's own records is published at any precision. The conclusions are published as break-evens, rankings and rates, which move only slightly across a tenfold range of property values — the small movement is caused by a fixed statutory cap and is tested and stated on the method page.
