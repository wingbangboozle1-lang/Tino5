Inheritance & tax

Who pays what: estate, inheritance and capital gains

Three different taxes are routinely confused: a tax on the estate, a tax on the heir, and a tax on a later sale. Only the first can be triggered by inheriting, and only if the estate is large.

Bottom line

A $15,000,000 federal exclusion applies per person in 2026, and it is not a per-heir allowance (IRS · Estate tax). California imposes no estate or inheritance tax (CA State Controller · Estate tax). An inheritance is not income (FTB · Gifts and inheritance). The realistic risks are (a) the transferor's total estate exceeding the exclusion, (b) losing the step-up because of how the trust is drafted, and (c) capital-gains tax on a later sale.

Real estates are rarely taxed at a flat 40%: deductions, marital transfers, charitable gifts, valuation discounts and the graduated schedule all matter, and state-level rules differ. The 40% figure is the commonly cited top marginal rate and is used here as an assumption; the rate schedule is in the Form 706 instructions (IRS · Instructions for Form 706). What matters for planning is the shape: below the threshold, no federal estate tax at all.

Why the step-up is worth more than the estate tax here

If A is included in the transferor's estate, its basis becomes the date-of-death value. Selling at or near that value then produces little or no gain. If instead the basis carried over at, say, a hypothetical $250,000, a sale at $6,531,230 in 2045 would produce a gain of about $6,281,230 and, at the assumed 37.1% combined rate, $2,330,340 of tax — an amount larger than any plausible property-tax difference between the three options. Confirm inclusion in the estate before relying on it (IRS · Inherited property basis · IRS · Publication 551).

What "considerable money" changes

  • If the whole estate is below the exclusion — including A, B if owned by the same person, life insurance, retirement accounts and prior taxable gifts — there is no federal estate tax and no California estate tax.
  • If it is above, the excess is taxed at the estate level. Cash set aside for that tax is not available to the heirs, which is why liquidity planning belongs in the trust review.
  • Gifts before death reduce the estate but also use exclusion and can affect the basis of what is given. That is a specialist question, not something this site can settle.
  • The 2040–2050 threshold is unknowable. The current law is permanent only in the sense that it has no sunset; a future Congress can change it.
