Inheritance, estate and property tax

Current inheritance and property-tax rules checked against official sources
RuleCurrent statementScope / caveatOfficial source
Federal estate-tax exclusionFor 2026, the federal basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000 per decedent.Estate-level calculation; not a separate per-heir inheritance allowance. Estate composition, deductions, prior taxable gifts and elections matter. Future amounts must be rechecked.IRS · Estate tax · IRS · Estate and gift tax updates
Federal estate tax rateThe top federal estate-tax rate is 40% on the taxable estate above the exclusion.Applies only if the taxable estate exceeds the exclusion; most simple estates do not require a Form 706 filing.IRS · Estate tax · IRS · Form 706 instructions
No federal inheritance taxThere is no federal tax on the person who receives an inheritance.The estate tax (if any) is paid by the estate before distribution; the heir's receipt is not income.IRS · Estate tax
California estate taxCalifornia requires no estate-tax return for decedents dying on or after 1 January 2005.This does not eliminate federal estate tax or income tax on later income/gain.California State Controller · Estate tax
California inheritance receiptCalifornia FTB: a gift or inheritance is not included in the recipient's income; income it later produces is taxable.Rental income, interest, dividends and taxable gain are distinct from receipt of the inheritance.California FTB · Gifts and inheritance
Inherited income-tax basisGenerally, basis is fair market value on the date of death; alternate valuation and consistent-basis rules can apply.A sale near basis is not automatically tax-free: valuation, trust/estate inclusion, later appreciation and expenses matter.IRS · Basis of inherited property · IRS · Form 706 instructions
California parent-child property-tax exclusionCurrent Prop 19 rules require an eligible family transfer and principal-residence requirements; value above the adjusted limit can increase taxable value.Claim timing and eligibility are critical; no exclusion for other real property under the current rule. Verify the applicable-year adjusted allowance.California BOE · Proposition 19 · California BOE · Proposition 19 fact sheet
Prop 19 value allowanceThe family-transfer value limit is current taxable value plus the inflation-adjusted $1,000,000 allowance.Applies to the family home/farm when transferor and transferee each use it as their principal residence and claims are timely.California BOE · Proposition 19 · California BOE · Proposition 19 fact sheet
Home-sale exclusion (§121)Up to $250,000 ($500,000 married filing jointly) of gain on the sale of a principal residence may be excluded, subject to ownership and use tests.Exclusion can generally be used once every two years; does not apply to rental or second-home portions.IRS · Publication 523, selling your home
Capital gains on inherited propertyGain is measured against the stepped-up (date-of-death) basis; only post-transfer appreciation is taxable.Later depreciation and selling expenses affect the calculation; keep records.IRS · Basis of inherited property · IRS · Publication 551, basis of assets
Property-tax assessmentCalifornia assessed value is generally subject to a 2% annual inflation cap; the base rate is generally 1% plus voter-approved bonded debt, with reassessment on change of ownership.The actual bill also depends on local tax-rate area and direct assessments; no property-specific amount is published here.California BOE · How property is assessed · California BOE · Property tax overview
