Q-03Estate & inheritance taxWill the estate owe federal estate tax?For 2026 the federal basic exclusion is $15,000,000 per decedent; it applies to the gross estate, not one home.The total estate size (including the considerable money and Property B) is not quantified, so taxability cannot be determined.Compile the gross estate and review Form 706 / portability with a tax professional.IRS · Estate tax · IRS · Form 706 instructions
Q-04Estate & inheritance taxWill California or local inheritance/estate tax apply?California has no estate tax for deaths on or after 2005-01-01, and an inheritance is not California income.None identified at state level; local rules depend on the (unnamed) municipality.Confirm with the California State Controller and FTB; check local ordinances.California State Controller · Estate tax · California FTB · Gifts and inheritance
Q-05Estate & inheritance taxWhat is the tax basis of Property A after inheritance?Inherited basis is generally the date-of-death fair market value (step-up), subject to exceptions; later gain is taxable.The date-of-death value is not yet established.Obtain a date-of-death appraisal and retain basis records.IRS · Basis of inherited property · IRS · Publication 551, basis
Q-06Property tax (Prop 13 / 19)Will Property A keep its low assessed value after transfer?Proposition 19 can preserve a limited reassessment for a family transfer where both parties used the home as a principal residence, subject to a value limit and deadlines.Eligibility (principal residence for both parties, occupancy timing, value limit) is unconfirmed; without it the property reassesses to full market value.Review BOE Prop 19 guidance and file the required claims within the one-year / three-year windows.California BOE · Proposition 19 · California BOE · Proposition 19 fact sheet
Q-07Property tax (Prop 13 / 19)What will the annual property tax be after transfer?California base ad valorem tax is 1% of assessed value plus bonded debt and direct charges; annual assessment increases are capped at 2% absent reassessment.The post-transfer assessed value, tax-rate area, and direct charges are not known.Request the assessed value and tax bill from the county assessor / tax collector.California BOE · Property tax overview · California BOE · How property is assessed
Q-08Operating costsWhat will insurance cost for Property A?Only an insurer can quote specific coverage after underwriting; California publishes statewide average premiums but not property-specific quotes.The premium, deductible, coverage, and insurability for Property A are unknown.Obtain binding homeowner-insurance quotes (including FAIR Plan context) before publishing any cost cell.California Department of Insurance · Home insurance · California Department of Insurance · Sustainable Insurance Strategy
Q-09Operating costsWhat will water, electricity, solar credit, and repair costs be?Solar generation can produce a credit; actual bills depend on the utility account, tariff, and property condition, none of which was reviewed.Monthly water, net electricity, solar credit, and maintenance/repair budgets are unknown.Review utility account history and tariff; obtain an inspection and contractor estimates.California Public Utilities Commission · Water · California Public Utilities Commission · Net energy metering
Q-10Rental economicsCan Property A lawfully be rented, and what rent could it earn?Rent law, vacancy, management, and income-tax treatment depend on the legal unit status and local ordinances, none of which was verified.Lawful unit status, market rent, vacancy, and management cost are unknown.Confirm permits/legal units and review comparable market rent evidence.California Legislative Information · Codes
Q-11Sell optionWhat would Property A sell for net of costs after transfer?Inherited basis is generally date-of-death fair market value; gain on a later sale is taxable; selling costs reduce net proceeds.The sale price, selling costs, and post-transfer gain are unknown.Obtain a market appraisal and estimate selling costs after transfer.IRS · Basis of inherited property
Q-12Compare A vs BShould the household live in Property A or keep Property B as the home?Property B is already owned; the inheritance of Property A does not change B's status. Q-13Future lawWill the federal estate-tax exclusion or California rules change before transfer?Current rules and indexed thresholds are known for 2026 (and 2027 where published); they are not future law.Future legislation, indexing, and jurisdictional rates decades out are unknown.Re-check IRS estate-tax updates, BOE Prop 19, and California law at the transfer year.IRS · Estate and gift tax updates · California BOE · Proposition 19
Q-14Decision methodWhich option — occupy, rent, or sell Property A — is best, and why?No option can be ranked without verified property-specific inputs; all three cases must be compared on the same horizon with taxes, transaction costs, vacancy, and after-tax reinvestment.Actual tax bill, insurance, rent, sale proceeds, and portfolio return are unknown.Build and stress the three verified cases; choose after transfer facts are known.IRS · Estate and gift tax updates
