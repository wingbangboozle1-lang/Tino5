Executive summary

A disciplined inheritance decision

Separate the future trust inheritance from the already-owned property; put decision-reversing unknowns first, use current official sources, and delay irreversible choices until transfer facts are verified.

Decision status: No path is a verified household recommendation yet. The facts most likely to change the result remain open; start with these questions rather than treating any model ranking as a decision.

Answer-changing unknowns — read first

1. Trust and transferConfirm the trust instrument, title, who has decision authority, and the actual transfer date. The expected planning window is not a verified transfer date.
2. Estate-wide tax and liquidityEstablish the whole estate, prior taxable gifts, any portability election, filing obligations, and liquid assets for expenses or tax. Estate tax is separate from income-tax basis.
3. Basis and timing of a saleObtain a defensible date-of-death valuation and the records needed to establish basis. Compare a sale in the transfer tax year with later sale timing; do not assume a tax result without the actual trust and tax facts.
4. Property A use and Prop 19Will an heir genuinely make A a principal residence, and for how long? Eligibility, occupancy and filing deadlines, reassessment, and the cost of leaving early can change the comparison.
5. Rental and operating evidenceFor A, establish market rent, vacancy, insurance, utilities, repairs, and maintenance from current records or written quotes. For already-owned B, separately verify existing rental income, leases, the vacant unit and possible owner occupancy, and its costs. Unknown figures stay blank.
6. After-tax path and risk policyCompare income tax, property-tax reassessment, and transfer costs separately. Ask advisers about section 121, depreciation and recapture, whether a like-kind exchange is available and suitable, estate liquidity, and the investment policy before any proceeds are invested.

Decision sequence

  1. Before transfer — establish trust authority, title, estate inclusion, basis records, prior gifts, portability, and current law.
  2. At transfer — obtain a date-of-death valuation and determine Prop 19 eligibility, occupancy and filing deadlines, and the property-tax outcome for A.
  3. Compare A's choices — occupy, rent, sell, or combine paths at the same dates and horizons, using verified costs, defensible rent, after-tax treatment, reserves, and liquidity.
  4. Keep B separate — record its existing ownership, tenants and rental income, possible vacant-unit occupancy, tax basis, and repair obligations independently.
  5. Write the decision down — agree on the evidence threshold, investment policy, adviser questions, decision, and date before an irreversible action.
