Inherit A, keep B, sell A unless you want to live in it

Two properties, three real options, one 25-year arithmetic comparison. Everything below is reproducible from the published assumptions on the Projections page.

The short answer. Inherit A, keep B, and treat selling A as the default. It is the only path that converts an illiquid, reassessed, low-yielding asset into a diversified portfolio without a capital-gains bill. Living in A is a legitimate choice, but it is a lifestyle purchase: on these assumptions it costs about $9,166,400 of wealth over 25 years compared with selling.

What this site is, and what it refuses to do

Verified rules, published arithmetic

Statutes, thresholds and rates were read on official pages on 11 November 2026 and are linked in every row. Projections are arithmetic on named assumptions — never presented as facts. Money is rounded to the nearest $10; property anchors are banded to $1,000 or $100,000 so they cannot be matched back to a public assessment record.

Deliberately withheld

Exact values, bills, rents, addresses, parcel numbers and family details. The historical assessment and tax-bill series is not republished: rounding to $10 does not anonymize a public record. Where a figure cannot be verified, the cell is blank and the item is on Flagged for review. Nothing here is individually tailored advice.
