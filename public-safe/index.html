A disciplined inheritance decision

Separate the future inherited home from the already-owned rental property; use official rules, protect private figures, and delay irreversible choices until verified transfer facts exist.

Executive summary: No defensible financial winner can be named today from official public sources alone. Property A is expected to arrive through a trust around the 2040–2050 planning window; Property B is already owned and is not part of that inheritance. Treat them as separate tax, operating-cost, and housing decisions.
Property A — future inheritanceExpected to arrive through a trust around 2040–2050. Transfer date, trust terms, income-tax basis, current insurance quote, utilities, and rent are not publicly verified here.
Property B — already ownedAlready owned by the future inheritor; it is a separate asset and is not inherited through the trust. Its basis, multi-unit legal status, operating costs, and rental economics require separate review.
Key distinctionInherited property (A) generally receives a date-of-death stepped-up basis and may qualify for the Prop 19 family transfer; already-owned property (B) keeps its existing basis and receives no step-up from this inheritance.
Known current federal ruleFor 2026 the federal basic estate-tax exclusion is $15,000,000 per decedent. It is an estate-level calculation, not a per-heir income-tax-free cash allowance.

Recommended decision sequence

  1. Before transfer — establish trust authority, title, estate inclusion, basis records, family eligibility, and current law.
  2. At transfer — obtain a date-of-death valuation and determine Prop 19 eligibility, deadlines, and the property-tax outcome for A.
  3. Compare options for A — occupying, renting, and selling, using actual quotes, the actual local tax bill, defensible rent, reserves, and after-tax investment alternatives.
  4. Hold B separately — keep B's ownership, existing tenants, housing use, tax basis, and repair obligations in a separate ledger; do not treat A's inheritance as B being inherited.
  5. Review suggestions first — use the Suggestions page to compare candidate actions and choose a path before implementing any change.

As a provisional strategy—not a numeric recommendation—avoid committing to A before verifying the trust and tax treatment. The verified rules below describe current law, not 2040–2050 law.

Scope. This public site contains no household names, addresses, ZIP codes, parcel identifiers, household bills, rents, property values, or account details. Not advice. Rules and thresholds may change before a future transfer. Confirm the then-current law and obtain legal, tax, insurance, appraisal, and investment advice for the actual estate.
