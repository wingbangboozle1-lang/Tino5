Black swans & uneven economic paths

Real economies do not grow smoothly for 50 years. This page grounds each stress in a verified historical episode, then applies non-linear paths to the same fictional starting point so live-in, rent and sell-and-reinvest can be compared on identical shocks.

How to read this page. Historical rows are verified as stated. Scenario rows are analyst assumptions — never forecasts, never assigned a probability. All scenario money uses a fictional $1,000,000 home, a fictional $1,000,000 sale portfolio and a fictional $15,000 annual operating budget; none describes either real property. No taxes, fees, rent, withdrawals or sale costs are included, so the tables isolate the shape of the path, not a final ranking.

Decision consequence

Under smooth assumptions the diversified portfolio pulls far ahead; under an early-crash path that advantage roughly halves, and under stagflation the first five years — not the long-run average — decide whether cash runs out. Renting concentrates local housing, tenant and cost risk in one asset; occupying converts return into housing consumed; selling converts housing risk into market-timing risk. The robust plan is therefore conditional: preserve the ability to choose at the transfer date, keep a liquid reserve, diversify any sale proceeds, and re-run all three options on the same shock path and the same end date before deciding. No single winner is certified on this page.
