S-1Obtain current written insurance quotes for Property A (primary-residence and landlord forms) before deciding to occupy or rent.Premium can dominate carrying costs and may differ sharply from any estimate; insurability affects both options and the sale price.Choose insurer, coverage amount and deductible; budget the quoted premium.California Department of Insurance · Home insuranceProposed
S-2Verify Prop 19 eligibility for Property A (both parties principal residence, one-year move-in, timely claims) before the transfer.Determines whether A keeps a low taxable value or is reassessed to full market value at transfer—the single largest tax lever.Confirm eligibility with the assessor and file required claims on time.California BOE · Proposition 19Proposed
S-3Obtain a date-of-death appraisal and document the stepped-up income-tax basis for Property A.Basis sets the tax on any later sale and must be supported by records.Engage a qualified appraiser at transfer; retain all basis documentation.IRS · Basis of inherited propertyProposed
S-4Confirm the lawful unit status and rental compliance of Property B (3 units; 2 rented, 1 vacant).Renting a non-conforming unit creates legal and financial risk; the vacant unit is a separate decision from the rented units.Review permits, local rental ordinance and lease records before acting on B's vacant unit.California Legislative Information · CodesProposed
S-5Compare net, not gross, outcomes: after-tax cash flow for rent, total cost of ownership for occupancy, after-tax proceeds for sale.Gross rent ignores vacancy, tax, insurance, management and repairs; comparing it to sale proceeds is misleading.Adopt a common horizon, tax treatment and risk measure for all three options.IRS · Basis of inherited propertyUnder review
S-6Stress-test each option under black-swan regimes (inflation, insurance shock, housing drawdown, interest rates) over the holding horizon.Growth and inflation are never constant over 50 years; a single average can hide ruinous sequences.Decide how much downside risk each option can tolerate before choosing.Federal Housing Finance Agency · House Price IndexProposed
S-7Do not implement any change to A or B until transfer facts (title, basis, tax bill, insurance, legal unit status) are verified.Irreversible choices made on unverified inputs can create large, avoidable tax and cost consequences.Owner sign-off on the verified fact base before acting on any suggestion.IRS · Basis of inherited propertyUnder review
S-8Set aside a capital reserve for repairs and maintenance (a percentage of value per year) for whichever property is held.Regular maintenance and capital items are unavoidable and grow with the property.Choose reserve percentage and funding source.California BOE · How property is assessedProposed
S-9Re-check the federal estate-tax exclusion and California rules at the transfer year, not today.The 2026 exclusion is indexed and legislation can change; a 2040–2050 transfer uses then-current law.Calendar a review well before the expected transfer.IRS · Estate and gift tax updatesProposed
S-10If Prop 19 is unavailable for A, seriously compare selling at transfer and reinvesting the after-tax proceeds.Reassessment to full market value can make holding expensive relative to reinvestment.Compare after-tax sale proceeds against projected holding costs and appreciation.California BOE · Proposition 19Proposed
How to use this page: each suggestion is a candidate action with its rationale, the owner decision it requires, and a status. Review the list, move items between Proposed → Under review → Approved / Declined, and implement only Approved items—and only after the transfer facts in the Process page are verified. No suggestion is a recommendation to act; each requires property-specific verification first.

Scope. This public site contains no household names, addresses, ZIP codes, parcel identifiers, household bills, rents, property values, or account details. Property A is the future trust inheritance; Property B is already owned. Those roles are stated without publishing identifying financial inputs.

Not advice. Rules and thresholds may change before a future transfer. Confirm the then-current law and obtain legal, tax, insurance, appraisal, and investment advice for the actual estate.
