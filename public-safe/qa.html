Q-1What is the federal estate-tax threshold before tax is due?Gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts for the year of death.For 2026 the federal basic exclusion is $15,000,000 per decedent; the top estate-tax rate above it is 40%. A 2040–2050 figure is not a forecast.Verified for 2026IRS · Estate tax
Q-2Is there a California estate or inheritance tax?Date of death and California law.No California estate-tax return for deaths on or after 1 January 2005; California has no inheritance tax; an inheritance is not income to the recipient.VerifiedCalifornia State Controller · Estate tax · California FTB · Gifts and inheritance
Q-3Does the heir pay income tax on receiving the inheritance?Nature of the bequest and later income it produces.No. The inheritance itself is not income; later income it produces (rent, interest, gain on sale) is taxable.VerifiedCalifornia FTB · Gifts and inheritance
Q-4What is the income-tax basis of an inherited home?Date-of-death fair market value and estate documentation.Generally the date-of-death fair market value (stepped-up basis); alternate valuation and consistent-basis rules can apply.VerifiedIRS · Basis of inherited property
Q-5Does Prop 19 let the family home keep a low taxable value after transfer?Transferor/transferee eligibility, principal-residence use, filing deadlines, value limit.Yes, only if both parties use it as their principal residence and claims are timely; value above current taxable value plus the adjusted $1,000,000 allowance increases taxable value.Verified; eligibility unconfirmed for ACalifornia BOE · Proposition 19
Q-6What happens to property tax if Prop 19 does not apply?Assessed value at change of ownership.The property is generally reassessed to market value on change of ownership, raising the taxable value and tax bill.VerifiedCalifornia BOE · How property is assessed
Q-7Can we live in A and keep B rented at the same time?Ownership, legal unit status, and occupancy of each property.Yes—they are separate decisions. A's inheritance is not treated as B being inherited; B's rented and vacant units are evaluated on their own records.Verified frameworkIRS · Basis of inherited property · California BOE · How property is assessed
Q-8What are the inheritance thresholds and how much headroom before tax?Estate size (all assets), lifetime taxable gifts, exclusions and elections.Federal: exclusion plus deductions determine taxable estate. California: none (post-2005 deaths). Headroom must be computed from the full estate, not one home.Estate size unknownIRS · Estate tax · IRS · Form 706 instructions
Q-9What will A be worth and what will its tax be at transfer?Transfer date, market value, assessed value, tax-rate area, Prop 19 outcome.Cannot be known now. Project from a verified transfer-year baseline using named growth assumptions; tax from the actual secured bill.Future values unknownFederal Housing Finance Agency · House Price Index · California BOE · How property is assessed
Q-10What does it cost to hold A (insurance, water, electricity, repairs)?Current quotes, utility tariffs, inspection, contractor bids.Owner-supplied estimates are not verified; obtain current written quotes before relying on any figure.Quotes neededCalifornia Department of Insurance · Home insurance · California Public Utilities Commission · Water · California Public Utilities Commission · Net energy metering
Q-11Which path—occupy, rent, or sell A—is best?Verified tax, insurance, rent, maintenance, sale costs, portfolio alternatives, and risk tolerance.No defensible winner from public sources alone; compute net after-tax outcomes under the same horizon and stress each option.Cannot rank yetIRS · Basis of inherited property · California BOE · Proposition 19
How to use this page: the right-hand column shows whether each item is verified from an official source, under review, or unverified. Items marked unverified are exactly the information that must be discovered before a decision can reasonably be made. Treat every answer as current law only; re-check at the time of transfer.

Scope. This public site contains no household names, addresses, ZIP codes, parcel identifiers, household bills, rents, property values, or account details. Property A is the future trust inheritance; Property B is already owned. Those roles are stated without publishing identifying financial inputs.

Not advice. Rules and thresholds may change before a future transfer. Confirm the then-current law and obtain legal, tax, insurance, appraisal, and investment advice for the actual estate.
