Inheritance process

From trust distribution to a signed decision

The transfer is an administrative process with hard deadlines. Missing the one-year homeowners' exemption claim is the most expensive clerical error available here, because it changes whether the Prop 19 exclusion applies from the transfer date or only from the year of a late claim.

Trust, not probate, is the assumption. A funded trust normally avoids probate, which is why the timeline below is built around trustee duties and assessor filings rather than court filings. If A is not actually held by the trust at death, probate is back on the table and the timeline changes (CA Courts · Probate). The small-estate affidavit is not a substitute for real property; it covers personal property only (CA Courts · Small estate affidavit).

The three deadlines that matter most

150 days — assessor notice

A death transfer must be reported to the county assessor within 150 days where the estate is not probated. The penalty for ignoring the assessor's request is the greater of $100 or 10% of the new tax, capped at $5,000 or $20,000 depending on homeowners'-exemption eligibility (SF Assessor-Recorder · Change in ownership).

1 year — homeowners' exemption

The Prop 19 exclusion applies from the transfer date only if the heir files for the homeowners' or disabled-veterans' exemption within one year. Later filing makes the exclusion prospective only (BOE · Proposition 19 · BOE · Homeowners' exemption claim (BOE-266)).

3 years — exclusion claim

The BOE-19-P claim is due within three years of the transfer and before any transfer to a third party, with a late-claim window of six months after a supplemental or escape assessment notice (BOE · Prop 19 fact sheet (Pub 801)).

9 months — Form 706

A federal estate-tax return, if required, is due nine months after death; a six-month extension is available, and portability depends on a timely filing (IRS · Estate tax FAQ).

Documents to assemble before 2040

  • Trust instrument, all amendments, schedule of assets and any pour-over will.
  • Grant deeds, title insurance, mortgage or reverse-mortgage statements, and liens.
  • Insurance policies, the solar agreement and utility bills for the last 24 months.
  • Property-tax bills, assessment notices and any exemption or exclusion correspondence.
  • Improvement receipts, permits and prior appraisals for basis and for the step-up record.
  • Leases, deposits, rent histories and rent-board registration for B.
