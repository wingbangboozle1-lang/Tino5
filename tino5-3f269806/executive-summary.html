Executive summary

The one-page answer, with what is verified and what is still missing.

Bottom line

1. Do not rent Property A. Under Proposition 19, the parent-child exclusion applies only if the heir lives in the home as a principal residence. Renting it puts the home back to full market value for tax. On owner estimates, 2026 tax would move from about $7,210 a year to about $41,410 a year (source: S08, S10).

2. If the heir moves in, the tax stays far higher than today but well below market. With the $1,044,586 add-on (S09), the taxable value would be about $1,595,130 in 2026 terms, giving about $21,310 a year at the owner-implied rate.

3. Living versus selling is not settled. It depends on one number you do not have yet: the rent you would otherwise pay (or the rent you would forgo) for comparable housing. In the 2040 base case, living in A only wins if that avoided rent is above about $16,900 a month. Below that level, the base case favours selling and reinvesting, but only while the after-tax return assumption holds (see scenarios).

4. Federal estate tax is a decedent-level question. The federal basic exclusion is $15,000,000 per person for 2026 deaths (S01). Heirs do not pay it. California has no estate or inheritance tax (S07).

What is verified, and what is not

  • Verified: federal exclusion and filing threshold, 2026 (S01, S02); gift exclusion $19,000 (S03); top estate rate 40% (S04); basis at date of death (S05); no CA estate tax (S07); Prop 19 rules (S08); the $1,044,586 add-on (S09); reassessment at date of death and the 150-day filing (S10); Prop 13 base rules and the $7,000 homeowners' exemption (S11); Santa Clara 1% plus bonds (S12); SF FY 2026-27 rate (S13).
  • Not verified: every owner figure (values, assessed values, tax bills, rent, utilities, insurance); the water provider for Property A; the solar credit; Property B's billed tax (it implies a rate about 2.14%, well above the SF rate). These are blank in the verified tables and flagged.
  • Assumptions: inflation 2% (anchored to the Fed goal, S14); property growth 2%; after-tax reinvestment return 4%; selling costs 6%; upkeep; insurance growth. All are labelled and can be changed in the model.

Leading path (decision as it stands)

  1. Treat renting Property A as the weakest path on current law unless the rent covers about $20,510 a month gross (2040, base).
  2. Collect the three missing inputs before choosing between living and selling: an appraisal for A, a comparable-rent study for A, and the trust's distribution terms. See questions and gaps.
