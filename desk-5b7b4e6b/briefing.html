Attorney and CPA briefing sheet

Prepared 2026-11-17. Property A: future trust inheritance, Santa Clara County. Property B: already owned by the heir, San Francisco County, three units, two let. Money rounded to the nearest $10.

1. What the household is deciding

Whether to live in Property A, let it, or sell it and invest the proceeds — and, if selling, whether to invest at once or in stages. Property B is held throughout and can house the heir at no loss of rent, which is why living in Property A does not save rent.

2. The four questions we need answered in writing

  1. Proposition 19. Confirm the exclusion is available only while Property A is the transferee's principal residence, and confirm the one-year filing deadline. Source: boe_nr2502, boe_pub801
  2. Basis. Confirm that the basis of Property A is its fair market value at the date of death, and therefore that a same-year sale produces almost no gain — and confirm what a like-kind exchange would or would not achieve. Source: irs_p551, irs_p544
  3. Estate tax. Confirm whether the estate will exceed the federal filing threshold of $15,000,000 for a 2026 death, and whether portability and prior taxable gifts change the answer. Source: irs_estate, irs_706
  4. Running costs and local taxes. Confirm the parcel's tax rate area and its published rate, the insurance position on both properties, and the effect of the parcel tax approved on 3 November 2026. Source: scc_trb, cdi, sfdoe_2026
