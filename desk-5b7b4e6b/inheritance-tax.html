Estate tax, basis and assessment

Three different taxes are easy to confuse, and they are governed by different rules. This page keeps them apart: the estate tax is charged on the estate at death, the income tax basis decides what is taxed if the property is later sold, and the property tax assessment decides the annual bill.

The one point that changes the plan

Because the basis of inherited property is its fair market value at the date of death, the gain on selling Property A in the same tax year as the inheritance is close to zero. That makes a like-kind exchange close to pointless for Property A — there is almost no gain to defer — and it makes the same-year sale the cleanest way to release the property. Property B is different: it is already owned, so its gain has been building for years. Any exchange idea belongs to Property B, not to Property A.
