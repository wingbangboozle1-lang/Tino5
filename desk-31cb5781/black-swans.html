Uneven-economy (black swan) reasoning

Growth and inflation are never constant over 50 years. The desk models Property A under uneven regimes anchored to the real historical record (inflation and interest-rate cycles), not a single smooth line. See the cbe desk's block-resampled inflation paths (1914–2026 CPI-U) for the full engine.

Phased-investing path (sale proceeds)

If Property A is sold at inheritance, do not dump 100% of proceeds into equities on day one. Instead:

  1. Months 0–6: Pay taxes, selling costs, and set aside an operating reserve.
  2. Months 6–36: Park the balance in 3-month to 2-year U.S. Treasuries (liquid, ~{t3}–{t2}% yields per the Treasury curve).
  3. Months 36–60: Phase into a diversified portfolio (e.g. 25% of the equity allocation per quarter over one year) so purchase is spread across market conditions.

Softening 1929 / 2008 crash entry risk

A lump-sum equity purchase at a 1929- or 2008-style peak can take years to recover in real terms. By holding in Treasuries for 24–36 months and phasing in, the heir buys across conditions: if a crash arrives during the hold, Treasuries preserve capital and the later equity purchases enter at lower prices. Reinvestment risk (the risk that rates fall while waiting, or that equities rally while in cash) is shown beside the headline — it is real, but smaller than the risk of a single bad lump-sum entry.
