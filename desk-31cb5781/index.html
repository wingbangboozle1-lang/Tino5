Clean dashboard for deciding what to do with an inherited house. Property A passes through a trust around 2040–2050; Property B is already owned outright. The two are analysed separately because one is inherited and the other is not.

Start here. The core question: when Property A passes to the heir (around 2040–2050), should the heir move in, rent it out, sell it and reinvest, or combine those over time? This desk answers with arithmetic that can be re-run, sourced law, and stress cases that break the arithmetic.

What would change the answer (read first)

The decision is not determined by today's headline price. It flips on three break-evens:

  • Gross rental yield on Property A vs. the cost of carrying it (tax, insurance, maintenance, water/electric). If net rent after carrying costs > after-tax portfolio return, keeping wins.
  • Price growth vs. portfolio return. If the house appreciates faster than a diversified portfolio (after tax), keeping compounds better.
  • Whether the heir moves in within one year of transfer. That is the switch between Prop 19's capped reassessment (child occupies) and full market reassessment (child does not occupy).
These break-evens are shown on the Executive Summary and the Scenarios page, with what-if controls so you can test them yourself.
