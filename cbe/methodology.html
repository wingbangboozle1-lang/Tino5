Privacy. No name, street address, parcel identifier, account number, postcode or city appears anywhere on this site. The two properties are called Property A and Property B. Every dollar figure is rounded to the nearest $10 so that a published number cannot be matched back to a public record. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published because they are statewide constants and identify nobody.

How every number on this desk was produced, what is deliberately not modelled, and what is kept off the site.

How the model works

StepHow it is computedStatusSource
Market valueThe owner's 2026 figure grown at the assumed rate, year by year, to the horizon.derivedNo official source — the starting figure is the owner's own, so every value downstream is a projection and none is sourced
Assessed value before the transferThe owner's assessed value trended at no more than 2% a year, which is the Proposition 13 factor.verifiedCalifornia State Board of Equalization
Assessed value if the market fallsThe lower of the trend and the market value, which reproduces the Proposition 8 decline-in-value rule. The bill then falls with the market.verifiedCalifornia State Board of Equalization
Assessed value at the transferWith the exclusion: the factored base year value, plus any amount by which market value exceeds the cap, which is the market value less the allowance. Without it: the full market value. The exclusion protects the allowance and nothing more.verifiedCalifornia State Board of Equalization
Property taxThe assessed value times the all-in rate derived from the owner's own records, held constant in real terms across the projection.derivedCalifornia State Board of Equalization
