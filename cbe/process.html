What actually happens when a property held in a family trust passes, in the order it happens, with the deadline for each step and the document that imposes it.

Two dates decide the tax outcome. You must occupy the home within one year of the transfer and file the homeowner's exemption within one year. Miss either one and the Proposition 19 exclusion is lost, and the assessed value resets to full market value — on the projections used here that is the difference between a $67,320 bill and a $87,250 bill in the first full year, or $19,930 a year, and it then compounds for as long as you hold the property.

Before the transfer

#StepWhy it matters, and the deadlineStatusSource
1Confirm the property is held in the trust, and that the trust is the kind the exclusion recognisesTransfers through a trust are eligible, because the trust is looked through to the present beneficial ownership. But an entity is not: property held by a legal person does not qualify, so the property must not have been moved into a company or a limited liability company.verifiedCalifornia State Board of Equalization
2Keep it as the settlor's principal residenceThe exclusion requires the home to be the principal residence of the transferor. A home that has become a rental does not qualify. Letting it out before the transfer would end the exclusion permanently, and nothing restores it.verifiedCalifornia State Board of Equalization
California State Board of Equalization
3Do not transfer it into a legal entityMoving the property into a company, including a single-member company, would make it ineligible for the exclusion and would also end the homeowner's exemption.verifiedCalifornia State Board of Equalization

In the first sixty days

#StepWhy it matters, and the deadlineStatusSource
5The trustee serves the statutory notificationWithin 60 days of the death the trustee must serve a notification on every beneficiary of the trust and every heir of the deceased settlor, in the manner provided by Probate Code section 1215, to the last known address.verifiedCalifornia Legislative Information
6Obtain a date-of-death valuationThe value at the date of death sets the income tax basis of what is inherited, and it sets the new assessed value if the exclusion turns out not to apply. A valuation obtained later is harder to defend than one obtained at the time.verifiedInternal Revenue Service

Property tax filings

#StepWhy it matters, and the deadlineStatusSource
7Notify the county assessor of the change in ownershipA change in ownership statement is required, and the assessor then decides whether to reassess and to what value.verifiedCalifornia State Board of Equalization
8Move in within one year of the transferThe law requires the transferee to occupy the family home within one year of the transfer for the exclusion to apply as of the date of transfer. This is the single most valuable date on this desk.verifiedCalifornia State Board of Equalization
9File the homeowner's or disabled veterans' exemption within one yearFiled on the form the Board prescribes with the county assessor. The exclusion is not available without it, and if it is filed after the year has passed the exclusion cannot be applied retrospectively once the property has been sold.verifiedCalifornia State Board of Equalization
California State Board of Equalization
10File the exclusion claim within three yearsThe statute ends the window at whichever comes first of three years, a transfer to a third party, and the date an eligible transferee no longer occupies the residence. A claim is also treated as timely if it is filed within six months of the mailing of a supplemental or escape assessment notice.verifiedCalifornia Legislative Information
11Answer any notice of potential eligibility within 45 daysIf the assessor writes to say you may be eligible, a certified claim is due within 45 days. The assessor may send a second notice giving 60 days. Miss both and a late claim can carry a one-time processing fee of the lesser of the assessor's actual cost and $175.verifiedCalifornia Legislative Information
12Note that the claim itself is confidentialA claim filed under this section is not a public document and is not open to public inspection. Only the parties, their legal representatives, the trustees and the executor or administrator may see it.verifiedCalifornia Legislative Information
13Expect a supplemental or escape assessmentAfter a change in ownership the assessor may issue a supplemental bill for the part of the year after the transfer, or an escape assessment if the value is determined late. Budget for a one-off bill in the first year.verifiedOffice of the Treasurer and Tax Collector

14Work out whether an estate tax return is requiredA return is required if the gross estate, increased by adjusted taxable gifts and with the specific gifts listed in the statute, exceeds the threshold for the year of death: $15,000,000 for 2026 and $15,480,000 for 2027. California imposes no estate tax at all, so this is the only death tax to consider.verifiedInternal Revenue Service

15File within nine months, or extendThe return is due nine months after the date of death. An automatic six-month extension of time to file is available on Form 4768 filed by the due date.verifiedInternal Revenue Service
16File even if no tax is due, if portability is wantedA return must be filed to elect portability of a deceased spouse's unused exclusion, regardless of the size of the estate. Where no filing requirement existed, a simplified late election is available under the revenue procedure the Service issued for that purpose.verifiedInternal Revenue Service

Afterwards
