Privacy. No name, street address, parcel identifier, account number, postcode or city appears anywhere on this site. The two properties are called Property A and Property B. Every dollar figure is rounded to the nearest $10 so that a published number cannot be matched back to a public record. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published because they are statewide constants and identify nobody.

On the central assumptions, selling Property A at the transfer and reinvesting the proceeds produces more wealth than keeping it — by about $3,802,370 in present value if you live in it, and $3,883,440 if you let it. That result is not robust, and this page says precisely how fragile it is.

Read this before the numbers. The ranking between keeping and selling is decided by one thing: whether the house compounds faster than a diversified portfolio after tax. On the assumptions used here the house grows at 4.0% and the portfolio at 7.0% less a 0.6% tax drag. That 2.4 point gap, compounding for thirty-one years, is the whole of the $3,802,370 gap. Nothing else on this desk moves the answer as much. The break-even rows below exist so that you do not have to trust either number.
The gap shrinks when the future stops being smooth. The $3,802,370 above comes from a model in which inflation is a constant 3.0%. Replayed against real five-year stretches of the official inflation record, the median gap narrows to $327,960 and keeping wins outright in about 36% of the 1,200 paths — the middle half runs from −$1,140,440 to $368,400. Decide on the break-evens below and on whether you want to live in the house, not on the headline gap. The black-swan page shows the whole distribution.

What the recommendation is, stated plainly

1. If you intend to live in Property A, occupy it — and claim the exclusion. Occupying beats letting on the full economic comparison by $81,070, and it is the only route that keeps the assessed value below market value. Move in within one year of the transfer and file the homeowner's exemption within one year, or the exclusion is lost outright.
2. If you will not live in it, the financial case favours selling over letting on these assumptions, by $3,883,440 in present value. Letting only wins if the house can be let for more than 5.99% of its value a year, which is $32,620 a month at the projected 2045 value.
3. The tax tail is large and it is not the one people expect. The Proposition 19 exclusion does not freeze the tax bill. Because the projected market value at the transfer ($6,531,230) is far above the value cap ($2,293,960), the exclusion saves only the allowance itself — $1,491,930 of taxable value, worth $19,930 in the first year. The bill still jumps from roughly $7,210 today to about $67,320 after the transfer.
4. The estate tax question is about the money, not the house. California imposes no estate, inheritance, succession or legacy tax at all. The federal exclusion is $15,000,000 for 2026 and $15,480,000 for 2027, and is projected at $24,143,520 by 2045 on the assumptions used here. Property passing at death receives a step-up in basis, so the appreciation built up during the settlor's lifetime is not taxed as a capital gain.
