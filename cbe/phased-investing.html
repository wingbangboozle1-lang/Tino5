  • Method and privacy
Privacy. No name, street address, parcel identifier, account number, postcode or city appears anywhere on this site. The two properties are called Property A and Property B. Every dollar figure is rounded to the nearest $10 so that a published number cannot be matched back to a public record. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published because they are statewide constants and identify nobody.

Test parking hypothetical sale proceeds in short Treasuries for 24–36 months, then phasing into stocks. This is an assumption-driven stress illustration, not a forecast or recommendation.

What would change the answer? Net proceeds after taxes and costs; actual Treasury yields and reinvestment rates; investment allocation; spending needs; and market return sequence. Short maturities reduce price sensitivity if held to maturity, but rolling securities creates reinvestment risk.

What-if controls

Illustration

CaseEnd valueInterpretation

Equal monthly purchases, constant user-entered annual Treasury yield compounded monthly; no fees, taxes, inflation, dividends, or recovery. Each equity purchase is assumed to encounter the same hypothetical decline. A lump-sum investment is exposed to the decline immediately. This does not replay historical 1929 or 2008 returns.

Risks beside the headline

  • Reinvestment risk: future maturities may only reinvest at lower yields; the fixed-yield control does not capture this, so it can overstate parking returns.
  • Opportunity cost: rising markets during the delay can make phased investing lag lump-sum entry.
  • Sequence risk: a decline after most tranches are invested can cause materially different losses.
  • Taxes/inflation: nominal values omit both; verify treatment with a tax adviser.
