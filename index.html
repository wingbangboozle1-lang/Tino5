Answer-changing unknowns — review these first

  1. Trust terms, ownership and actual transfer date

    Confirm the trust instrument, title, transfer authority, estate inclusion and timing. The expected planning window is not a verified transfer date.

  2. Whole-estate exposure and liquidity

    Establish estate composition, prior taxable gifts, any portability election, filing obligations and available cash. Estate tax and liquidity are separate from income-tax basis.

  3. Basis, valuation and sale timing

    Obtain a defensible date-of-death valuation and basis records. Compare a sale in the transfer tax year with a later sale; do not assume the income-tax result.

  4. Property A occupancy and Prop 19 eligibility

    Will an heir genuinely occupy A as a principal residence, meet the filing deadlines and stay long enough? Do not assume an exclusion or ignore reassessment if those conditions are not met.

  5. Property income and operating evidence

    Verify A's achievable rent, vacancy, insurance, utilities and repairs. For already-owned B, separately verify existing rental income and leases, its vacant unit and possible owner occupancy, and its own costs. Unknown values stay blank.

  6. After-tax path and investment policy

    Compare income tax, property-tax reassessment and transfer costs separately. Ask advisers about section 121, depreciation and recapture, like-kind exchange suitability, reserves, risk tolerance and the policy for any proceeds.
