Inheritance decision desk

One question: when the inherited house arrives, should the heir live in it, let it out, or sell it and invest? This site answers it once, with sourced law, ranges instead of points, and a stated break-even rather than a forecast.

Why there is now one site instead of sixteen. Earlier versions of this repository published sixteen separate decision sites. An audit found that fifteen of them served household sales values, a household rent, prior-year assessed values and the two city names from a public web host. They have been withdrawn from publication and replaced by the single desk below, which publishes no household figure at any precision. Read what was exposed and what remains.

How to read any number on this site

LabelMeaning
VERIFIEDQuoted from an official document that was opened and read, with the link in the row or the source register.
STATUTORYA figure fixed by law, quoted exactly. Never rounded, because rounding a legal threshold misstates it.
ASSUMPTIONA modelling input with no official source. Ranged, and every page that uses it says so.
UNVERIFIEDSomething that should exist but could not be confirmed. Left blank and flagged rather than filled in.
CARRIEDA figure that does not exist yet — a statutory amount not yet published, or a date not yet known. Carried forward and marked, never extrapolated.

Money on the desk is rounded to the nearest $10 and derives from a declared round modelling base, not from either property's value. The published conclusions are break-evens, a ranking and a share of resampled histories; the tests show the ranking is identical across a tenfold range of property values and the break-even moves by about one tenth of a percentage point across it. No name, street, parcel number, postcode, city or neighbourhood appears anywhere on the site. Not legal, tax or investment advice.
