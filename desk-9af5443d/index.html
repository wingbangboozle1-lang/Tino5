Gap-filling decision desk: Prop H with official terms, Form 540 MHST, rent comparables, maintenance plan, estate liquidity, distribution chart and decision log.

What this desk adds. The merged desks already cover the core live / rent / sell model, the phased-investing (Black swan) path, the briefing sheets and the question registers. This desk closes the remaining gaps from the full requirement list: Prop H modelled with its official terms (both the Nov-2024 firefighter-pension measure and the Nov-2026 Muni parcel tax), the Form 540 1% tax and capital-gain treatment read line-by-line from the official FTB instructions with the sensitivity value updated, Cupertino rent comparables from official Census ACS evidence, a five-year maintenance and capital plan, an estate liquidity plan, a distribution chart and a decision-and-date log.

What would change the answer (read first)

  • Whether the heir occupies Property A within one year of transfer. That is the switch between the Proposition 19 capped reassessment and full market reassessment — the single largest lever on Property A's tax bill.
  • Whether any single year pushes CA taxable income over $1,000,000. A large one-year gain (sale, vesting, business sale) then adds the 1% MHST on the excess — see the updated sensitivity on the CA income tax page.
  • SF Measure H (Nov 2026). A new annual parcel tax on SF parcels from the FY2027-28 bill adds a small, fixed cost to Property B (base $249 for multifamily; size-based additions unverified until building area is known).
  • Rent evidence for Property A. The official Census ACS median gross rent for the area is $3,500+/month; a property-specific written rent opinion is still needed before ranking the rent path on its own numbers.
  • Estate size versus the $15,000,000 federal exclusion. The "considerable money" in the inheritance decides whether an estate liquidity plan and Form 706 deadlines matter at all.

Glossary

Terms used across this desk. Wording is the desk's plain-English gloss, not statutory text; the statutory term of art is linked in the relevant row.

TermMeaning
Assessed valueThe value the county uses to calculate property tax; under Prop 13 it grows at most 2%/yr until a change in ownership reassesses it to market value.assumption
Behavioral Health Services Tax (MHST)California's 1% surtax on taxable income over $1,000,000, computed on Form 540 line 62 (FTB instructions).assumption
Black swan regimeA stress scenario built on real historical drawdowns (1929, 2008) rather than a constant growth rate; growth and inflation are never consistent over 50 years.assumption
Change in ownershipDeath, transfer or new construction that resets assessed value to market value unless an exclusion (e.g. Proposition 19) applies.assumption
DSUE / portabilityDeceased spousal unused exclusion: a surviving spouse can inherit a deceased spouse's unused federal estate exclusion if portability was elected on Form 706.assumption
Factored base year valueA property's assessed value carried forward at up to 2%/yr under Prop 13; the starting point for Proposition 19 calculations.assumption
Like-kind exchange (1031)A tax-deferred exchange of certain property; must be structured before a sale and requires professional advice.assumption
MHST sensitivityThe table showing how much the 1% surtax adds once CA taxable income crosses $1,000,000 in a single year.assumption
Parcel taxA per-parcel tax (not based on value); SF Measure H (2026) is a parcel tax added to the regular property tax bill.assumption
Phased investingParking sale proceeds in short-term Treasuries for 24-36 months, then moving into stocks gradually to soften crash-entry risk.assumption
Proposition 19 allowanceThe inflation-adjusted dollar amount above the parent's factored base year value that can pass without reassessment for a qualifying primary residence ($1,044,586 for 2025-2027).assumption
Reinvestment riskThe risk that cash parked for the phased-investing path earns less than the portfolio it replaces, or that markets rise while the money is parked.assumption
Step-up in basisThe heir's tax basis in inherited property resets to fair market value at the date of death (IRC §1014).assumption
