How the inheritance process would look — step by step

California revocable living trust administration (no probate if the trust is funded). Deadlines and forms are from official sources. Costs are planning ranges — get quotes from a CA estate attorney and CPA.

Before death (do now, 2026–2030s) — so 2040s is painless

  1. Confirm the trust is funded (title transferred)

    Real estate must be deeded into [TRUST NAME] and recorded with the county. Bank/brokerage accounts retitled. An unfunded trust does not avoid probate for assets left outside it.

    Official CA Courts — courts.ca.gov (probate/trust self-help) · SF Probate Court: sf.courts.ca.gov — Trusts

  2. Name a successor trustee and keep documents findable

    Successor trustee ([TRUSTEE]) needs: trust document, recorded deed, account list, mortgage/insurance info, and a one-page “where things are” memo. Do not publish this memo on the website.

    Official SF Probate Court — Trusts

  3. Order a baseline appraisal + solar tariff check (Cupertino)

    Keep a current appraisal and the solar Permission-to-Operate / tariff vintage (NEM 2.0 vs Net Billing). These control basis, Prop 19, and the “solar profit” question (Flagged F-07).

    Official CPUC — Customer Generation / NEM & Net Billing

  4. Decide the live-in plan before death

    Prop 19 requires the beneficiary to occupy Cupertino as primary residence within 1 year of transfer. If the plan is to rent or sell, Prop 19 is forfeited — model that branch now (see Decision Analysis).

    Official BOE Publication 801 — Prop 19 Fact Sheet

At death — trust administration (weeks to a few months, no probate court if funded)

  1. Obtain certified death certificates; successor trustee accepts

    Order multiple certified copies. Successor trustee confirms acceptance in writing and obtains a new EIN for the trust if needed.

    Official CA Courts · SF Probate Court — Trusts

  2. Notify beneficiaries & heirs within 60 days

    Trustee must send statutory notice (Prob. Code §16061.7) within 60 days of the trust becoming irrevocable. This starts the 120-day contest window. Keep proof of mailing.

    Official SF Probate Court — Trusts (notice & accounting duties)

  3. Secure & insure the house; keep paying mortgage, tax, utilities, insurance

    Change locks, notify insurer (trust as additional insured / named insured as trust requires), keep water/electric on. Do not let coverage lapse — a lapse can void a claim.

    Official CA Dept. of Insurance — insurance.ca.gov — Market Share (Homeowners)

  4. Inventory & value all trust assets as of date of death

    Retrospective appraisal for Cupertino (and SF if in trust). This appraisal is the stepped-up basis (IRC §1014) and the Prop 19 fair-market value. Keep it permanently.

    Official IRS Publication 551 — Basis of Assets

  5. Pay debts, final expenses, and taxes in priority order

    Funeral/medical, secured debts, then unsecured. File the decedent’s final Form 1040; file trust Form 1041 (federal) and Form 541 (CA) for the administration year. File Form 706 only if the estate exceeds the federal exclusion (see Tax Law).

    Official IRS — Working Families Tax Cuts · CA FTB

  6. Prop 19 filings (Cupertino, live-in branch only) — 1-year clock

    Move in as primary residence within 1 year of transfer. File homeowners’ exemption (BOE-266) within 1 year. File BOE-19-P with the Santa Clara County Assessor within 3 years (earlier is better — late filing loses retroactive relief). Keep the assessor’s determination letter.

    Official BOE NR 25-02 · BOE Pub. 801 · Santa Clara Assessor: sccassessor.org

  7. Distribute per trust terms; provide accounting; close

    Distribute cash, securities, and real property per [TRUST NAME]. Trustee provides accounting to beneficiaries. Retain records (appraisal, tax returns, Prop 19 determination) for basis purposes for as long as the property is held.

    Official SF Probate Court — Trusts

If the trust was not funded: Assets left outside the trust go through probate (public, court-supervised, 12–18 months typical, statutory fees). That is why Step 1 (funding) matters more than the trust document itself. Probate thresholds and fees change — confirm current figures with the court at the time.
