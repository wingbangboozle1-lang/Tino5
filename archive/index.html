Bottom line (conditional — read the caveats)

On 2026 snapshot math, “live in Cupertino + keep SF as a rental” is the lowest-cost, highest-certainty path — if you actually move into Cupertino as your primary residence within 1 year of transfer and file on time. That preserves a partial Prop 19 exclusion worth about $13,950/yr versus renting Cupertino (which forfeits the exclusion entirely), avoids a second housing cost, and keeps the SF rental’s $85,800/yr gross income working. Selling Cupertino and reinvesting only wins on paper if long-run stock returns stay well above housing appreciation and you do not need the home — see the break-even math on the Decision Analysis page. Renting Cupertino is the weakest option on current numbers because the break-even rent (≈ $61,630/yr under Prop 19, ≈ $75,570/yr fully reassessed, before vacancy/management) is high and the Prop 19 exclusion is lost.

This is a conditional planning conclusion from modelled scenarios, not a guarantee and not personal financial advice. It depends on the transfer year (2040–2050), the Prop 19 cap in that year (unknown — it resets every 2 years), market growth, and whether the cash portion of the inheritance pushes the estate over $15M/$30M. Full reasoning, formulas, and sensitivities are on the Executive Summary and Decision Analysis pages.
