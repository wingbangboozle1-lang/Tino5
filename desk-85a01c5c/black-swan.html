Black swan & phased investing

Reinvestment risk, stated beside the headline. The phased-investing path - park the after-tax sale proceeds in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries for 24-36 months, then phase into equities - adds about $1,300-$2,100 per $10,000 over ten years on a flat market (at the 7.0% base-case return assumption; the official ERP record's mean total return is 13.2%). Subtracts nothing in the official 2008 replay at trough+60 (phased ends $15,930 vs $12,590 all-in at the peak) and roughly halves the 1929-style drawdown damage. It is insurance whose premium is measurable, not a free lunch.

Why a flat projection is not enough

Growth and inflation are never consistent over 50 years. The official record shows annual equity price changes from about -38% to +31% and bill rates from 0.03% to 14.03% between 1975 and 2025. Two official episodes anchor the stress replays:

Reading: all-in at the 1929 peak leaves $1,100 at the trough and needs the official 25 years to regain the pre-crash price level; parking 36 months preserves the full principal to $10,580 through the trough and phases in at prices far below the peak. In the 2008 replay every phased variant ends ahead of all-in-at-the-peak at trough+60, on the official ERP recovery path. Both replays use price indexes only (no dividends), interpolated between official endpoints; the 1929 recovery and parking yield are labelled assumptions. If no crash arrives, the cushion costs $1,300-$2,100 per $10,000 over ten years (base-case return). That pair of numbers - not either alone - is the decision.

What the replay does not cover

  • Dividends are excluded from both replays (price index), which understates the all-in strategy's recovery; the pages say so wherever the numbers appear.
  • The 1929 monthly path is an interpolation between official endpoints (plus the official mid-November 1929 "lost almost half" point); the real path was jagged.
  • Taxes on the parking leg (interest is ordinary income) are noted, not modelled - a CPA question for the year of the sale.
  • A crash during the park that coincides with a housing drawdown (about -30% mid-2006 to mid-2009) hits the keep paths too; the decision is between two risk exposures, not risk versus safety.
