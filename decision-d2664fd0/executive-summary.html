Executive summary

The whole decision on one page. Property A passes through a trust around 2040–2050; Property B is already owned outright. Figures are model outputs rounded to the nearest $10; statutory amounts are quoted as published.

Today's model answer. Sell and invest has the highest present value at the 7.00% portfolio assumption, by $3,802,360 over Live in. That answer depends on the inputs below. Each is tested on the What-if page.

What would change the answer

  1. How well the sale proceeds grow. Selling beats living in only if the proceeds earn about 5.20% a year before the 0.6% tax drag. The 3-month Treasury bill is 4.32% and the 2-year note 4.88% (13 Nov 2026), both below that line. At the bill rate, selling gives $5,937,320 against $7,193,900 for living in. [S1 U.S. Treasury]
  2. When the transfer happens. Each year of delay moves the answer. Compare 2040 and 2050 on the What-if page.
  3. House price growth. Selling keeps its lead only while house prices grow more slowly than 5.58% a year (model break-even). At 3.0% a year, selling leads Live in by $4,618,350; the lead closes as growth rises toward the break-even.
  4. Whether the Proposition 19 exclusion is claimed. The exclusion applies only if the heir moves in and makes the home a principal residence within one year of the date of death. Without it, the house is reassessed to market value on transfer. [S7 BOE Proposition 19]
  5. Rent and running costs for Property A. No rent quote, insurance quote, or official utility figure for Property A has been verified. Living in is valued at the rent it avoids: at the 2.50% placeholder yield it is worth less than selling. Living in matches selling only if that avoided rent is at least 4.68% of value a year (model break-even). unverified
  6. Federal estate size. The 2026 filing threshold is $15,000,000 per person. An estate under it files no Form 706. [S5 IRS]
