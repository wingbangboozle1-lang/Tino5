Mixed paths

Not only the three pure choices. Mixed paths combine occupying, renting, and selling over time. All figures are model outputs rounded to the nearest $10, based on labelled assumptions — not predictions.

Live in, then sell

The heir moves into Property A within one year of transfer (securing Prop 19's capped reassessment), lives there for a period, then sells. If the home was the primary residence for 2 of the 5 years before sale, §121 may exclude up to $250,000/$500,000 of gain. Present value depends on how long the heir lives there and the sale price — test on the What-if page.

Rent, then sell

Property A is held as a rental for a period, then sold. Rental income is taxable; depreciation is taken; depreciation recapture (up to 25% on unrecaptured §1250 gain) applies at sale. Step-up basis at death resets the starting point. Present value depends on rent, expenses, and holding period — test on the What-if page.

Park 24–36 months, then phase into stocks

Sale proceeds are parked in 3-month–2-year Treasuries for 24–36 months after inheritance, then phased into equities in equal tranches. This softens 1929/2008 crash-entry risk: if equities fall right after the sale, the parked portion is preserved and the phased portion buys at lower prices. See Black swan & phased investing for the crash-entry replay.
