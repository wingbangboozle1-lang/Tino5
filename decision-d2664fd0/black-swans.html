Black swan & phased investing

Uneven-economy conditions: growth and inflation are never consistent over a 50-year period. The phased-investing path parks sale proceeds in 3-month–2-year Treasuries for 24–36 months after inheritance, then phases into equities, tested against the official 1929 and 2008 drawdowns.

Phased-investing path (Treasuries 24–36 months, then phase into stocks)

Sale proceeds are parked in 3-month–2-year Treasuries for 24–36 months after inheritance, then phased into equities in equal monthly tranches. This tests how parking softens 1929/2008 crash-entry risk: if equities fall right after the sale, the parked portion is preserved and later tranches buy at lower prices.

Reinvestment risk beside the headline. The sell option is only as good as the return on the money it releases. In a crash-entry year, immediate equity purchase can lock in large losses; the phased-investing path reduces that exposure but extends the time the money earns Treasury yields (4.32% 3-month / 4.88% 2-year, 13 Nov 2026 — below the model's 5.20% break-even). Real economic conditions: baseline projections

Growth and inflation are never consistent over a 50-year period. Baseline projections should use ranges, not points: house-price growth (3%–6%/yr), portfolio return (4%–8%/yr), inflation (2%–4%/yr), and carrying costs escalating with inflation.
