Executive summary

  • Scope. Property A is a Santa Clara County home expected to pass to a trust between 2040 and 2050. Property B is a San Francisco County three-unit building already owned. This public site analyses Property A in detail. Property B's figures and tenancy details are held internally and not published (privacy); its open questions are listed on the Questions page.
  • Options. For each possible transfer year (2040, 2045, 2050), the model values three options 10 years later: live in the home, rent it out, or sell at transfer and reinvest the after-tax proceeds. Values are shown relative to the live option (= 100). All values are nominal.
  • Result. In 24 of 27 modelled cases, selling and reinvesting gives the highest value. Living in the home is highest in 3 cases (high growth with the lowest reinvestment return). Renting is highest in 0 cases.
  • Size of the gap. In the base case for a 2045 transfer with equity-like reinvestment, selling gives about 25% more value than living in the home. Renting gives about -8% relative to living in the home. These are estimates and depend on the rent and tax-rate assumptions (see Sensitivity).
  • Prop 19. If an heir occupies the home, the taxable value resets to the factored base value, or to market value less the transfer allowance if that is higher. If the home is rented, it is reassessed to market value at transfer. This rule is the key legal input.
  • Federal estate tax. The 2026 basic exclusion is $15,000,000 and the 2027 amount is $15,480,000 (both read in IRS sources: the estate-tax page and Rev. Proc. 2026-35 section 3.44). California has no estate tax for deaths on or after 1 January 2005 (State Controller). Japan: if any heir is a Japanese tax resident, Japanese inheritance tax can apply to worldwide assets at 10%-55% above a basic deduction of JPY 30,000,000 plus JPY 6,000,000 per statutory heir (NTA No.4102, verified); a US-Japan estate and gift tax treaty is in force. See the Inheritance page.
  • Limits. The rent proxy is a secondary HUD figure and is probably low for this property. The property-tax rate is an assumption. Every such input is tagged ASSUMED or OWNER on the Assumptions page.
