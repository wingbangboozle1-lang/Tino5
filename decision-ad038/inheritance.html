Inheritance steps and tax thresholds

Step-by-step process after a death, and the thresholds that matter for this estate.

StepWhat to doStatusSource
1Confirm the death; gather the death certificate, trust documents and asset list.Practical step; no figure.S06
2The successor trustee takes control of the trust assets. Confirm the trust terms with an attorney.Legal step; no figure.MODEL
3Obtain a date-of-death appraisal. The date-of-death value sets the federal basis (step-up).Basis rule verified.S19
4Check whether the Prop 19 exclusion applies. The heir must claim the homeowners' exemption and make the home a principal residence within one year.Verified rule; claim within one year.S06 S03
5Report the change in ownership to the county assessor: within 150 days of the date of death if there is no probate; at the same time the inventory and appraisal is filed if the estate is probated.Verified (BOE change-in-ownership FAQ; R&T 480)S26

8Decide whether to keep, rent or sell. Sales carry costs and possible capital gain; the step-up limits the gain.Model step.MODEL S17

Japan inheritance tax thresholds (verified)

Whether Japan taxes this inheritance depends on the tax residence of each heir at the date of death, not on where the house sits. The figures below were read in the National Tax Agency's own page (Tax Answer No.4102) on 13 November 2026. Amounts in JPY are exact statutory figures.

ItemRuleStatusSource
Basic deductionJPY 30,000,000 + JPY 6,000,000 x number of statutory heirs (adopted children counted with limits)Verified (NTA No.4102)S27
Rate on the excess (taxable estate)10% to 55% progressive (8 brackets)Verified (NTA)S27
Return and payment deadlineWithin 10 months of the day the heir learns of the deathVerified (NTA No.4102)S27
Scope for an heir resident in JapanAll property acquired, wherever situated (US real property included)Verified (NTA No.4102 scope table)S27
Scope for an heir neither resident in Japan nor a Japanese nationalProperty situated in Japan onlyVerified (NTA No.4102 scope table)S27
Double taxation reliefA US-Japan estate and gift tax treaty is in force; the United States may tax US real property; credits relieve double taxationVerified that the treaty is listed (IRS treaty table); treaty text not readS28

Example (statutory arithmetic, not a forecast): a single heir with no other statutory heirs gets a JPY 36,000,000 basic deduction. If any heir is a Japanese tax resident, get cross-border advice before choosing between occupying, renting and selling; the Japanese tax can exceed the US estate tax because the US exemption is high and Japan's basic deduction is comparatively low.
