Executive Summary — Inheriting the Cupertino & San Francisco Homes by Trust (c. 2040–2050)

Prepared 2026-11-10 · All findings verified against official sources where available · Unverified items are blank or flagged, never invented · Not legal/tax advice.

Bottom line up front

  1. Federal estate tax is very unlikely to apply. The 2026 basic exclusion is $15,000,000 per person ($30,000,000 with spousal portability), indexed for inflation from 2027. Projected to ≈$20,620,000 (2040) and ≈$23,100,000 (2045) per person at a 2.3% inflation assumption. The two houses are projected at ≈$11,310,000 combined in 2045. Estate cash would need to exceed roughly $11,800,000 (single filer) or $34,900,000 (couple, portability) on top of the houses in 2045 to trigger any 40% federal estate tax.
  2. California has no estate, inheritance, or gift tax — the only material "transfer tax" in CA is property-tax reassessment (Prop 13/Prop 19), not a tax on receiving the inheritance. Federal basis steps up to fair market value at death (IRC §1014), so an early sale of either house triggers little or no capital-gains tax.
  3. Property B (SF rental) is the money-loser if held. Full reassessment at the owner's death would reset its taxable base to ≈ market value — ≈$21,240/yr at 2026 values (vs ≈$2,440/yr today, a ~9× increase), rising to ≈$49,020/yr by 2045. Net rental yield (~3.2% today) collapses to ≈1–2%. Fed by: $7,150/mo rent vs ≈$27,620/yr operating costs (2026). Best options: sell at inheritance and reinvest, or — only if genuinely desired — one heir occupies a unit as a principal residence (possible partial Prop 19 exclusion for the family-home portion; needs County Assessor confirmation).
  4. Property A (Cupertino) is the keeper — but only as a home. Move in within 1 year of transfer to claim the Prop 19 exclusion (2025–27 cap: $1,044,586 + factored base-year value; projected ≈$1,292,000 by 2045), capping the reassessment to ≈$78,730/yr (2045) instead of ≈$95,870/yr (full reassessment) — saving ≈$17,130/yr, indexed. If A is not wanted as a residence, sell immediately at death (stepped-up basis ≈ $0 gain) and reinvest ≈$6,760,000 (2045) at a 7% assumption → ≈$26,160,000 after 20 years vs ≈$17,250,000 if kept at 4.5% appreciation.
  5. Recommended sequence (highest certainty, lowest cost, 2040–2050 horizon): (1) confirm trust + pour-over will before death; (2) at death, file the County Preliminary Change of Ownership Report within 150 days on both properties; (3) claim Prop 19 (form filed within 3 years — occupants within 1 year), elect portability Form 706 if needed within 9 months (or within 5 years under Rev. Proc. 2022-32); (4) decide A: live-in (Prop 19, best non-financial/lifestyle case) or sell + reinvest (best pure math) — do not hold A as a rental; (5) decide B: sell unless one heir truly wants to occupy it; (6) reinvest net proceeds into a diversified index portfolio + Treasuries; (7) revisit every 2 years against the biennial Prop 19 cap adjustment (next: Feb 2027) and inflation-indexed federal exemption.

What this means for 2040–2050

The inheritance architecture is sound (revocable living trust + pour-over will avoids probate and preserves the §1014 step-up). The three real decisions are all after death: Property A — keep as principal residence (Prop 19) vs sell + reinvest; Property B — sell vs occupy-one-unit; cash — stay under the indexed exclusion or accept 40% on the excess. Every scenario, projection table, and the step-by-step settlement checklist used to reach these recommendations is documented in the linked sections. Nothing on this page is personal financial advice; it is a research brief for the family attorney/CPA to validate.
