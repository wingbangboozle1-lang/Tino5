Exclusion path: $15,000,000 (2026, OBBBA §70106, fixed) indexed at 2.3%/yr from 2027. Houses path: A $3,100,000 + B $1,800,000 growing 4.5%/yr. Headroom = exclusion − houses (single) or 2× exclusion − houses (couple with timely portability election, Form 706 within 9 months; late elections to 5th anniversary per Rev. Proc. 2022-32). If estate cash + houses ≤ threshold → $0 federal estate tax. If above → 40% on the excess only. California: no estate/inheritance/gift tax at any level (FTB; federal "pick-up" tax = $0 for deaths after 2004). Neither county nor city levies an inheritance tax. The only California-specific friction is property-tax reassessment (next page-tables) and ordinary income tax on rent.

What "considerable amount of money" would trigger federal tax? In 2045, the estate needs to exceed ≈$23,110,000 (single) or ≈$46,210,000 (couple) total — i.e., cash + financial assets of roughly $11,800,000 (single) / $34,900,000 (couple) in addition to the two houses. If the family's cash+portfolio exceeds that, the excess is taxed at 40% (after deductions/credits). Note the 2026 $19,000 annual gift exclusion per recipient and the $15,000,000 GST exemption (2026) for dynasty-trust planning. S.4196 (pending, not law) would restore $3,500,000 / 45% — see pending legislation.

D.3 · The step-up in basis — the single most valuable rule in this plan

Under IRC §1014 and IRS Pub 551, inherited property's basis = FMV at the date of death (or 6 months later, alternate valuation, IRC §2032, if elected). Revocable living trust assets are treated as inherited at death — the trust does not defeat the step-up. Practical effect: all pre-death appreciation (e.g., decades of Silicon Valley growth) is never taxed — federally or in California — if the heirs sell soon after death. An immediate post-death sale locks that in at ~0 capital-gains cost (only ordinary income tax and any recapture on depreciated rental components applies if held as/ converted from a rental).

D.6 · Decision rules (remove the uncertainty)

  1. Cash-first rule: estate must stay ≥ $0 headroom. If portfolio + cash are projected above the D.2 threshold at the planned death year, implement exemption-saving tools NOW (annual gifts $19,000/recipient; ILIT for life insurance; credit-shelter bypass planning via the trust; spousal portability election discipline).
  2. Property A rule: occupy within 1 year → keep (Prop 19). Otherwise list within 180 days of death certificate. Never default-rent it.
  3. Property B rule: sell unless (a) heir occupancy is genuine and (b) the SF Assessor pre-confirms the Prop 19 family-home treatment for the occupied portion and (c) post-reassessment two-unit NOI still covers ≥110% of debt-free carrying costs. Otherwise, reinvest proceeds.
  4. Reinvestment rule: net proceeds → (i) Treasury ladder/I-bonds to cover 24 months of household needs, (ii) 80–90% diversified low-cost index (total US/total intl/bond per risk tolerance), (iii) 10% tactical/opportunity bucket. No single-property concentration.
  5. Tax-hygiene rule: file Form 706 if required OR to elect portability (even below threshold); file county PCOR within 150 days of death for both properties; file BOE parent-child exclusion claim within 3 years (1-year occupancy test); get DOD appraisals (licensed CA appraiser) as the §1014 basis evidence.
  6. Review rule: re-run this model biennially (Prop 19 cap + federal indexation both adjust on schedules) and after any change in law (S.4196, SALT sunset 2030, exemption tweaks).
