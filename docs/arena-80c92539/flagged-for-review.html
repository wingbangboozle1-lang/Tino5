Flagged for Review — Unverified, Anomalous, or Assumed Items

Why this page exists. The verification standard for this project: every published figure traces to an official source, or it is flagged here — never silently estimated. Nothing below blocks publication; you (the owner) review these at your leisure. Each item shows what is known, what is not, and exactly how to verify it yourself. Order: highest-impact first.

F-01 · Property B effective tax rate anomaly — derived 2.14% vs expected ~1.18%

What the data shows: User-provided 2025 bill $2,440 (rounded) ÷ user-provided 2025 assessed value $114,250 (rounded) ≈ 2.14% — nearly double SF's ~1.18% composite (1% Prop 13 + voter-approved debt). A healthy bill on that base would be ≈$1,350.

Likely explanations: (a) the bill includes parcel taxes/direct assessments (SF: schools, parks, library bonds) and/or the FBYV excludes separately-assessed land/new-construction components; (b) the "assessed" figure transcribed is the land-only or improvement-only component; (c) transcription drift (a transposed digit is possible in any manual table); (d) prior-year supplemental bill riding along.

Impact: Low for decisions (both keep and sell scenarios use market-value reassessment, which is verified). All projections for B use 1.18% on new base, not the anomaly. The legacy-base path is used only illustratively.

How to verify: pull the 2025-26 secured tax bill (SF Treasurer & Tax Collector, sftreasurer.org) and compare the "ad valorem" and "direct charges" sections; compare with SF Assessor-Recorder assessed roll detail (sfassessor.org). If the bill genuinely exceeds 1.18%, the difference is parcel/direct charges — expected, and it follows the property after transfer.

FLAGGED — do not use 2.14% for any projection.

F-02 · Property B Prop 19 treatment of the occupied unit (triplex) — assessor-specific

Prop 19 excludes reassessment for a transfer of a parent→child family home the child will occupy within 1 year. Whether a single unit inside a 3-unit building (with two rental units) qualifies — in whole or in part — is not answered by the statute at face; BOE guidance addresses siblings/family homes but the multi-unit apportionment is county-assessor-specific and the FMV allocation among units (land vs improvements per unit) must be agreed.

What this study assumed: Property B is assumed not to qualify (worst realistic case); Property A is assumed to qualify under the 1-year occupancy test.

How to verify: pre-death inquiry with SF Assessor-Recorder (sfassessor.org) + written confirmation; alternatively claim and appeal if denied (Assessment Appeals Board). Require appraisal allocation (unit-by-unit) from the DOD appraiser.

FLAGGED — $20,000+/yr property-tax swing; highest-value unanswered question on this site.

F-05 · Cupertino market rent — deliberately not estimated

To evaluate "hold A as a rental," a verifiable current market rent is required. None was provided, and fabricating one would breach the no-invention rule. Decision-relevant answer still exists: with full reassessment (≈$41,500/yr at 2026 values vs ≈$7,400 today) plus ≈6%/yr insurance growth and ≈$15,500/yr maintenance, a single-family lease would need gross rent of roughly $4,750–$5,500/mo merely to break even on carrying costs at a ≥0% margin, before income-tax drag — a figure the family should test against any real offer rather than this guess.

How to close: one written CMA from a local broker or 3 comparable signed leases.

FLAGGED

F-08 · California House Price Index trend vs 4.5% assumption

FHFA purchase-only CA HPI: +0.38% 1-yr (2025Q2), +41.38% 5-yr, +320% since 1991; all-transactions 5-yr ≈ +40%. A 4.5%/yr nominal assumption is below the 30-year trend and roughly double the 18-month trend — chosen deliberately. Every projection table shows 3.0%/6.0% sensitivities (Decision Analysis D.5). If the family believes CA HPI mean-reverts lower for a decade (San Francisco ex-urbanization, insurance-capacity migration), re-run with 3%.

Assumption — labeled everywhere

F-09 · Transaction-cost, reinvestment-return, and maintenance assumptions

5.5% sale cost (negotiable, post-NAR-settlement), 7.0% portfolio return (below S&P 500's ≈10% nominal long-run; above 10-yr TIPS real yield), maintenance 0.5%/1.0% of value, utility escalators 3–4% — none of these has an official source; all are standard planning assumptions, labeled on every table, with sensitivities adjacent. Maintenance especially: SF multi-unit building may warrant 1.5–2.0%; that strengthens the sell case, not the keep case.

F-13 · Privacy decision on raw history publication

The year-by-year raw assessed-value/tax-bill tables supplied in the brief (2014–2026 Property A; 2016–2025 Property B) are not reproduced on this public site. If the owner wants the raw tables published anyway (e.g., inside a private fork with auth), that is a one-line change + privacy sign-off — flagged here for an explicit decision.
