B.5 · Recommendation

Default recommendation: sell Property B at or soon after the trust's distribution and reinvest the net proceeds (diversified equity index + Treasury ladder). The legacy 1.1%-of-FBYV property tax was the entire economic case for keeping it; the transfer destroys it. Expected outcome vs keeping: ≈$3,000,000 more wealth after 20 years (≈$15,190,000 portfolio vs ≈$12,200,000 house + net rent of the two rented units; taxes on eventual portfolio liquidation not included), with lower effort, lower concentration risk, and no CA landlord-regulatory exposure.

Exception — keep only if ALL true: (1) an heir wants it as a principal residence; (2) the SF Assessor confirms the occupied portion qualifies for a Prop 19 partial exclusion (family-home portion only); (3) the remaining two units' rent covers ≥ the post-reassessment carrying cost with margin; (4) the household accepts tenant law obligations. Even then, re-run this page's numbers before and after the first property-tax bill arrives post-transfer.
Portfolio/diversification note. Concentrated single-asset real estate (two Bay Area houses, one metro) is the risk this plan retires. After both decisions execute, the estate's risk profile moves from ~100% illiquid Bay Area real estate to a diversified portfolio — the goal stated in the brief ("maximize profit, lower costs") is best served by that shift, subject to the household-housing exception above.
