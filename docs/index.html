Bottom line — base case, transfer in 2045

Recommendation: sell Property A at inheritance and reinvest the net proceeds

Under the model’s base case (4% annual appreciation, 5% nominal portfolio return), a sale at the date-of-death value produces $10,000,370 of wealth after ten years, versus $7,847,410 for renting it out and $7,208,090 for occupying it. The driver is the stepped-up basis: basis equals fair market value at death (IRS Publication 551), so an immediate sale realises essentially no taxable gain.

Two hard numbers make the case unusually clear. Renting would have to achieve roughly $16,260 per month in 2026-equivalent dollars to match selling, against an official HUD 4-bedroom benchmark of $5,010 per month. Occupying would need roughly 8.49% annual appreciation to match selling, against a 4.00% base assumption. Both hurdles are far above the inputs.

Property B is the reason the decision is not forced by housing need: it is a low-assessed-value, income-producing property where the beneficiary can reside without giving up the two rented units (client claim, unverified). Occupying Property A would also be required to satisfy Proposition 19 — and would conflict with continuing to use Property B as the principal residence.

Highest-priority open question

Was Property A the transferor’s principal residence, and will a beneficiary occupy it within one year? Proposition 19 depends on both. If either fails, the property is reassessed to full market value at the date of death (BOE Publication 800-9). See FLAG-010.
