The three options

All three are evaluated at 2040, 2045 and 2050 using a ten-year horizon, 4% base appreciation, 5% nominal portfolio return, 6% selling cost and a 37.10% combined tax on gains above the stepped-up basis. Option 1 assumes Prop 19 eligibility; Option 2 assumes full market reassessment because the home is not owner-occupied; Option 3 sells at the date-of-death value and invests the net proceeds.

Operating cash flows are shown before beneficiary-level income tax (filing status, other income and depreciation basis are unknown — FLAG-012). Adding that tax would reduce, not improve, the occupy and rent results, so the ranking is conservative.

Conclusion

3 · Sell & reinvest is the base-case recommendation. Renting needs roughly $16,260 per month in 2026-equivalent dollars (against a $5,010 official HUD benchmark); occupying needs roughly 8.49% annual appreciation (against 4.00% base). Even adding the full HUD benchmark back as imputed housing value does not close the gap.

The recommendation is conditional, not absolute: verify Prop 19 eligibility, re-run after the 2026 election results are certified, and re-run if the stepped-up basis rule changes. Non-financial objectives may legitimately dominate.
