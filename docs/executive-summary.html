Executive Summary

Findings, reasoning and the recommended plan — with clear numbers

Official values are linked to government sources Client inputs are blank in official columns Model outputs are labelled and flagged

1. The decision in one paragraph

Property A (Cupertino, ZIP 95014) is expected to pass through a trust between 2040 and 2050. The beneficiary already has a residence at Property B (San Francisco, ZIP 94122), a three-unit building with two rented units. Three choices exist: live in Property A, rent it, or sell it and reinvest after tax. Under the base-case model, 3 · Sell & reinvest is the highest-wealth option: $10,000,370 of ten-year wealth versus $7,847,410 (rent) and $7,208,090 (occupy).

2. Important numbers

Estate tax: the 2026 federal exemption is $15,000,000 per person, or $30,000,000 for a married couple using portability, with a 40% top rate. California and local estate/inheritance tax are $0 by statute (R&T Code §13301). The estate’s liquid-asset value was not supplied, so no estate-size-specific figure can be stated; a scenario matrix is on the Estate Tax Thresholds page.

Property tax: at a 2045 transfer the preserved Proposition 13 base produces about $10,710 per year. A full reassessment at death produces about $87,210 per year — a jump of roughly $76,500 a year. Even if every Proposition 19 condition is met, the modelled tax is about $66,480 per year: the exclusion saves only $20,730 a year because the projected market value ($6,531,230) far exceeds the factored base year value ($802,030) plus the inflation-adjusted allowance (projected $1,552,200; the only official amount today is $1,044,590).

Insurance: no ZIP-specific official premium was retrieved. The model uses the California Department of Insurance statewide average of $1,570 per year, flagged as unverified for these properties.

Property B: modelled 2026 net operating cash flow is about $73,100 per year before income tax, on a very low assessed value. That income is not lost if the beneficiary lives there (client claim, unverified).

Why selling wins

Basis steps to fair market value at death (IRS Publication 551). Selling immediately therefore realises essentially no capital gain. Holding instead creates a large taxable gain over the next decade, taxed at up to 37.10% combined (20% federal + 3.8% NIIT + up to 13.3% California), after any §121 exclusion. Section 1031 cannot defer a gain reinvested in stocks — it applies only to business/investment real property.

3. Conditions that would change the recommendation

Proposition 19 eligibility. The home must be the transferor’s principal residence and become the transferee’s principal residence within one year, with a homeowners’ exemption filed within one year and BOE-19-P/19-G filed within three years. None of these facts is confirmed. If ineligible, full reassessment applies.

Repeal of the stepped-up basis rule. If §1014 were repealed before the transfer, an immediate sale would realise the decedent’s full unrealised gain and the sell-and-reinvest advantage would shrink sharply.

Appreciation above 8.49% a year, or portfolio returns far below the 5% base, would favour holding.

Market rent above $16,260 per month (2026-equivalent) would favour renting. The official HUD 4-bedroom benchmark is $5,010 per month.

Ballot and legislative outcomes. November 3, 2026 California results are not certified until December 11, 2026; measures touching high-income tax rates, a one-time wealth tax and local revenue limits are unresolved.

Non-financial preferences. The model does not price attachment, control, or the option value of a family home. These can rationally override a wealth ranking.

1. Privacy. No personal information appears anywhere on this site; identities, street addresses, trust names and account identifiers were replaced with placeholders. Any further data added must follow the same rule.

2. Confirm the decedent’s principal residence before the transfer and decide whether a beneficiary will actually occupy Property A within one year.

3. Diarise the statutory clocks: trustee notice within 60 days; BOE-502-D within 150 days; occupancy and homeowners’ exemption within 1 year; BOE-19-P within 3 years; Form 706 within 9 months if the estate exceeds the exemption.

4. Quantify the gross estate, including lifetime taxable gifts and whether a portability (DSUE) election exists.

5. Obtain ZIP-specific insurance quotes for a primary-residence policy (95014) and a landlord/dwelling-fire policy (94122) through the CDI comparison tool.

6. Reconcile the client tax/assessment figures against actual tax bills: they imply effective rates of 1.34% and 2.13%, above the official ad valorem rates.

7. Re-check official 2026 election results after certification and re-run the model with the law then in force.
