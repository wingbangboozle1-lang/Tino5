Recommendation and reasoning

  1. Decide the occupancy question first. Under Prop 19, only an heir who occupies the inherited home as a primary residence within 1 year (and claims the homeowners' exemption, filing BOE-19-P within 3 years) keeps a protected taxable value (Santa Clara County Assessor — Prop 19 parent-child transfer: move in within 1 year, homeowners' exemption, claim BOE-19-P within 3 years). Renting it out triggers full reassessment to market value — projected $72,580/yr tax in 2045 vs. $35,170/yr if lived in, a difference of about $37,420/yr.
  2. If the heir can live in Cupertino: keep the house and live in it (Scenario A). It preserves the Prop 19 tax base, avoids a taxable sale (step-up basis IRS — Publication 559, Survivors, Executors and Administrators: basis of inherited property = FMV at date of death (IRC 1014)), and the SF triplex can be fully rented. This is the lowest-tax, lowest-friction path.
  3. If the heir prefers to stay in San Francisco: sell the Cupertino house (Scenario C). The stepped-up basis makes the sale nearly tax-free; about $5,109,720 net (6% costs, 2045 projection) invested at a 6%/yr assumption grows to about $6,837,950 by 2050 — likely more wealth than the rental scenario can produce after the $72,580/yr reassessed property tax, unless Cupertino market rent is exceptionally high (unverified — flagged).
  4. Renting the Cupertino house (Scenario B) is tax-disadvantaged (full Prop 19 reassessment) and should only be chosen if verified market rent comfortably exceeds the $72,580/yr tax plus insurance, maintenance, vacancy, and management costs.
  5. Estate tax: with the projected 2045 federal exemption of about $26,302,590 (assumption) and no CA estate tax (FTB — California estate 'pick-up' tax is zero (no CA estate tax since federal credit repealed post-2004)), the estate is likely under the federal threshold unless the cash portion is very large — the cash amount is internal-only and flagged for review.
