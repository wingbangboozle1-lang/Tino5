priority-critical		Will the $10 rounding actually protect the figures?
You asked for figures rounded to $10 so they cannot be traced. For a single number that is broadly true. For a multi-year series of assessed values it is not: San Francisco publishes its secured roll as open data and the county assessor publishes assessment rolls, so a 13-row history rounded to $10 still matches exactly one parcel. Q-02
priority-critical		Who are the beneficiaries of the trust, and how many?
A single heir can claim the whole Prop 19 exclusion and the whole §121 exclusion. Two heirs holding A as co-owners split both, and the §121 exclusion is $250,000 per person only if each meets the use test. The entire decision changes with the answer, and we do not have it.		Obtain the trust instrument and the schedule of beneficiaries. Do not model a 50/50 split until it is confirmed.Blocked on a documentTopic no. 701
Q-03
priority-critical		Does 'inherit by trust' mean a revocable living trust, and does A pass outright or in further trust?
If A passes into a continuing trust rather than to you outright, the trustee — not you — decides whether to sell, and the Prop 19 exclusion depends on the transferee occupying the home. BOE states plainly that a transfer occurring at death counts as a transfer whether inside or outside a trust, but the beneficiary's powers differ.		Read the trust's distribution article. Ask the trustee in writing whether the beneficiary receives the property or an interest in a trust holding it.Blocked on a documentLTA 2026/026

Q-07
priority-high		Will the federal estate exclusion still be ~$15M in 2045?
It is $15M for 2026 by statute and indexed thereafter, but a future Congress can change it. The estate tax has been repealed, reinstated and re-shaped repeatedly since 2001 — the IRS's own threshold table shows $5M in 2011, $5.5M in 2017 and $11.2M in 2018. A projection to 2045 that assumes indexation is a scenario, not a fact.		Model the estate at transfer with the law then in force. Keep the headroom table on the inheritance page as a live check rather than a fixed plan.Watch itemEstate tax
Q-08
priority-medium		Will Prop 19 itself survive to 2045?
Prop 19 was a constitutional amendment (ACA 11) approved in November 2020 and is not amendable by simple statute, but it can be amended by another constitutional amendment, and the value cap is re-indexed every second February to the FHFA California house-price change. The cap for a transfer after 15 February 2027 is not yet published.		Track the BOE Letters to Assessors each February. Re-run the transfer table when the new allowance is published.Watch itemLTA 2025/009
Q-09
priority-medium		Does the third unit in B satisfy the rent ordinance, and is it registered?
B's third unit is vacant and the owner intends to live in it. San Francisco requires residential units to be reported into the Rent Board Housing Inventory and charges a fee per unit; a rent-increase licence depends on that reporting. Occupying a unit yourself also has its own rules (owner move-in) that we have not verified.		Check the Housing Inventory record for B and confirm that occupying unit 3 is lawful without an eviction or a change-of-use permit.Not verifiedRent Board

priority-medium		Is A's solar credit durable?
The owner reports electricity is free and slightly positive because of solar. Under the net-billing tariff that replaced net metering for new interconnections, export compensation is usually below the retail rate, and legacy net-metering terms run for a fixed period. The credit may therefore shrink or invert.		Find A's interconnection agreement and tariff (NEM or NBT), and the legacy-period end date. Q-11
priority-medium		What was A's basis, and does any of the house need depreciating?
Basis resets to date-of-death fair market value, so the pre-death basis does not matter for income tax — unless A has been rented, or partly used for business, before the transfer. Any depreciation taken by the transferor reduces the carryover in some circumstances.		Obtain the transferor's depreciation schedules and prior returns if A was ever rented.Blocked on a documentPublication 551
Q-12
priority-low		What will A's property tax be if we do nothing for a year after the transfer?
The intergenerational exclusion requires the transferee to occupy within one year and to file for the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption within one year. If that is missed, the exclusion is removed and the full market value is enrolled from the following lien date.		Put the two deadlines — occupancy and exemption filing — on the executor's calendar the week of death, not the week of distribution.Process riskRevenue & Taxation Code §63.2
Q-13
priority-low		Does the county transfer tax apply to an inheritance?
The documentary transfer tax under R&TC §11911 is imposed on a deed by which realty is 'sold'. A transfer by death into a trust or to a beneficiary is normally exempt, but the exemption is claimed on the face of the declaration of trust or the recorded document. We have not read the county's exemption form, so this is flagged rather than answered.		Confirm with the county recorder at the time of recording; the amount at stake is small (0.11% of value) but the paperwork is not.Not verifiedRevenue & Taxation Code §11911

What is already settled

These are no longer open questions, so they should not be re-litigated:

  • California imposes no inheritance tax and no estate tax on the estate of a person dying on or after 1 January 2005 (California Estate Tax).
  • The federal test is estate-level, not per-heir, and the 2026 exclusion is $15,000,000 per decedent (Estate tax · What's new).
  • Property A's income-tax basis resets to date-of-death value, so a sale at the transfer pays no capital-gains tax on the pre-death appreciation (Publication 551).
  • The Prop 13 base is broken by the transfer; Prop 19 limits the damage only if a qualifying child occupies the home (Proposition 19 · Revenue & Taxation Code §63.2).
  • Property B is not inherited and gets no basis step-up; selling it would trigger tax on decades of gain, which is why it is treated separately throughout.
