Decision analysis

Occupy, rent, or sell — and why

The answer, stated carefully. In the central case, selling Property A at the transfer and reinvesting wins in all three transfer windows, and it wins by a wide margin. But the margin is produced mostly by two assumptions — the reinvestment return and A's future growth — neither of which is knowable. The honest conclusion is: sell unless you expect a specific, defensible reason why A will outgrow a diversified portfolio, or unless living in A is worth the difference to you as a preference.

Why Property B is the hinge

Property B is already owned, already protected by its own Proposition 13 base, and already producing rent from two units while leaving a third for you to live in. That means the decision about A is not a housing decision — the housing need is already met. It is a question about where to put roughly three million dollars of concentrated, illiquid, single-asset risk. That framing is what makes selling the default rather than the fallback.

Why occupying A is the weakest path despite the tax break

Occupying A is the only way to keep any of the Prop 19 exclusion, and it saves real money: $23,840 a year at a 2045 transfer. But occupying A also gives up the rent from B's third unit, adds A's full carrying cost, and leaves you living in the more expensive of two houses you own. The tax saving does not cover the rent given up.

What would change the answer

  • Reinvestment return falls toward 3% after tax. The sell advantage narrows sharply and the rent and occupy paths become live options — see the sensitivity table on the projections page.
  • A appreciates at 6% instead of 4%. Renting overtakes selling. That is the same tension the housing-bust regime on the black-swan page tests from the other side.
  • Two or more beneficiaries. The §121 exclusion and the Prop 19 occupancy requirement both get harder; selling becomes more attractive still.
  • The insurance premium is enormous. It is not: the projections page prices each $1,000 of premium and shows it is small next to the spread between the paths.
