Insurance: the blank, priced explicitly

The only official insurance figure that exists is a statewide one: the Department of Insurance's market snapshot reports an average homeowner premium of $1,571 against a national average of $1,512 (Sustainable Insurance Strategy). It publishes no premium for an individual address, and neither property is an average — A is a single-family house in a wildfire-adjacent county and B is a three-unit building needing a landlord policy. Rather than guess, here is what each $1,000 a year of premium does to the 25-year comparison on a 2045 transfer. Add the premium you are actually quoted to the annual cost of whichever path you choose.

Illustrative arithmetic only: undiscounted years of premium, not the model's compounding. It exists to show the scale — insurance at $3,000 a year is $75,000 over 25 years, which is small next to the differences on the decision page. Insurance is therefore an important fact to establish, but it is not the variable that decides the question.
