Executive summary

A disciplined inheritance decision

Executive summary. On the numbers we can defend, the cheapest housing move is to keep Property B as the place you live and to treat Property A as a financial asset, not a home. Selling A at the transfer and reinvesting the proceeds wins the central case by $2,571,930 over renting A out and by $12,495,750 over moving in, on a 25-year horizon from a 2045 transfer. That result is not robust. It flips to "rent A out" if A appreciates at about 4.6% a year or faster for 25 years, or if the reinvested proceeds earn less than about 4.4% after tax. Both of those are things we do not yet know. See the decision page for the conditions under which each path wins.

What the answer actually depends on

1. The reinvestment returnassumption Every planning model of this kind is driven by this one number. At the central 5% after tax, selling wins. Below about 4.4%, renting A out wins outright, because the house's own cash flow and appreciation then beat the portfolio. We have no way to know your future after-tax return, so the whole 3–7% range is reported.
2. How fast A appreciatesassumption Selling stops you owning the house, so it loses whenever house growth beats the reinvestment return. At 4.6% a year for 25 years the paths converge.
3. Whether you would really live in Averified Occupying A is the only route to the Prop 19 exclusion, and it is the worst of the three paths in the central case — the tax saving ($23,840 a year at a 2045 transfer) is smaller than the rent you give up by leaving B's third unit empty and the maintenance you take on.
4. Facts we do not have The trust's terms, the number of beneficiaries, insurance quotes, A's achievable rent, and the size of the cash inheritance. Each one is on the questions page with the specific decision it blocks.

Recommended sequence (do not skip the order)

  1. Nothing irreversible before the facts. The transfer date is a decade or more away; the only work that pays now is collecting documents and keeping the trust and title clean.
  2. Get three insurance quotes for each property. Premium is the largest genuinely unknown cost and the only figure on this site that is still blank.
  3. At the transfer, decide the occupancy question within one year. The Prop 19 exclusion requires the transferee to occupy and to file for the homeowners' exemption within a year (Revenue & Taxation Code §63.2); the claim itself can be filed within three years (Proposition 19). Miss it and the tax bill roughly doubles.
  4. Only then choose between occupy, rent and sell, using the live numbers on the decision page.
