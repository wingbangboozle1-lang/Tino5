Reinvestment and crash-entry risk

If the proceeds earn the 3-month bill rate, selling is worth $1,256,580 less than living in. A 1929-size fall with a lump-sum purchase at the peak leaves a low of $675,330 on $6,139,360. Parking 24 to 36 months and buying in over 12 months avoids buying at the peak.

Law points to confirm

  1. Date of death is the change in ownership for trust property; the reassessment and exclusion clocks start then. [S7]
  2. Parent-child exclusion: heir must make the home the principal residence within one year of the change in ownership; claim on BOE-19-P. [S7]
  3. BOE-502-D within 150 days after death, trustee filing for trust property. [S7]
  4. BOE-100-B within 90 days if an entity holds any interest. [S7, S8]
  5. Federal filing threshold for a 2026 death: $15,000,000; Form 706 due date to confirm. [S5, Flag F04]

Questions for the attorney

  1. Is the house held in the trust directly, or through an entity? (Changes BOE-100-B.)
  2. Does the trust give the heir a right to live in the house, and on what date?
  3. Who is the trustee for BOE-502-D and for the 150-day clock?
  4. What is the probate position if the trust does not cover the house?

Questions for the CPA

  1. Basis: what is the house's basis on the date of death, and how is it documented?
  2. Is Form 706 needed? If the estate is near $15,000,000 in 2026, what counts toward it?
  3. Income tax on the sale, including the rate that applies to the heir's gain above the basis.
  4. Whether a rental year changes the answer through depreciation or the loss of the exclusion.
