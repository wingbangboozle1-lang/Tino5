Crash-entry replay: phased entry against the 1929 and 2008 drawdowns

The question: does parking the sale proceeds for 24 to 36 months, then buying in over a year, soften the damage if the market falls just after the sale? What the replay shows, and what it does not

  • Lump sum at the peak carries the whole drop. In 1929 the lowest value is $675,330, against $6,139,360 invested. The phased strategies never buy at the peak.
  • Parking for 24 or 36 months keeps the money in Treasury bills while the fall happens. A 36-month park is still in cash through the 1929 trough, so its lowest value is the amount parked ($6,139,360). A 24-month park starts buying at month 24, while the 1929 fall is still under way, so its lowest value is $4,151,610. Its risk is the rate on the parked money and the price paid during the buying period.
  • A phased entry that buys into a fall can beat no-crash. This is a result of buying at low prices if the index later recovers. It is not a forecast, and it reverses if prices stay low. The replay assumes a recovery, and the recovery shapes are straight lines, which the real markets did not follow.
  • Reinvestment risk. The cash rate used for parking is 4.32%, the 3-month Treasury bill on 13 Nov 2026. If bill rates fall, the parked money earns less.
  • The replay is not a forecast of 2040 to 2050 markets. It answers one question: how big is entry risk, measured against two historical drawdowns. The hypothetical constant-decline calculator from another desk is on cbe / phased investing, and is not a historical replay.
