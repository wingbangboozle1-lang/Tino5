## Key interactions for a trust inheritance

* A revocable living trust does not remove assets from the gross estate. Form 706 is filed on the
  **estate**, not on the beneficiary's share, and is due nine months after death. See [IRS_I706](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706)
  and [IRS_ESTATE_FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/frequently-asked-questions-on-estate-taxes).
* The beneficiary generally has no income on receipt, but the basis of the real property becomes
  its fair market value at death. A sale at that value realises essentially no gain. See
  [IRS_P525](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525) and [IRS_P551](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551).
* Reinvesting sale proceeds in stocks cannot use §1031: like-kind exchange treatment applies only
  to real property held for investment or business use. See [IRS_FS1031](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-news/fs-08-18.pdf).
* If the property is held, later gain is taxed at up to 20% federal plus 3.8% NIIT, and up to
  13.3% California (which taxes capital gains as ordinary income). §121 can exclude $250,000
  ($500,000 joint) after two years of ownership and use. See [IRS_RP2532](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf),
  [IRS_TC559](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc559), [FTB_FEDCHANGES](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/about-ftb/data-reports-plans/summary-of-federal-income-tax-changes/index.html) and [IRS_TC701](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc701).
* Public Law 119-21 §70106 made the $15M basic exclusion permanent and inflation-indexed after
  2026. Congress can still amend it. See [CONGRESS_HR1](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1/text) and [CONGRESS_HR1_SUM](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1).
