Local rules that bind

Four sets of local rules apply to these two buildings, and three of them are capable of changing an outcome by more than the difference between two paths. Each is stated with what is known, what is not, and the document that would settle it.

1. San Francisco rent ordinance (Property B)

What the rent ordinance does to this building, and what is unverified.
Question What is known What is not Status
Is the building covered? A three-unit building is within the ordinance's coverage threshold in general terms. Whether any unit is exempt, for example a substantially rehabilitated building or a unit with a recent certificate of occupancy. derived
How much can rent rise each year? Annual increases are capped by a formula based on a local price index, and notice must be given in writing. The current allowable percentage, and whether any banked increases exist for either unit. derived
Can the owner move into a let unit? Owner move-in eviction is permitted under defined conditions, with notice and possible relocation payments. Whether the third unit is vacant, which would make the question moot. derived
Does moving in change the assessed value? No. Occupancy by the owner is not a change of ownership. Nothing. derived

No value on this desk depends on a rent-control figure, because the model grows rent at general inflation. That is a simplification and it is optimistic: if the cap binds, B's cash flow is lower than modelled. The ordinance text was not fetched for this session; it is the first item on the gap register for local rules.

4. Ballot measures that touch either building

The November 2026 measures that bear on this decision, with their outcome.
Measure What it would do Outcome Effect here Status
H — Parcel Tax to Fund Muni Operations A per-parcel tax on property in San Francisco, scaled by type and size, from fiscal 2027-28 to 2041-42, indexed annually. San Francisco Department of Elections Passed, 74.40% on preliminary results. San Francisco Department of Elections About $250 a year on Property B. Immaterial, but modelled.
I — Changes to the Real Property Transfer Tax Would have changed the transfer tax structure. Failed, 51.66% against. San Francisco Department of Elections Nothing. The existing tiered rates stay in force.
J — Removal of the foreclosure exemption Would have removed an exemption from the transfer tax for foreclosures. Passed, 80.88%. San Francisco Department of Elections Nothing on a voluntary sale.
A cliff, not a slope. San Francisco's transfer tax is charged on the full price at a single tier, not progressively. A sale of Property B at more than $5,000,000 would pay 2.25% on the entire price, not 0.75% on the first $5,000,000. That threshold effect makes selling B far more expensive than a marginal-rate reading would suggest, and it is one more reason the sell-B path ranks last.
