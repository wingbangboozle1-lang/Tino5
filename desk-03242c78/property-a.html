The rate that actually applies is not published anywhere

California property tax is one percent of assessed value plus whatever the voters in that tax rate area have approved. The base one percent never changes. Everything else — county bonds, school bonds, city and district measures — varies by tax rate area, and a county has hundreds of them. The county's own booklet states the composition of a typical area and warns that it is typical, not universal. Santa Clara County Office of the Assessor

The rent assumption carries the letting case. The rent figure is the weakest number on this page. At the low end of the band the house yields 2.32% gross; at the high end, 3.48%. Both are below the two-year Treasury yield of 4.88% observed on 13 November 2026, before taxes, insurance, water and maintenance. A rental that yields less than a government bond, before expenses, is a bet on price growth, not an income asset. That is the whole case against letting it out.

The valuation that will actually matter

The number this desk uses, $3,100,000, is the household's rough anchor for November 2026. The number that will govern the tax outcome is different: the fair market value on the date of death, which becomes the income-tax basis automatically and the estate-tax value for the federal return. If the transfer happens in 2045 at the assumed growth rate, that figure is $5,959,750. See date-of-death valuation for how to establish it defensibly.
