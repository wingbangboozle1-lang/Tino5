Measure H: a new parcel tax on Property B

San Francisco voters approved a parcel tax to fund Muni operations on 3 November 2026. It is not a property tax on value; it is a flat charge per parcel, scaled by property type and building size, and it is indexed every year. Property B is a multifamily residential parcel and will pay it from fiscal year 2027-28.

Status
Passed on preliminary results; the operative schedule is in the ordinance.

Yes 244,760 (74.40%), No 84,209 (25.60%), needing a simple majority. The results page was headed preliminary when read, with a further count scheduled for 17 November 2026. The figures below are quoted from the ordinance text, which does not change with the count.

What the ordinance says

The operative provisions, taken from the 'Stronger Muni For All Ordinance' text. Quoted values are exact statutory amounts.
Provision What it says Effect on Property B Status
Effective date The tax takes effect 1 July 2027 for fiscal year 2027-28 and continues through 30 June 2042, then expires by operation of law. Nine years of the twenty-year projection, then nothing. derived
Multifamily residential rate $249 if building area is 5,000 sq ft or less; plus $0.195 per sq ft above 5,000; maximum $50,000. A three-unit building of ordinary size is at the $249 base: about $21 a month. derived
Single-family rate (for comparison) $129 if building area is 3,000 sq ft or less; plus $0.42 per sq ft above 3,000 to 5,000; then $1.99 per sq ft above 5,000. Not applicable to B. Would apply to A only if A were in San Francisco, which it is not. derived
Indexation From fiscal year 2028-29 every dollar amount is adjusted annually by the Bureau of Labor Statistics consumer price index for the local metropolitan area as of 31 December of the preceding year, rounded down to the nearest dollar. The charge rises with local inflation, not with the 2 percent property tax cap. derived
Senior exemption A parcel where an individual aged 65 or over before 1 July owns a beneficial interest and occupies a dwelling unit as principal residence is exempt if single-family; a multifamily parcel is reduced by $249 for each such unit. If the household moves into B's third unit after turning 65, B's tax falls by $249 — to zero at the base rate. derived
Exemption for non-taxable parcels Parcels on which no ad valorem property tax is levied are exempt. Not applicable; B pays ad valorem tax. derived
Definition of building area Excludes garages, porches and decks; includes accessory dwelling unit square footage. B is almost certainly at the flat rate regardless of how the area is measured. derived

All provisions quoted from the ordinance text. San Francisco Department of Elections Election result: San Francisco Department of Elections

Small in dollars, uncertain in detail. The rates above are quoted exactly from the ordinance, but the measure's own fiscal analysis and the city's implementation guidance were not read, and the results were preliminary. The desk therefore models only the base $249 and does not model the inflation index, the senior exemption, or any litigation outcome. The amount at stake for Property B is small — about $2,240 over nine years at the base rate — so the uncertainty does not move the recommendation.

How it enters the model

The model adds $249 a year to Property B's tax for every year from the transfer through fiscal year 2041-42, growing with the general inflation assumption, and adds nothing after that. It does not apply the senior exemption because the household's ages at the transfer date are not in the file. If the household is over 65 and occupies the third unit, the line goes to zero. That is one of the what-if switches on the workbook.

What it means for the decision

Nothing. Nine years of about $250 is a rounding error against a $1,800,000 asset. It is documented here because the desk's rule is that every tax that hits either property gets its own line and its own source, not because it changes the answer. Documenting small items is what makes the larger ones credible.
