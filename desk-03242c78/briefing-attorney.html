Attorney and CPA briefing sheet: title, trust and deadlines

One page: what must be true for the recommended path, and the dates that make it true or false.

Prepared 15 November 2026. A planning document prepared for review, not advice. No trust instrument, deed, or title report was read in preparing it. Owner-supplied values are unverified.

The three legal conditions that carry money

Each condition, its deadline, and the consequence of failure.
Condition Deadline Consequence of failure Source Status
Occupy Property A as principal residence within 1 year of the transfer The Proposition 19 exclusion is lost; the house is reassessed at market value, costing about $0 a year California Legislature
File the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption within 1 year of the transfer The exclusion is conditioned on the claim; without it the benefit does not exist California Legislature
Continue to occupy; do not let the property out every year until sale Reassessment as of the date eligibility ends, with a supplemental bill California Legislature
Claim the exclusion within 3 years, or before transfer to a third party A backstop only; the occupancy facts must exist regardless California Legislature
File a timely Form 706 if portability is wanted within 9 months of death The deceased spouse's unused exclusion is wasted Internal Revenue Service

Title and transfer questions for counsel

  1. How does the trust hold Property A, and what does the instrument direct on the second death — outright distribution, continuing trust, or a power of appointment?
  2. If the property continues in trust rather than distributing outright, does the transferee meet the Proposition 19 definition of an eligible transferee, and who occupies it?
  3. Is a distribution out of the trust to the beneficiary a transfer for property tax purposes, and when does it take effect?
  4. Does the trust direct a sale, or does it give the trustee discretion that conflicts with the heir's plans?
  5. Is there a mortgage, reverse mortgage, or lien that survives the transfer?
  6. Does the deed into the trust describe the property in a way that would affect the tax rate area or the parcel's assessment?
  7. What probate or administration filing does this estate actually require, given that Property A alone exceeds the small-estate threshold of $166,250?
  8. Are there any claims, creditor issues, or litigation that would make a sale necessary regardless of the financial analysis?

What the desk recommends, in one line

Sell Property A at the transfer date; do not sell Property B; decide on occupancy only if the household genuinely intends to live in the house for years, because the exclusion is conditional and revocable. The financial margin is small enough that a non-financial reason to keep the house is a legitimate basis for deciding differently.

This is a planning document, not advice. It is prepared for review by a licensed California attorney and a CPA before any step is taken. No legal, tax or investment advice is given or implied. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly and marked; every other figure is an assumption, an owner-supplied anchor, or arithmetic on those.

Nothing here identifies a person, a street address, an assessor's parcel number, or a ZIP code. Owner-supplied figures are approximate and unverified. Money is rounded to the nearest $10 except where a statute or official table states an exact amount.
