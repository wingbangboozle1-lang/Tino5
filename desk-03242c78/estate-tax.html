Estate tax, and whether this estate is liquid enough

Three separate taxes are often confused in this discussion. Only one of them is an estate tax, and on the figures available it is unlikely to apply. The other two — income tax on gain and property tax on assessed value — apply either way and are far larger. This page deals with the first; the other two have their own pages.

Bottom line
No federal estate tax is expected, and California has none.

The federal exclusion is $15,000,000 for deaths in 2026 and $15,480,000 for deaths in 2027. A $3,100,000 house, even after two decades of growth, sits far below either figure unless growth is extreme and the rest of the estate is large. California imposes no estate or inheritance tax: the Revenue and Taxation Code has prohibited one since 1982, and no statute on the books today imposes one. The real risk is not the tax, it is a filing obligation nobody notices, and a portability election nobody makes.

Two things that could still bite

Portability is not automatic

When the first spouse dies, the unused exclusion does not pass to the survivor by itself. It passes only if the executor files a timely federal estate tax return electing portability — even where no tax is due. Internal Revenue Service Internal Revenue Service A return that is never filed because 'the estate was under the threshold' can permanently waste an exclusion equal to the whole threshold. For a married household this is the single most valuable piece of paperwork in the file.

Prior taxable gifts change the arithmetic

The exclusion is a unified amount: lifetime taxable gifts use it up, and the estate gets only what is left. A household that made large gifts decades ago may have far less exclusion left than the headline figure suggests. The desk cannot compute this without the gift tax returns, which are not in the file. It is question 4 on questions and gaps.

What happens if the estate exceeds the exclusion

At 40 percent, an estate over the threshold needs cash. A house is not cash. The estate has nine months to file and, absent an election, to pay. There are three tools, and each has a cost:

Estate liquidity tools, with their conditions and why each is a last resort.
Tool What it does Conditions Why it is not free Status
Section 6166 installment election Defers the estate tax attributable to a closely held business over up to 14 years, at a favourable rate on the first portion. The business interest must exceed 35% of the adjusted gross estate; the decedent must have been a US citizen or resident; a 2% portion is capped. Interest accrues, a bond or lien may be required, and the estate must stay compliant for up to 14 years. derived
Special-use valuation, section 2032A Values qualified real property at its actual use rather than its highest and best use, to a statutory ceiling. Property must pass to a qualified heir, be used in farming or a trade or business, and meet the 5-of-8-year use and material-participation tests. The ceiling is $1,460,000 for 2026, and the deferred tax is recaptured if the heir stops qualifying. derived
Sale of estate assets Raises cash directly. Needs a buyer, an appraisal, and time. A forced sale of a house under deadline pressure usually realises less than a planned sale, and the proceeds may themselves be taxable to the estate. derived

The filing that people forget

Separately from the estate tax return, the executor must give the IRS and each beneficiary a statement of the property's value — a consistent-basis report — due within 30 days of the estate tax return's due date, or of its filing if earlier. The beneficiaries need those figures to compute their own gain. Without them, the heir's basis becomes an argument. Internal Revenue Service
