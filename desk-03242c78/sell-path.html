Sell Property A and reinvest

Sell at the transfer date, pay the costs, and put the proceeds to work. This is the path that ranks first on the central assumptions and the one that carries the most reinvestment risk, because it exchanges a building for a price series.

The negotiable number. The 6 percent selling cost is an assumption, and it is large: $357,580 on this value. If commission falls to 5 percent the path gains about $59,600; at 7 percent it loses about the same. This is one of the few inputs the household can actually negotiate, and it is worth more attention than most of the tax questions on this desk.

What happens to the money afterwards

Selling is not a decision about a house; it is a decision to hold a portfolio instead. The model invests the net proceeds at the assumed equity return and adds the rental income from B. That is a simplification: a real portfolio would be diversified, would hold bonds, and would be drawn on for living expenses.

The four ways this path fails

  • Bad timing at entry. Selling A puts a large lump sum into the market on a single date. The 1929 and 2007 records both show what that can mean. See phased investing for the mitigation and its cost.
  • Poor real returns. Below about 4.5% nominal the house wins outright, and there is no way to know in advance which regime is coming.
  • Spending the proceeds. A liquid portfolio is spendable; a house is not. This path requires a written policy, which is here.
  • Tax drag on the portfolio. The model applies a gross return. A taxable portfolio of this size loses part of its return to federal and state tax on dividends and realised gains every year, which the model does not deduct.
The fourth point matters. A taxable account returning 6.5 percent gross might return 5.7 percent after tax in a high bracket. Over twenty years that difference is larger than the gap between the best and second-best paths. It is listed as a gap on questions and gaps rather than modelled, because the household's bracket and location at the time are unknown.
