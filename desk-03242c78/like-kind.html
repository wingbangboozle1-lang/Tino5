Like-kind exchange: the option that needs a decision, not a calculation

A section 1031 exchange would let the household sell Property A and roll the proceeds into other rental property without paying tax on the gain. For an inherited house sold soon after death, the gain is close to zero, which means an exchange would defer almost nothing while imposing real constraints. For a house held and rented for years, the analysis is different.

The desk's position
Do not exchange a freshly inherited house. The step-up has already done the work.

An exchange defers gain that does not exist yet. The cost is that the replacement property must be identified within 45 days, acquired within 180 days, and the taxpayer must hold it for investment or business use — which forecloses living in it. Applying that machinery to a house sold at a near-zero gain buys nothing.

The rules, and their cost

What a section 1031 exchange requires, and why each requirement matters here.
Requirement What it means Why it is a cost in this case Status
Qualified property Real property held for productive use in a trade or business, or for investment. A personal residence does not qualify. A house the household lives in cannot be exchanged. The household would have to hold it as a rental, which forfeits the Proposition 19 exclusion. derived
Identification period 45 days after the sale to identify replacement property in writing. A household grieving and administrating an estate has 45 days to commit to a replacement property. That is a deadline, not a plan. derived
Exchange period 180 days after the sale to close. Both periods run from the sale date, not from when the household feels ready. derived
Basis carryover The replacement property takes the old basis, reduced by any boot received. Internal Revenue Service The deferral is permanent only if the property is held until death, when a step-up wipes it out. That is a bet on holding for decades.
No personal use The replacement property must be held for investment or business use. The household cannot live in it, cannot easily use it as a second home, and must have a genuine rental or business purpose. derived
Section 121 interaction Gain is not excludable under section 121 if the home was acquired in a like-kind exchange within the past five years. Internal Revenue Service An exchange forecloses the live-then-sell option, and with it the exclusion.
Deferral is not saving. An exchange is not a tax-free sale; it is a postponement with conditions. The conditions are strict, the deadlines are short, and the replacement property carries the old basis forward. A household that exchanges into a building it does not want, to meet a 45-day deadline, has solved a tax problem by creating a worse investment problem.

When it would be worth considering

Only in one configuration: the household has decided to rent Property A out for a substantial period, has accumulated real gain beyond the section 121 exclusion, and wants to move the equity into other rental property rather than into securities. That is a coherent strategy. It is not the strategy this desk recommends, and it starts from a path that already ranks last among the three pure choices.

This is a decision for the attorney and the CPA together, on the specific facts. The desk does not model an exchange, because modelling it would require assuming the terms of a replacement property that does not exist. It is listed on suggestions for review as an open question, not as a recommendation.
