Live in Property A

Move in, keep the assessed value, and let all three units at B. This is the only path that captures the Proposition 19 exclusion, and it is also the only path where the household's own housing is the asset under discussion.

The risk in this path. This path puts most of the household's net worth into one building in one county, at an assessed value that only stays low while the house stays occupied. It is concentrated, illiquid, and conditional. The financial case for it is a bet on California house prices growing faster than 6.1% a year. If they do not, selling wins.

Why it wins in bad markets and loses in good ones

Living in A converts the household's largest future expense — housing — into something it already owns. When financial assets do badly, that is worth more than any portfolio. When they do well, the house cannot keep up: it is one asset, undiversified, with carrying costs and a cap on how fast its tax basis can rise. The model reflects exactly that.

Mixed versions of living there

Living in A does not have to be permanent. The model also runs two variants:

Living in A and then selling, against living in A for the whole horizon.
Path Years lived in Section 121 used Net worth incl. shelter Difference from living throughout Status
Live in A throughout 20 $0 $22,963,440 $0 derived
Live 5 years, then sell 5 $250,000 $26,901,860 $3,938,420 derived
Live 10 years, then sell 10 $250,000 $24,016,370 $1,052,930 derived

All three satisfy the Proposition 19 occupancy condition, and the two that sell also satisfy the section 121 use test. A household that intends to stay five to ten years gets both benefits, which is why the mixed paths score above either extreme.
