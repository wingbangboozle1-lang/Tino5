Glossary

Every term used on the desk, in plain language, in alphabetical order.

Ad valorem tax
A tax on value, as opposed to a flat charge per parcel. California's one percent general levy is ad valorem; Measure H is not.
Assessed value
The value the county uses to compute property tax. Not market value, and usually far below it for a long-held property.
Basis
The amount against which gain is measured. For inherited property it is the value on the date of death, automatically.
Boot
Cash or other property received in an exchange that is not like-kind. It is taxable even where the exchange itself is deferred.
Consistent-basis report
The statement an executor must give the IRS and each beneficiary, on Form 8971, stating the value used for basis.
Crossovers
The value of one assumption at which the ranking of two paths reverses. More useful than a point estimate because it states the condition.
Documentary transfer tax
A tax on the recorded transfer of real property, set by statute for counties and often matched by cities.
Factored base year value
The assessed value under Proposition 13, adjusted each year by the lesser of the consumer price index and two percent.
Fair market rent
A published rent benchmark, the 40th percentile of gross rents for standard-quality units, used for federal voucher programs. Not an appraisal of a particular dwelling.
Form 706
The federal estate tax return. Also the only vehicle for electing portability of a deceased spouse's unused exclusion.
Form 8971
The form on which consistent-basis reports are made.
Imputed rent
The rental value of a home the owner occupies. Not taxed and not deductible, which is why comparing it on both sides of a decision cancels out.
Like-kind exchange
An exchange of business or investment real property for other real property, on which gain is deferred rather than eliminated.
Net investment income tax
A 3.8 percent federal surtax on investment income above an income threshold.
Net worth including shelter
The ranking measure used throughout: financial assets plus property held plus the market rent of housing consumed over the horizon.
Parcel tax
A flat charge per parcel rather than a percentage of value. Measure H is one.
Portability
The ability of a surviving spouse to use a deceased spouse's unused estate tax exclusion, available only by a timely election on Form 706.
Proposition 13
The 1978 initiative that set the one percent general levy, the two percent assessment cap, and the reassessment-on-change-of-ownership rule.
Proposition 19
The 2020 initiative that replaced the parent-child exclusion with a narrower one: for a family home, an exclusion worth the applicable amount, conditional on the child occupying it.
Recapture
The part of a gain that represents depreciation previously deducted, taxed at up to 25 percent rather than the capital gain rate.
Regime
A named set of market assumptions anchored on a published record, used to test a decision against a plausible past rather than an average.
Section 1031
The like-kind exchange provision.
Section 121
The income tax provision excluding up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 joint) on the sale of a principal residence.
Step-up in basis
The reset of an asset's basis to its value at the owner's death. The single most valuable rule in an inheritance.
Tax rate area
A geographic unit within a county carrying its own combination of local levies. Explains why two properties on the same street can pay different rates.

This is a planning document, not advice. It is prepared for review by a licensed California attorney and a CPA before any step is taken. No legal, tax or investment advice is given or implied. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly and marked; every other figure is an assumption, an owner-supplied anchor, or arithmetic on those.

Nothing here identifies a person, a street address, an assessor's parcel number, or a ZIP code. Owner-supplied figures are approximate and unverified. Money is rounded to the nearest $10 except where a statute or official table states an exact amount.
