Black swans and extreme paths

A twenty-year projection built on average growth is a comfortable lie. The record is not a smooth trend; it is long stretches of ordinary returns punctuated by crashes, inflation spikes and decades of nothing. This page runs the comparison through five such paths and reports what survives.

What survives every regime
Two findings hold in all five regimes tested.

First, letting Property A out never beats living in it, at any transfer date, in any regime. Second, selling Property B is always among the worst two choices. Everything else — whether to keep A, whether to park the proceeds — depends on which world arrives.

The deflation regime produces outcomes an order of magnitude smaller than the baseline. That is what a repeat of 1929 to 1933 does to every asset at once, and it is a reminder that the choice between paths is a small question next to the choice of how much to hold in any asset at all.

What is extreme but not modelled

  • A California earthquake that damages one or both buildings. Insurance covers some of it; the deductible, the loss of rent and the rebuilding period do not appear in any projection here.
  • A change to Proposition 13 or Proposition 19. Both are constitutional and both have been amended before. A repeal of the parent-child exclusion would remove the entire occupancy benefit and make selling more attractive still.
  • A federal wealth tax or a change to the step-up in basis. The step-up is statutory, not constitutional, and has been proposed for repeal repeatedly. Its removal would change the arithmetic of every path on this desk.
  • A long period of rent control tightening. Would hurt the letting paths, which already lose.
  • Extended long-term care for either spouse. The largest unmodelled risk in most households of this age, and the one most likely to force a sale at the worst time.
Every one of these is reviewed annually as part of the annual re-run. None of them is priced, because pricing a scenario that has no probability is worse than naming it.
