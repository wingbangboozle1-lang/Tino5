Deadline timeline

The dates below are the ones that carry a penalty, a lost benefit or an irreversible consequence. They are in the order they occur, counted from the date of death, because that is the event the household will actually be reacting to.

  • Day 0
    Date of death. Basis of every asset is fixed at this moment. Order the appraisal of Property A immediately; a value established months later is a reconstruction, not a valuation. official Internal Revenue Service
    Executor or trustee
  • Within 40 days
    The small-estate affidavit becomes available, but only if the entire California estate is worth $166,250 or less. Property A alone exceeds that, so formal administration will be needed. official California Legislature
    Executor
  • Months 1-3
    Obtain an employer identification number for the estate; marshal the assets; notify insurers; secure both buildings; change the locks if the house is vacant. Internal Revenue Service
    Executor
  • Within 9 months
    Federal estate tax return due, if one is required — and the portability election can only be made on that return. Filing late can forfeit an exclusion worth more than either building. official Internal Revenue Service Internal Revenue Service
    Executor and CPA
  • Within 1 year of transfer
    The Proposition 19 clock. The heir must occupy Property A as a principal residence and file for the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption within one year. Miss it and the house is reassessed at market value, costing about $0 a year. official California Legislature
    Heir and attorney
  • By 15 February, each year
    Homeowners' exemption claim for that assessment year. Late claims may be accepted to 10 December with a penalty. official Santa Clara County Office of the Assessor
    Heir
  • Within 30 days of the estate return
    Consistent-basis reports to the IRS and to each beneficiary, on Form 8971. The heir needs these figures to compute gain later. official Internal Revenue Service
    Executor
  • By 15 April, year after death
    The estate's and the heir's income tax returns. A sale of Property A in the year of death is reported here. Internal Revenue Service
    CPA
  • 2 July to 15 September, each year
    The window to appeal an assessed value in Santa Clara County. Free, no attorney needed, and worth doing if the value looks high. official Santa Clara County Office of the Assessor
    Heir
  • Within 3 years of transfer
    The outer limit for a Proposition 19 claim, or before the property is transferred to a third party, whichever is earlier. This is a backstop, not a plan. official California Legislature
    Heir and attorney
  • Any time after the transfer
    If the decision is to sell: sell. Section 121 requires two of the last five years of ownership and use, and the exclusion is capped at $250,000 for a single filer. official Internal Revenue Service
    Heir
  • Annually, from then on
    Re-run the model, check the recommendation against the inputs, and record the date of the decision. Annual re-run.
    Household

The three dates that actually cost money

Of the twelve dates above, three carry a consequence large enough to change the recommendation if missed.
Date Cost of missing it Amount Reversible? Status
Within 1 year: exemption claim Property A reassessed at market value; the Proposition 19 exclusion is lost, and with it the annual saving. $90 No. The claim window can be extended, but the exclusion depends on facts, not on a filing. derived
Within 9 months: estate return Portability lost. The surviving spouse's exclusion is not increased by the deceased spouse's unused amount. $15,000,000 of exclusion at stake Only with a late election, which the IRS may or may not grant. derived
Within 30 days: basis reports The heir cannot substantiate basis; the gain on a later sale becomes an argument with the IRS. unquantified Partially, with appraisals and records. derived

This is a planning document, not advice. It is prepared for review by a licensed California attorney and a CPA before any step is taken. No legal, tax or investment advice is given or implied. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly and marked; every other figure is an assumption, an owner-supplied anchor, or arithmetic on those.

Nothing here identifies a person, a street address, an assessor's parcel number, or a ZIP code. Owner-supplied figures are approximate and unverified. Money is rounded to the nearest $10 except where a statute or official table states an exact amount.
