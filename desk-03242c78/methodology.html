Method, rounding and reproducibility

The desk is a deterministic model plus a static site generator. Both are in the repository. Given the same inputs, it produces the same pages, and anyone can check the arithmetic rather than take it on trust.

How the model works

  1. One household, one dwelling. Every path houses the household in exactly one place. Shelter is valued at market rent and reported separately, so no path receives free housing.
  2. Monthly steps. Cash flow is annual but portfolio growth is monthly, because the parking paths switch assets mid-year and a monthly step needs no day-count convention.
  3. Same dates, same horizon. Every path is measured from the same transfer date to the same end date. Nothing is annualised differently between paths.
  4. Taxes computed per path. Each path applies the rules that actually apply to it: step-up in basis, section 121 only where the use test is met, depreciation and recapture only where the property is let, Proposition 19 only where the household occupies.
  5. Regimes, then a grid. Five named regimes anchored on published records, plus a five-by-five grid of house growth and equity return for the distribution.
  6. Crossovers, not just outcomes. Where the ranking turns on an assumption, the desk solves for the value at which it turns, and publishes that instead of a point estimate.

Rounding

The rounding rules, and why they exist.
Rule What it means Why Status
Money to the nearest $10 Every dollar figure on every page, except the statutory literals listed below. Privacy. A figure precise to the dollar can be matched to a public record. This is the household's own instruction and it is applied without exception. derived
Statutory amounts quoted exactly Thresholds, ceilings, rates and published tables are printed as published and marked exact. Rounding a legal threshold misstates it: $1,044,586 rounded to $1,044,590 is a different number.
Percentages to one or two decimals Rates are shown to enough precision to be checkable, not to imply precision. A rate of 1.34% is a rate; 1.3400% claims four digits of accuracy that do not exist. derived
Years as whole numbers No fractional years anywhere. The underlying events happen on dates, not on fractions. derived

Privacy rules applied at build time

Every rule the build enforces, and how.
Rule Enforcement Status
No personal names, addresses, parcel numbers or ZIP codes The build checks the generated HTML against a pattern list before writing it. derived
No city or neighbourhood names beyond three county-level identifiers Only 'California', 'Santa Clara County' and 'San Francisco County' appear; the build rejects a longer locality list. derived
No figures precise enough to match a public record Assessed values are rounded to $10,000 and $5,000 before they enter the model; money is formatted through a single rounding function. derived
No external requests No JavaScript, no webfonts, no images, no analytics, no third-party stylesheets. The pages are static HTML and one local CSS file. derived
No owner figures in the repository's history Owner figures are rounded before they are stored; the household's year-by-year tax history is deliberately not in the repository at all, only the rate band it implies. derived

How to reproduce it

python3 desk-03242c78/_src/model.py
python3 desk-03242c78/_src/build.py
python3 desk-03242c78/_src/verify.py

The first writes the model output, the second writes the pages from it, and the third checks every table row for a source link or a status tag, every internal link, and every rounding rule. The build fails rather than publishing a page that breaks a rule.

This is a planning document, not advice. It is prepared for review by a licensed California attorney and a CPA before any step is taken. No legal, tax or investment advice is given or implied. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly and marked; every other figure is an assumption, an owner-supplied anchor, or arithmetic on those.

Nothing here identifies a person, a street address, an assessor's parcel number, or a ZIP code. Owner-supplied figures are approximate and unverified. Money is rounded to the nearest $10 except where a statute or official table states an exact amount.
