Property B: the building already owned

A three-unit building in San Francisco County. It is not inherited and it is not part of the decision about A. It is included because it changes the household's cash flow, its housing and its tax position, and because any plan that ignores it would be wrong.

Its tax bill is not the published rate applied to its value

The city publishes a secured property tax rate of 1.18162% for fiscal year 2026-27. San Francisco Office of the Treasurer & Tax Collector Applying that rate to the assessed value gives $1,360. The household's 2025 bill was about $2,440. The difference, roughly $1,080 a year, is parcel taxes, bond measures and direct assessments that sit outside the ad valorem rate. The model carries that difference as a separate line so the bill is reproduced, and labels it for what it is: arithmetic on two owner figures, not a verified breakdown.

Why the rent matters more than the value

Two units at about $7,150 a month pay the building's own costs and then some. The third unit is the household's housing, or its rent-free option. That is an unusual position: the household can house itself and still collect rent from the same building. Any plan that sells B trades that for cash, and the cash is then taxed, invested and spent.
