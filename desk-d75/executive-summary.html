The situation

A house is held in a family trust and is expected to pass to the heir between 2040 and 2050. Its market value is about $3,100,000 and its assessed value is about $550,540, so the tax charged on it is roughly $7,210 a year where a buyer at today's price would pay about $36,700. That difference is the largest single thing in this analysis, and it is conditional: California's Proposition 19 lets the base pass to a child only if the child makes the house a principal residence within a year, claims the homeowners' exemption within a year, and keeps qualifying. If the heir later stops qualifying, the assessor enrols the value as if the exclusion had never applied - the relief is a lease, not a deed. The heir already owns another house outright, a three-unit building letting two units with a third available; it is not part of the inheritance, and it is the alternative place to live, which is exactly why "sell" is not a choice made in isolation. On the central assumptions the ranking at a 2045 transfer is Sell it at transfer, reinvest the money > Rent it out, live at the other house > Live in it ten years, then sell > Live in it, keep it.

Wealth at 2076 in nominal dollars, after tax, attributable to the inherited house, on the baseline assumptions (house values 4.0% a year, reinvestment 3.9% a year after tax, costs at the rates on the methodology page). The already-owned house is held in every path, so it does not appear in these numbers except through its vacant unit: living in the inherited house means that unit is let and not living there means the heir lives in it, which is worth $3,706,130 by 2076 at a 2045 transfer and is credited inside the occupy rows.

What would change the answer. Living in the house and never selling catches up with selling if house values compound at about 5.4% a year instead of 4.0%, or if the after-tax reinvestment return falls to about 2.6% instead of 3.9%. Those two numbers decide the ranking, not the carrying costs: every cost line moves the answer by hundreds of thousands, against a $7,259,780 spread between the best and worst paths.

What is still unknown, and how much it matters

19 of the 28 questions on the questions page are open. Two of them can move a number by more than a factor: what the two tax bills actually contain, and what the cash side of the estate is. The rest change confidence rather than direction.
