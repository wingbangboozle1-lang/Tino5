Which assumptions can overturn the model's ranking of occupy, rent and sell?

Ratios of after-tax wealth at the end of the holding period, from the reference engine run on the desk's own assumptions. A ratio above 1.00 means the first path ends ahead. Dollar levels are deliberately not shown.

Conditional, not a recommendation. The ordering is a property of the model and of inputs that are not yet verified from official sources (gross rent, tax-rate area, insurance, transfer date). Treat it as a hypothesis the stress tests below try to break.

What living in A must be worth

If the heir lives in A, the model charges all holding costs and gives no credit for housing. The table converts the wealth gap into the yearly value of living there, as a share of A's market value in the first year (growing with inflation afterwards), and sets it beside the model's own gross rent yield.

Reading: living in A beats selling only if it is worth about 2.7% of the home's value each year, roughly 1.5 times the model's gross rent yield. It beats renting only if worth about 1.3% a year. Both depend on the unverified rent input.
