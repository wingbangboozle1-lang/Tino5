The main dashboard tests a few scenarios that each hold one growth rate for decades. Real economies do not do that. This section takes what actually happened to prices, house values, rents, interest rates and stocks in each year from 1977 to 2025 and replays those years, in order, over the next half century.

What this is not. It is not a forecast, and it does not say what Property A or Property B is worth. Nothing here uses a household figure. The replay shows how much the answer to "live in, rent, or sell and reinvest" can move when good and bad years arrive in a different order.

What the replay says about the decision

  • The order of good and bad years moves the result a long way. Every path has the same average growth. By 2045 the home-price multiple ranges from 1.76× (the path that begins with the years from 2007) to 6.64× (the path that begins with 2020). After inflation the range is 1.10× to 2.65×.
  • Sell and buy stocks: ahead on price in 25 of 25 recorded 25-year periods. That is before rent, costs and tax. Keeping the house matched stocks only if it paid net cash, after all costs and taxes, of 1.1% to 7.7% of its value each year (middle 4.2%). Living in the house counts as that cash only to the extent it replaces rent the owner would otherwise pay.
  • Sell and hold savings: behind in 25 of 25. Treasury bills ended ahead of home prices in 0 of 25 periods. The house could have cost its owner 1.3% to 4.0% of its value a year and still matched bills.
  • The replay can only reorder the record. Over these 49 years stocks with dividends returned about 11.5% a year and local home prices rose about 6.7% a year. It cannot show an economy worse than the record itself (flags S02, S11).
  • Renting out the inherited home resets its taxable value to market value. In 21 of 49 paths that new value was later above the market on at least one 1 January. A decline-in-value review applies in those years (flag S06).
  • The Prop 19 amount and the estate exclusion move with different indexes than the house. See the transfer-year tables for how far each can drift.
