How a trust inheritance actually runs

Transfer process

Privacy. No name, street address, parcel or account number appears anywhere on this site. Properties are identified only as Property A (Santa Clara County, to be inherited through a trust) and Property B (San Francisco, already owned). All money is rounded to the nearest $10 so published figures cannot be matched back to a public record. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published because they are statewide constants and identify nothing.

A trust inheritance in California, step by step, with the form and the deadline for each step. This is the sequence, not legal advice.

Before the transfer — while the settlor is alive

  1. Confirm the property is titled in the trust

    A transfer of a home into a revocable trust where the settlor remains the beneficiary is excluded from reassessment, so it does not reset the property tax base. If the deed into the trust was never recorded, the remedy later is a court petition, not a form.

  2. Establish whether Property A is the settlor's principal residence

    The Proposition 19 exclusion requires the home to have been the transferor's principal residence. If the settlor has already moved out, the exclusion may be unavailable.

    California State Board of Equalization California State Board of Equalization

  3. Diary the two clocks

    The homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption is due within one year of the transfer; the BOE-19-P claim within three years of it, or before any transfer to a third party, whichever comes first.

    Clock: 1 year and 3 years from the transfer date

    California State Board of Equalization County of Santa Clara, Office of the Assessor

  4. Decide the occupancy question in principle

    The exclusion requires the home to become the transferee's principal residence within one year and to stay that way. This is a household decision with a tax consequence of tens of thousands of dollars a year, so it should not be made in the grief of the moment.

    California State Board of Equalization

At and immediately after the transfer

  1. The trustee serves the statutory notification

    Within 60 days of the settlor's death the trustee must serve a notification on every beneficiary of the trust and every heir of the deceased settlor. The duty is the continuing or successor trustee's, and any one co-trustee may serve it.

    Clock: 60 days

    California Legislative Information

  2. Determine the date-of-death value

    This single figure does three jobs: it is the new basis for income tax, it is the value reported for estate tax if a return is due, and it is the amount the county will use on reassessment if no exclusion is claimed. Use a qualified appraisal.

    IRS

  3. Work out whether a federal estate tax return is due

    California imposes nothing. Federally, a return is required only if the gross estate increased by adjusted taxable gifts exceeds the filing threshold for the year of death: $15,000,000 for 2026 and $15,480,000 for 2027.

    Clock: 9 months after death, extendable

    IRS IRS California Legislative Information

  4. File the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption

    Required within one year of the transfer to receive the Proposition 19 exclusion as of the date of transfer. A BOE-19-P claim cannot be filed before this exemption claim.

    Clock: 1 year

    California State Board of Equalization

  5. Move in, if the exclusion is being claimed

    The transferee must live in the home as a principal residence within one year and keep living there. If the property later stops being anyone's principal residence it is reassessed to its inherited market value, adjusted by the inflation factor.

    Clock: 1 year, then continuously

    California State Board of Equalization California State Board of Equalization

  6. File BOE-19-P with the Santa Clara County Assessor

    The claim form for the parent-child reassessment exclusion, filed with the assessor of the county where the property sits, not with the Board of Equalization.

    Clock: 3 years

    California State Board of Equalization County of Santa Clara, Office of the Assessor County of Santa Clara, Office of the Assessor

  7. Expect a supplemental or escape assessment

    On a change in ownership the assessor reappraises and issues a supplemental bill for the difference, prorated for the part of the fiscal year remaining. If the new value is determined late, an escape assessment follows instead.

    City and County of San Francisco, Office of the Treasurer & Tax Collector

After the transfer — the ongoing position

  1. Keep occupying, or lose the exclusion

    At least one eligible transferee must continually live in the property for the exclusion to survive. This is a permanent condition, not a one-year test.

    California State Board of Equalization

  2. If the property is let instead

    The exclusion does not apply to a rental transfer. The property is reassessed to full market value, rental income becomes taxable, depreciation begins over 27.5 years, and the statewide rent cap of 5% plus inflation, subject to a 10% ceiling, governs increases.

    California State Board of Equalization California Legislative Information IRS

  3. If the property is sold

    Basis is market value at death, so a sale soon after the transfer realises almost no gain. Hold it for two of the five years before selling and up to $250,000, or $500,000 filing jointly, of gain is excluded; that exclusion also removes the gain from the net investment income tax.

    IRS IRS

  4. Re-run this analysis each year

    The break-even thresholds move with the market, and the law-watch items move with Congress and the California ballot. An annual re-read is the cheapest insurance available.

    Clock: Annually

The two dates that carry money. One year for the homeowners' exemption and for moving in. Three years for the BOE-19-P claim. Both run from the date of transfer, not from the date anyone gets around to it. Late is not fatal — a late exemption claim moves the start of the exclusion forward — but it costs the back years.
