Two properties, one decision. Property A is in Santa Clara County and is expected to pass through a family trust somewhere in the 2040–2050 window. Property B is in San Francisco and is already owned; it has three units, two of them let. The question is whether to live in Property A, let it, or sell it and reinvest.

The finding that drives everything else. California assessment is acquisition-based, so today's bill reflects a base year value set decades ago. The moment ownership changes the property is reassessed. Even with the Proposition 19 parent-child exclusion claimed in full, the bill multiplies; without it, it multiplies again. Every other line in this analysis is an order of magnitude smaller.
