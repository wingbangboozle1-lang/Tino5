What must be known before deciding

Questions &amp; gaps

Privacy. No name, street address, parcel or account number appears anywhere on this site. Properties are identified only as Property A (Santa Clara County, to be inherited through a trust) and Property B (San Francisco, already owned). All money is rounded to the nearest $10 so published figures cannot be matched back to a public record. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published because they are statewide constants and identify nothing.

Everything that has to be known before a decision can be made on the merits, grouped by subject. Each entry says what is known, what is missing, what decision the gap blocks, and precisely how to close it.

Trust and title

Q01 Is Property A already titled in the revocable trust, or will it be transferred into it later?  unknown

Known: A transfer of a home into a revocable trust where the settlor remains the beneficiary is excluded from reassessment, so it does not itself reset the property tax base.

Missing: The current vesting and whether a deed into the trust has been recorded.

How to find it: Read the grant deed and any trust transfer deed; confirm with the trustee. If the deed was never recorded, a petition may be needed to confirm the trust assets.

Q02 Who is the trustee and successor trustee, and is the trust a single or joint revocable trust?  unknown

Known: On the death of a settlor the trustee must serve a notification on every beneficiary and every heir within 60 days under Probate Code section 16061.7.

Missing: Names and the succession chain. Deliberately not recorded on this site.

How to find it: Read the trust instrument's trustee provisions. Keep the answer off any public page.

California Legislative Information

Q03 Is Property A held as community property with a surviving spouse, or as separate property?  unknown

Known: How the property is held changes who must sign, what passes, and what basis adjustment applies at each death.

Missing: Marital property character of the parcel.

How to find it: Check the deed vesting and any pre- or post-marital agreement. Ask the estate attorney.

Q04 Are there other beneficiaries of the trust, and does the trust require the property be sold or allow it to be distributed in kind?  unknown

Known: If other beneficiaries are entitled to a share, distributing a house in kind creates a liquidity problem and may force a sale regardless of the analysis here.

Missing: The distribution provisions and the identity and number of beneficiaries.

How to find it: Read the distribution article of the trust. This single answer can override every financial conclusion on this site.

Timing

Q05 What is the expected date of transfer - 2040, 2045, 2050, or outside that window entirely?  unknown

Known: The owner's stated planning window is 2040-2050. Every projection on this site is shown at 2040, 2045 and 2050 for that reason.

Missing: An actual estimate. Note the transfer date sets the reassessment date and the market value used for the step-up.

How to find it: Discuss with the trustee and the settlor. Revisit this page if the window moves: the taxable value and the bill change materially with each year.

Q06 Will the settlor still be occupying Property A as a principal residence at the transfer?  unknown

Known: The Prop 19 parent-child exclusion requires that the home was the transferor's principal residence. If the settlor has moved out, the exclusion may not be available at all.

Missing: Whether Property A will still be the settlor's principal residence.

How to find it: Confirm with the trustee. If the settlor may move into care or elsewhere, this is the single highest-value question to resolve early.

Q07 What would Property A rent for on the open market, unfurnished, today and at transfer?  unknown

Known: Nothing. No rental comparable has been supplied. The analysis uses a gross yield assumption of 2.6% of market value, shown with a 2.0%-3.2% band.

Missing: The number that decides option 2. Renting only ties selling at a gross yield of about 4.76%.

How to find it: Get three written rental opinions from agents who manage single-family lets in the same Santa Clara County submarket, and re-run the scenarios page with the real figure.

Q08 What is the real home insurance premium for Property A, and is the property in a carrier-restricted area?  unverified

Known: The owner's estimate is about $1,500 a year. The California Department of Insurance publishes ZIP-level residential insurance data, but no figure for this property was read from it in this pass.

Missing: A real quote, and whether the FAIR Plan is a fallback.

How to find it: Pull the CDI Residential Property Insurance Report Part II ZIP-level data for the property's ZIP and get two quotes from admitted carriers.

California Department of Insurance

Q09 What is the maintenance profile - roof age, foundation, HVAC, sewer lateral, and any deferred work?  unknown

Known: Nothing supplied. The model assumes 1.0% of market value a year, shown with a 0.5%-1.5% band.

Missing: Any capital plan. A single roof or foundation event can be tens of thousands of dollars and is the main reason the maintenance figure is a band, not a number.

How to find it: Commission a whole-house inspection and a sewer lateral scope now, while the property is still occupied and easy to access.

Q10 What net metering or net billing tariff is the solar system on, and when does it expire?  unverified

Known: The owner reports the system normally produces a small credit, about $10-$20 a month. That is owner input with no official confirmation.

Missing: The interconnection agreement, the tariff version, and the enrolment end date. Solar economics change materially between tariff generations.

How to find it: Pull the interconnection agreement and the last twelve months of statements from the serving utility, and confirm the tariff with the California Public Utilities Commission.

Q11 Is Property A a single-family home, and is any part of it separately rentable (accessory dwelling unit, junior unit)?  unknown

Known: The owner describes water use consistent with a single household (about $100-$150 a month).

Missing: The unit count and whether an ADU exists or could be added.

How to find it: Check the assessor's property characteristics record and the building permits on file.

Property B

Q12 What would the San Francisco unit you currently occupy rent for if you moved into Property A?  unknown

Known: Two of three units currently produce about $7,150 a month. The third unit's market rent has never been stated.

Missing: The figure that determines the cash cost of choosing to occupy Property A.

How to find it: Get two rental opinions for the exact unit. Note the offset: the rent you collect is matched by the housing you give up, so this is cash, not pure gain.

Q13 When was Property B built, and are the tenancies subject to the San Francisco rent ordinance or only to the statewide cap?  unknown

Known: Statewide, Civil Code section 1947.12 caps annual increases at 5% plus the change in the cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower, with a vacancy-decontrol rule and exemptions for newer housing and, with notice, for separately alienable single-family homes and condominiums. San Francisco's own ordinance is separate and stricter where it applies.

Missing: The year built for the parcel and the tenancy start dates.

How to find it: Pull the assessor's property characteristics and the original building permit. If the building predates the local ordinance cut-off, get advice before any change of terms.

Q14 Why is the San Francisco bill about $1,090 a year higher than assessed value times the official rate?  unverified

Known: The official SF secured rate for FY2026-27 is 1.18162182%. Applied to the owner's 2025 assessed value that gives about $1,350, against a reported bill of about $2,440. The residual of about $1,090 is being carried as fixed charges.

Missing: The actual bill, with the ad valorem, parcel tax, direct assessment and special charge lines separated.

How to find it: Download the current secured tax bill and match each line. If the residual is a parcel tax or a bond charge it behaves differently from the ad valorem part as value grows.

City and County of San Francisco, Office of the Treasurer & Tax Collector

Q15 What is the landlord insurance premium for Property B, and does it cover loss of rents?  unverified

Known: The owner's estimate is about $1,500 a year; no confirmation.

Missing: A real premium for a three-unit let property, which is materially different from an owner-occupied single-family policy.

How to find it: Get two quotes for a dwelling-fire or landlord policy with loss-of-rents cover.

Taxes

Q16 Roughly how large is the rest of the estate - the 'considerable amount of money' mentioned - and will it approach the federal exemption?  unknown

Known: The federal filing threshold is $15,000,000 for a death in 2026 and $15,480,000 for 2027, indexed after that. Projected forward at 2.5 percent a year it reaches about $24,143,520 by 2045.

Missing: Any estimate at all of the estate's total value. Without it, no statement can be made about whether federal estate tax will be owed.

How to find it: Prepare a confidential schedule of assets for the estate attorney. Never put the figure on a website.

IRS IRS

Q17 Has any lifetime gift used up part of the federal exemption?  unknown

Known: Lifetime taxable gifts are added back and consume the same unified exemption. The annual exclusion ($19,000 per recipient in 2025) shelters gifts below it.

Missing: Whether past gift tax returns were filed.

How to find it: Ask whether Forms 709 were ever filed, and retrieve them if so.

Congressional Research Service (Library of Congress)

Q18 Is portability available, and was a portability election made on any earlier death?  unknown

Known: A surviving spouse may elect to use a deceased spouse's unused exemption, but only on a timely filed estate tax return.

Missing: Whether there is a surviving spouse and whether an election was made.

How to find it: Check for a filed Form 706 on any earlier death; the election is made there and nowhere else.

IRS

Q19 What is the filing status and marginal tax position of the heir in the transfer year?  unknown

Known: It drives the NIIT (3.8% above $200,000 single / $250,000 joint), the California bracket (top 12.3% over $742,953 single / $1,485,906 joint for 2025), and whether the $250,000 or $500,000 section 121 exclusion applies on a later sale.

Missing: Everything. No tax profile has been supplied.

How to find it: Work it out with a tax adviser in the transfer year, not now - the answer will change.

Market

Q20 What long-run home price growth should be assumed for the two submarkets?  assumption

Known: The California House Price Index rose 2.15% in 2024, which is the figure the Board of Equalization used to adjust the Prop 19 allowance. The official FHFA index files are public.

Missing: A defensible forward assumption. This site uses 4.0% nominal with a 2.5%-5.5% band and shows the break-even growth rate explicitly.

How to find it: Download the FHFA state and metro index files and compute the long-run rate for California and for the two relevant metropolitan areas.

Federal Housing Finance Agency California State Board of Equalization

Q21 What inflation rate should be used for costs?  assumption

Known: The most recent official reading available when this was built is the BLS 12-month change for all items, 3.7% to September 2026; the CPI rose 0.4% in October 2026. The annual averages were 8.0% in 2022, 4.1% in 2023 and 2.9% in 2024.

Missing: A forward assumption. This site uses 3.0% with a 2.0%-5.0% band.

How to find it: Track the BLS release and reset the band each year.

Q22 What discount rate and portfolio return should the comparison use?  assumption

Known: Neither can be sourced from an official publication. This site therefore leads with break-even rates rather than a forecast.

Missing: A defensible pair of numbers for this household.

How to find it: Set them from the household's actual risk tolerance and the cost of any debt, and read the break-even table on the scenarios page instead of trusting a single point estimate.

Law change

Q23 Will the step-up in basis still exist at the transfer date?  reported - verify

Known: It exists today. CRS lists 'taxing unrealized gains under the income tax at death or eliminating step-up in basis' among the options Congress could consider, and several bills have been introduced. No bill has been enacted.

Missing: Any certainty past the current Congress.

How to find it: Watch congress.gov for the bills listed on the law-watch page, and revisit this question in every year the transfer is still pending.

Congressional Research Service (Library of Congress)

Q24 Will Proposition 13 or the Prop 19 exclusion still be in the same form?  reported - verify

Known: No measure on the November 3, 2026 California ballot changed either. Proposition 43, which would have required a two-thirds vote for voter-proposed local special taxes, was reported as not carrying; Propositions 40, 41 and 42 were competing tax measures. Results are certified in December 2026.

Missing: Certified official results, and any measure on a later ballot.

How to find it: Check the Secretary of State's certified results and the qualified-measures list for each future statewide ballot.

Q25 Do you actually want to live in Property A?  unknown

Known: Nothing in the numbers substitutes for this. Occupying is the only route to the Prop 19 exclusion, and the exclusion is worth about $21,350 a year at a 2045 transfer.

Missing: The household's own answer.

How to find it: Decide as a household question first; use the numbers to price the answer, not to produce it.

California State Board of Equalization

Q26 Are there other heirs who would need to be bought out if one heir keeps the house?  unknown

Known: Nothing supplied.

Missing: The number of heirs and whether any would need cash rather than a share of the house. This is often the binding constraint.

How to find it: Establish the beneficiary list and ask each directly, in writing, before the transfer date.
