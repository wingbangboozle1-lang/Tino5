Proposition 13 and Proposition 19

Property tax

Privacy. No name, street address, parcel or account number appears anywhere on this site. Properties are identified only as Property A (Santa Clara County, to be inherited through a trust) and Property B (San Francisco, already owned). All money is rounded to the nearest $10 so published figures cannot be matched back to a public record. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published because they are statewide constants and identify nothing.

California assesses property at what it was worth when it was last acquired, not what it is worth now. That single fact produces both the low bill today and the large increase on transfer.

Proposition 13 assessment cap
California State Board of Equalization		Annual increases in base year value are limited to no more than 2%, and the property is reassessed to market value only on a change in ownership or new construction.
California State Board of Equalization		The whole reason the current bill is low, and the whole reason it jumps.BOE Publication 29

What counts as a change in ownership
California State Board of Equalization		A transfer of a family home between parent and child on or after 16 February 2021 is governed by Proposition 19, not Proposition 58.
California State Board of Equalization		The transfer date decides which rule applies. The window here is 2040-2050, so Proposition 19 will govern.BOE Proposition 19

Proposition 19 value limit
California State Board of Equalization		The value cap is the factored base year value plus $1,044,586 for transfers from 16 February 2025 through 15 February 2027. Market value above the cap is added to the taxable value. The amount was $1,000,000 for 2021-2023 and $1,022,600 for 2023-2025, and is adjusted every two years.
California State Board of Equalization		The exclusion is partial, not total. For a house worth several million dollars, most of the gap between the old and new assessed values is still taxed.BOE LTA 2025/009
California State Board of Equalization
Proposition 19 occupancy requirement
California State Board of Equalization		The transferee must live in the home as a principal residence within one year of the transfer and must continue to occupy it. A transfer of a rental home between parent and child does not qualify at all.
California State Board of Equalization California State Board of Equalization		This is the clause that makes 'live in it' and 'rent it' different by an order of magnitude.BOE Publication 800-1
California State Board of Equalization
Consequence of moving out later
California State Board of Equalization		At least one eligible transferee must continually live in the property. Once it stops being anyone's principal residence it gets a new taxable value as of the lien date following the move-out, equal to the fair market value on the date it was inherited, adjusted by the inflation factor.
California State Board of Equalization		Occupying is not a one-year test. It is a continuing condition.BOE Proposition 19
California State Board of Equalization
Homeowners' exemption deadline
California State Board of Equalization		The homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption must be filed within one year of the transfer to receive the exclusion as of the date of transfer. Filed later, the exclusion begins in the year the claim is filed.
California State Board of Equalization		A late filing does not lose the exclusion, but it loses the back years.BOE Publication 800-1
California State Board of Equalization
Claim deadline
California State Board of Equalization		The claim, on form BOE-19-P, must be filed with the county assessor within three years of the transfer, or before the property is transferred to a third party, whichever comes first.

The allowance is $1,044,586 only for transfers between 16 February 2025 and 15 February 2027. For a transfer in the 2040s it is projected forward at the California House Price Index change the Board of Equalization last used, 2.15% a year, which is an assumption, not a published figure. The next published adjustment is in February 2027 and every second February after that.

What the numbers say. The exclusion is worth tens of thousands of dollars a year, but it does not keep the old bill. The bill multiplies either way. That reframes the question: it is not how to avoid the increase, it is whether the property is worth holding at the new carrying cost at all.
