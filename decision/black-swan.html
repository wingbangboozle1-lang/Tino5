Black swans

Privacy. No name, street address, parcel or account number appears anywhere on this site. Properties are identified only as Property A (Santa Clara County, to be inherited through a trust) and Property B (San Francisco, already owned). All money is rounded to the nearest $10 so published figures cannot be matched back to a public record. Statutory amounts are quoted exactly as published because they are statewide constants and identify nothing.

A single straight-line growth rate compounded for fifty years is the least realistic thing in any long-horizon property model. This page replaces it with regimes anchored on what actually happened.

Each regime re-runs the same model with different growth and inflation paths. The net present value columns use the housing-inclusive comparison at a 2045 transfer. Growth and inflation are never constant over fifty years, and this table is the honest way to say so: it does not predict which regime arrives, it shows what each one would do. Two rows - a Proposition 13 change or split roll, and a restriction on the step-up in basis - deliberately reproduce the baseline numbers. The model can re-price growth and inflation, but it cannot price a law that does not exist yet, and inventing a figure for it would be the one thing this site promises not to do. Read those two rows as a statement of exposure, not as a forecast.

Every number in this table was read from the official source named beside it. The regime paths in the table above are interpretations of that record, not predictions.

Two regimes deserve particular attention here. A change to Proposition 13 or a split roll would move the assessed value towards market value and would dwarf every other variable on this site. A restriction on the step-up in basis would cut the net proceeds of selling, which is the option this analysis currently prefers. Neither is law; both are listed on the law-watch page.
