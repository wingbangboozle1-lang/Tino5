3. Utility & Solar Tariff Mechanics: Why 2045 Looks Different From 2026

Property A Solar: Legacy NEM Credit Today → CPUC Net Billing Tariff Tomorrow Today Property A enjoys a $0 to -$20/mo net electricity credit (modeled at -$180/yr), characteristic of legacy Net Energy Metering (NEM 1.0/2.0) retail export credits and annual Net Surplus Compensation (~$0.02–$0.03/kWh). Under CPUC Decision 22-12-056 (effective April 15, 2023), legacy NEM grandfathering lasts 20 years from original interconnection PTO (Flag F-10). Before or during the 2040–2050 inheritance window, the system transitions to the Solar Billing Plan (Net Billing Tariff) with avoided-cost export rates, mandatory TOU rates, fixed monthly base charges, and inverter/battery replacement (~15–25 yr life), shifting net electricity from -$180/yr to +$600/yr (2026 dollars).
Property B Water & Rent Squeeze: SFPUC +12.5%/yr vs. SF Rent Board 1.6% Owner-paid water & sewer across Property B's 3 units ($330–$400/mo = $4,380/yr midpoint) is governed by SFPUC's Proposition 218 tariff, which enacted ~7% water and ~15% wastewater hikes for FY 2026–27 (~12.5% combined residential bill increase) and another ~12.5% for FY 2027–28 to fund capital infrastructure. Meanwhile, Property B's 2 rented units are capped by the SF Rent Board at 1.6% (Mar 1, 2026 – Feb 28, 2027; 60% of Bay Area CPI). Our model escalates Property B water at 3.5%/yr vs. 2.0%/yr rent growth to capture this structural operating margin compression.
