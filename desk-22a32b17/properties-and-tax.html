Property A vs. Property B: Proposition 13, Proposition 19 & Basis Rules

Detailed legal reconciliation of Santa Clara County single-family Property A (inherited via trust) and San Francisco County 3-unit Property B (already owned), verified against BOE Publication 801, LTA 2026/002, LTA 2026/026, R&TC §63.2, and IRC §1014.

1. Core Legal & Tax Asymmetry: Inherited Property A vs. Already-Owned Property B

The single most important structural fact in this decision is that Property A (single-family home in Santa Clara County) is being inherited at death via trust, whereas Property B (3-unit building in San Francisco County) is already owned by the beneficiary today. Under federal and California tax law, those two postures produce completely opposite tax incentives:

Property A — High Reassessment Risk, Zero Sale Tax at Death At the settlor's death, Property A undergoes a change in ownership under CA R&TC §60/§63.2 (triggering massive property-tax reassessment even WITH Proposition 19's partial allowance because its multi-million-dollar value far exceeds FBYV + $1,044,586). Simultaneously, under IRC §1014(a), Property A's income-tax basis resets to 100% of date-of-death FMV—meaning an immediate sale pays $0 federal and $0 California capital-gains tax and only $0.55 per $500 (0.11%) county transfer tax.
Property B — Protected Prop 13 Tax Base, Heavy Tax Penalty if Sold Because the beneficiary already owns Property B, the settlor's death triggers zero reassessment (keeping its ~$2,440/yr property tax bill intact) and zero IRC §1014 basis step-up. Selling Property B would trigger ~37.1% combined federal + California capital-gains/recapture taxes on embedded gain PLUS San Francisco's steep tiered transfer tax (0.75% at $1M–$5M; 2.25% at $5M–$10M), while forfeiting $85,800/yr in 2-unit rent and a free owner-occupiable 3rd unit.

2. Proposition 19 Formula & Property A Reassessment Schedule (2026, 2040, 2045, 2050)

Under California Constitution Article XIII A, §2.1 and R&TC §63.2 (effective February 16, 2021), an inherited property avoids full reassessment only if: (1) it was the transferor's principal residence, (2) the child moves in as their principal residence within 1 year of death and files Form BOE-266 ($7,000 Homeowners' Exemption), and (3) Form BOE-19-P is filed within 3 years. Even when all three tests are met, the excluded value is capped at Factored Base Year Value + Adjusted Allowance ($1,044,586 for transfers Feb 16, 2025 – Feb 15, 2027 per BOE LTA 2025/009; next biennial adjustment Feb 16, 2027). Any FMV above that cap is added dollar-for-dollar to the old FBYV.
